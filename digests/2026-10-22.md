### [Retrieval Augmented Generation Compositionality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29722)
**Score 10/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.29722

> Focuses on the composition and evaluation of RAG methods, critical for the user's research.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) grounds large language models in external knowledge. Modern pipelines combine embedding, chunking, retrieval, reranking, query augmentation, context compression, and agentic verification, yet these techniques are typically evaluated one at a time. It remains unclear whether their isolated gains survive composition, whether retrieval quality and answer faithfulness favor the same design choices, and whether conclusions from small corpora hold at large scale. We pre-declare three hypotheses and introduce LASAGNA, an open-source framework for controlled RAG evaluation. We test 450 configurations across eight benchmarks and corpora ranging from 5K to 200K documents. Three findings emerge. First, individually strong techniques from different phases of a RAG pipeline do not necessarily compose: three retrieval techniques predict a +47% combined improvement but deliver only +17% when used together. Second, component gains stack differently for retrieval and faithfulness, and optimizing one can hurt the other. Third, the best composition changes as the corpus grows: the combination that wins on a small corpus may not remain best at large scale. These findings show that gains measured in isolation are not reliable predictors of realistic performance, and that new RAG techniques should be tested with the full surrounding stack. We call on researchers to do so and supply LASAGNA to simplify these experiments.

### [C$^2$U in RAG: Compositional Concept Unlearning in Retrieval-Augmented Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29223)
**Score 10/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.29223

> Directly contributes to handling unwanted concepts in RAG architectures.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented generation can expose unwanted concepts through retrieved evidence even when the generator is moderated. We study query-side concept suppression with a fixed encoder and document index. Compositional Concept Unlearning (C$^2$U) routes a profile-derived direction through an augmented atom bank, adding only a bounded edited-minus-original delta to the raw query. Across five datasets, four encoders, and three seeds, the validation-selected configuration reduces pooled binary target Hit@5 by 4.25 percentage points with non-positive control degradation and target-exposure changes. Under the evaluated query transforms, it suppresses target density without increasing budgeted control-query exposure. Separately, ArXiv four-concept composition improves over an averaged-direction comparator after independent validation gates and first-use test evaluation.

### [Reasoning-Search-Augmented LLMs: An Operational Taxonomy, Benchmark Analysis and Controlled Evidence Synthesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29189)
**Score 10/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.29189

> Analyzes reasoning-search loops relevant for grounded/faithful generation in RAG.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large language models (LLMs) demonstrate impressive emergent reasoning capabilities, yet they remain constrained by fixed model parameters and finite context windows. Even when augmented with static retrieval, they can produce statements that are unsupported by, or even contradictory to, retrieved evidence. This leads to hallucinations, difficulty incorporating up-to-date information, and failures to correctly interpret or reconcile sources. Recent work addresses these limitations by tightly coupling step-by-step reasoning with interactive search, treating retrieval as an action within an iterative agentic loop rather than a one-shot preprocessing step. This study reviews frameworks that implement such reasoning-search loops by interleaving planning, querying, retrieval, integration, verification, and stopping. We analyze these systems through four architectural axes: retrieval coupling, controller design, knowledge source, and grounding strictness. We separately treat the learning signal as a cross-cutting dimension because prompting, supervision, preference optimization, and reinforcement learning can instantiate the same architecture while producing materially different search, stopping, and grounding behavior. We further distinguish benchmark difficulty from RSA-loop demand and synthesize controlled within-study ablations and quality-cost deltas, avoiding invalid pooling across heterogeneous model backbones, retrievers, corpora, and evaluation protocols. Our goal is to clarify the landscape of reasoning-search-augmented LLMs and outline a unified perspective on the design choices shaping future research.

</details>

### [What the Author Wrote, What the Agent Reads: Silent Cue Loss in Enterprise Document Ingestion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30026)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.30026

> Highly relevant as it discusses issues in document ingestion, directly applicable to RAG systems.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Enterprise agents act on documents. Before an agent can follow a policy, answer a compliance question or file a ticket, some ingestion layer must turn a Word file or PDF into tokens - and that layer is where an agent deployed in the wild silently loses information. We show that one layer is lost by every mainstream converter - the meaning an author encodes in how text looks - and that the loss makes agents confidently wrong rather than visibly incomplete. We build a task from 76 real policy documents: recover the requirements the author singled out, scored against the file's own OOXML properties by a deterministic matcher with no LLM judge. Formatting-blind ingestion collapses from micro F1 0.690 to 0.150 (docling) and 0.075 (plain text), a gap that replicates across six answering models from two vendors - five Claude models spanning the Opus, Sonnet and Haiku tiers, and OpenAI's GPT-5.6. An agent that cannot see the cue guesses, and docling's false-positive rate on requirements the author did not flag is four times ours. A span-level leave-one-out localises the loss to two cues - highlight (+0.302) and colour (+0.262), both Holm-adjusted p < 1e-4 - while bold, italic, monospace and strikethrough each sit within +/-0.003 of zero. The same asymmetry appears in PDF. Colour is also the cue no mainstream converter preserves and no parsing benchmark scores, and it is common: 32.8% of 6,460 documents carry a deliberate colour or highlight, 10.3% on an obligation. A pre-registered test shows that typing a cue for the agent adds +0.085 beyond merely preserving it (Holm-adjusted p = 0.049). We validate the labels against rendered page images through an independent modality (kappa = 0.72) and bound what wording alone can achieve (AUC 0.664). For agent builders the practical finding is that ingestion, not reasoning, is where this reliability is won or lost, and that the fix is cheap and measurable.

</details>

### [Graph Topology Meets Energy-Driven Pathfinding: Preserving Contextual Consistency in Retrieval-Augmented Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29933)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.29933

> Addresses RAG architecture by introducing a context-preserving knowledge structuring framework, very relevant.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) frequently integrates knowledge graphs (KGs) for complex multi-hop question answering. However, their performance is increasingly bottlenecked by the knowledge structuring and reasoning processes. (i) Severe semantic loss: Distilling continuous texts into discrete symbols strips away contextual nuances and narrative coherence. (ii) Pronounced modality mismatch: Fragmented graph structures clash with the continuous texts LLMs are pre-trained on, while rewriting them into natural language introduces generative noise and omits information. To address these limitations, we propose ThreadRAG, a semantic-topology-based RAG framework for context-preserving knowledge structuring and modality-native evidence reasoning. ThreadRAG introduces a Semantic Topology Mapper (STM), which organizes retrieved documents into context-intact Semantic Nodes and constructs a Semantic Topology Graph (STG) through narrative, semantic, and lexical edges. It further incorporates Energy-Driven Pathfinding (EDP), which identifies query-relevant subgraphs via energy diffusion and deterministically linearizes them into coherent textual evidence paths. Experiments on three multi-hop QA benchmarks demonstrate that ThreadRAG consistently outperforms strong baselines. Notably, it achieves a 14.4% EM improvement over text-centric methods on HotpotQA and surpasses the best baseline by 5.8% EM on 2WikiMultihop, while reducing token cost to 23.3% of the strongest baseline.

</details>

### [MAPS: Macro-Adaptive Parallel Reasoning for Regulating Search Momentum in Retrieval-Augmented Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29792)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.29792

> Directly relates to RAG by addressing retrieval and reasoning in LLMs, essential for the user's project.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) improves the performance of Large Language Models (LLMs) by retrieving external knowledge. Recently, some RAG methods interleave reasoning and retrieval, enabling LLMs to progressively generate sub-queries and retrieve external knowledge. However, these methods may suffer from redundant or ineffective sub-queries and insufficiently diverse query exploration. Moreover, due to the tightly interleaved nature of reasoning and retrieval, it remains unclear how the evolving reasoning process influences query intent. To characterize the evolution of query intent during reasoning, we introduce search momentum, which captures the accumulated retrieval tendency induced by historical changes in generated sub-queries along the reasoning trajectory. Through momentum-based analysis, we find that the query generation process tends to continuously evolve along existing retrieval trajectories. Based on this observation, we propose MAPS, a Macro-Adaptive Parallel Reasoning framework for regulating search momentum and mitigating query-intent drift. MAPS first retains the retrieved knowledge and a refined reasoning state, thereby reducing the influence of historical reasoning trajectories on subsequent sub-query generation. Subsequently, MAPS extends the conventional sequential reasoning process into a parallel reasoning paradigm. At each iteration, MAPS identifies complementary retrieval directions, generates corresponding sub-queries for parallel retrieval, and consolidates the retrieved evidence to update the knowledge and reasoning states. Experiments on six question-answering benchmarks demonstrate that MAPS consistently outperforms representative RAG methods across different backbone models. All code and resources are available at https://github.com/NEUIR/MAPS.

</details>

### [A Diagnostic Study of Faithfulness in Diffusion Language Models for Table-to-Text Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29602)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.29602

> Investigates faithfulness in grounded table-to-text generation, crucial for grounded, accurate responses.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Diffusion language models generate by iteratively denoising a masked sequence under bidirectional attention, without the strict left-to-right commitment of autoregressive decoding. Whether this difference affects how reliably they remain grounded in structured inputs is largely unexplored. We study this question in table-to-text generation using ToTTo, where highlighted source cells make factual grounding directly testable. We compare the masked diffusion models MDLM and LLaDA with GPT-2 and GPT-Neo under an identical retrieval-based few-shot protocol, together with a QLoRA-adapted LLaDA, and we score reference similarity and source grounding as separate axes. The two pull apart. Within LLaDA, additional demonstrations raise BLEU from 11.3 to 22.0 while NLI entailment against the source table falls from 0.78 to 0.52; QLoRA reaches the best BLEU of the study, 24.15, and grounds no better for it. Manual annotation shows the failure modes differ in kind: the autoregressive baselines tend to wander off the table, while LLaDA-based models more often stay on topic and swap out individual entities. Reference-based metrics alone are insufficient to assess faithfulness in table-to-text generation.

### [Mitigating Query Shortcuts in Chain-of-Thought with Context-Aware Decoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29518)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.29518

> Focuses on mitigating shortcuts in CoT prompting, highly relevant for improving answer quality.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

While Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting improves the reasoning performance of large language models (LLMs), recent studies have shown that final answers do not always depend on the generated rationales. In particular, LLMs may rely on query-specific shortcuts rather than the information provided by their reasoning steps. In this paper, we investigate whether context-aware decoding (CAD), an existing contrastive decoding method, can mitigate such shortcut behavior when applied to CoT reasoning. We study this application as Rationale Contrastive Decoding (RCD), which contrasts the next-token distributions conditioned on the query with and without its rationale, amplifying tokens whose likelihood increases due to the rationale. We provide a theoretical interpretation of this decoding rule via a KL-regularized reward-maximization objective. Experiments across five reasoning benchmarks demonstrate that RCD improves answer accuracy over standard CoT. Furthermore, intervention analyses using ground-truth and random rationales reveal that RCD increases the behavioral dependence of final answers on the rationale content. Our findings suggest that contrastive decoding offers a simple, training-free approach to mitigating query shortcuts.

</details>

### [Beyond Binary Labels: Diagnostic Evaluation of LLM Safety with Prompt-Level Rubrics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29225)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.29225

> Addresses LLM safety, crucial for guardrails in production systems.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Current LLM safety benchmarks predominantly rely on binary safe or unsafe labels, lacking explanatory depth and suffering from low consistency across automated judges. To address this, we introduce DiagSafe, a diagnostic evaluation framework that moves beyond binary classification by employing prompt-level rubrics. Grounded in globally applicable AI safety policies, each rubric provides explicit criteria along two dimensions: safety, which targets concrete harms with specific exemptions to suppress false positives, and usefulness, which independently evaluates instruction fulfillment. To ensure a robust and realistic evaluation, we construct a bilingual (English-Chinese) benchmark grounded in a comprehensive 84-label safety taxonomy, consisting of two subsets: DiagBase, which covers single- and multi-turn prompts with direct harmful queries and template-based jailbreaks, and DiagAttack, which embeds harmful intents into complex instruction-following tasks to form challenging instruction attacks. Extensive experiments demonstrate that DiagSafe significantly enhances the reliability of LLM-as-a-judge evaluation, achieving 95.34% agreement with human annotations -- substantially higher than the 69%-87% achieved by the baselines -- and high inter-judge consistency (Cohen's $κ$ = 0.864). Furthermore, applying our framework reveals that leading LLMs differ markedly in their unsafe rates, and each model also exhibits unique vulnerabilities along different dimensions. In addition, we observe that models are particularly susceptible in Chinese and multi-turn contexts. Ultimately, DiagSafe provides a reliable and explainable tool for auditing and optimizing LLM alignment.

</details>

### [Learning Marginal Viewpoint Utility for Budgeted Evidence Selection in RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29234)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2610.29234

> The focus on marginal viewpoint utility for evidence selection is directly relevant for optimizing RAG performance.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented generation over multi-viewpoint queries must preserve distinct answers and positions from the retrieved evidence while operating under a fixed context budget. We introduce LMVU, a lightweight ranker that learns to prioritize candidates by the marginal viewpoint coverage they add to the current selection. Training uses viewpoint annotations from each benchmark's development split to compare candidates within the same selection state. At inference, LMVU repeatedly scores budget-feasible candidates as the context grows, without accessing these annotations. We evaluate two forms of recovered viewpoint coverage: RVC$_1$ assigns each viewpoint full credit for at least one supporting source unit, while RVC$_2$ assigns half credit for one and full credit for two or more distinct source units. Exact oracles measure achievable coverage under the same evidence pool and budget. Across four viewpoint-annotated benchmarks at a 1,024-token budget, LMVU achieves gains of up to 0.047 and 0.052 points in RVC$_1$ and RVC$_2$, respectively, over the strongest non-oracle baseline for each benchmark and metric, and reaches the exact viewpoint-breadth ceiling on two benchmarks.

### [Uni-Mem: Learning a Long-Horizon Agentic Memory Manager with Unified Online Policy Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29671)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.29671

> Explores long-term memory for agents, relevant for conversational memory management in chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Long-term agent memory is shifting from predefined workflows toward learnable policies for memory construction and retrieval. Existing learning-based methods either optimize construction alone or combine retrieval-side online reinforcement learning (RL) with offline construction supervision, limiting co-adaptation between the two stages. In this paper, we explore end-to-end online RL for a unified memory manager (Uni-Mem) that learns both capabilities under a shared policy. However, joint optimization with existing RL algorithms such as GRPO faces confounded group comparisons and sparse feedback over long memory-management trajectories. To this end, we propose Stage-Decoupled GRPO, which alternates construction and retrieval optimization while controlling variation from the other stage within each rollout group, yielding stage-specific learning signals. We further introduce Event-Wise Credit Assignment (ECA), which localizes feedback to responsible events and propagates event-level advantages to their constituent operations. Experiments on LoCoMo and LongMemEval show that Uni-Mem outperforms the strongest RL-based memory, demonstrating the effectiveness of jointly learning memory construction and retrieval via Stage-Decoupled GRPO and ECA.

</details>

### [Conflicted Records: Typed Adversarial Contracts for Auditable LLM Output Quality Enforcement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29669)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.29669

> Focuses on quality enforcement in LLM outputs, relevant for grounded generation and hallucination detection.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

LLM-generated records increasingly populate production information systems where they are treated as data assets subject to the same quality expectations as records from operational databases. A single such record can simultaneously satisfy and violate a quality rule: a grounded fact adjacent to a hallucination, a useful answer adjacent to a stereotype. Classical data cleaning cannot resolve this because the two judgments are incompatible over the same span; a single LLM judge cannot either, since it shares the training biases that produced the conflict. Existing interventions such as prompt engineering, chain-of-thought, and RLHF reduce surface error rates but provide no audit trail and cannot distinguish genuine remediation from blanket suppression. The question is whether a deterministic check over structured inter-agent records can catch the defects that referential checks alone miss. We present BiasGuard-D: two adversarial roles drawn from distinct model families exchange typed claim documents, a three-model quorum adjudicates by majority vote, and a combined referential-plus-semantic invariant validates the verdict. The semantic component fires on verdicts referential checks miss; removing the iterative loop drops invariant satisfaction sharply. The deliberation layer transfers from stereotype to hallucination remediation on HaluEval QA with only role-framing changes, raising right-answer recovery by 49 percentage points; the triage gate required a cross-family second auditor to maintain recall on objective hallucination. Every claim, vote, and instruction is stored as a typed record giving operators a provenance trail auditable under emerging AI regulations.

### [WRDO: Weighted Refusal Direction Optimization in Aligned Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29555)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.29555

> Proposes a method for optimizing LLM refusal behavior for safety in production systems.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Aligned large language models are trained to comply with benign requests while refusing harmful ones. However, recent work shows that this refusal behavior is fragile and can often be disrupted by simple activation- or weight-space interventions. In particular, refusal has been shown to be largely mediated by a single residual-stream direction, typically computed as a difference-of-means vector between harmful and harmless prompts. However, this standard construction aggregates heterogeneous harmful prompts into one uniform average, potentially conflating distinct refusal-related phenomena. In this work, we study whether refusal interventions can be strengthened by learning how to combine prompt-specific refusal directions. We propose Weighted Refusal Direction Optimization (WRDO), a procedure that constructs a composite refusal direction from a bank of per-prompt directions using learned aggregation weights. This approach allows different harmful prompts to contribute unequally to the final intervention direction. We evaluate WRDO against prior single-direction methods on three open-weight language models using three guard judges and HarmBench as an external evaluator. We further measure the effect of the intervention on general model capability through standard utility benchmarks. Finally, we investigate a defense-oriented setting in which the same optimization machinery is used in reverse to restore refusal behavior in an abliterated model checkpoint. Our results show that weighted aggregation provides a more effective and flexible mechanism for manipulating refusal representations.

</details>

### [Beyond Small Talk: Modeling Disclosure Trajectories in Human-AI Conversation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29413)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.29413

> Discusses memory and disclosure trajectories in conversations which ties into long-term memory for chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Recently, we see a rise in conversational agents as a form of social and emotional support, where disclosure will unfold over time. Yet, current evaluations of these agents typically examine isolated conversational states. In analyses of naturally occurring conversations, disclosure history and current disclosure are confounded which tends to make their effects difficult to isolate. We thus introduce Trajectory-based Disclosure Evaluation (TraDE), an evaluation framework grounded in Social Penetration Theory that utilizes predefined disclosure trajectories and evaluates how preceding disclosure history shapes model behavior under matched current disclosure. Through the use of TraDE, we demonstrate across multiple models and relational behavior benchmarks that responses to disclosures matched on depth can differ depending on the preceding trajectory, particularly in whether models accept or challenge the user's framing. We also observed that these differences track accumulated exposure to deeper disclosure rather than any single moment of vulnerability or the order of disclosures. It also tends to persists for several turns after disclosure returns to shallow levels. For several behaviors, we also demonstrate that models diverge most consistently from human supporters in the Emotional Support Conversation corpus (ESConv) not when users open up but rather when they step back in their disclosure. TraDE thus provides a systematic way to expose history-dependent relational behavior that evaluations of isolated conversational states would otherwise miss.

### [Memory-Centric Continual Learning for AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29357)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.29357

> Addresses long-term memory for chat assistants and continuous learning, relevant for context management.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Autonomous agents powered by foundation models can reason, plan, and use tools, but their fixed weights and context limits hinder sustained learning from experience over long-term deployment. While external memory systems attempt to bridge this gap, they mostly act as passive retrieval caches, evaluated primarily by whether they can recall stored facts or past interactions. Whether memory can drive genuine continual learning remains an open question. In this paper, we introduce Memory-Centric Continual Learning (MCCL), arguing that memory should be judged by whether it improves future actions, not merely what it retrieves. MCCL has three main parts. First, a three-axis taxonomy organizes memory by where state is stored (substrates), what form it takes (assets), and how it is managed (policies). This defines a two-loop lifecycle that updates memory state in an inner loop while refining management policies in an outer loop based on task feedback. Second, an evaluation protocol measures retention under interference, adaptation to change, and task transfer to isolate genuine behavioral gains from simple context expansion. Third, a memory scaling framework models how agent performance scales with experience, storage capacity, and compute budgets across non-parametric and parametric memory. Together, these components reframe agent memory from passive lookup to continuous learning, offering a principled route toward self-improving autonomous agents.

</details>
