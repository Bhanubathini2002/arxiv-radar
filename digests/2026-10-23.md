### [Memory-Aware Reward Modeling: Learning Human Preferences for LLM Memory Use](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31272)
**Score 10/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.31272

> Directly investigates memory use in LLMs, crucial for conversation continuity in chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Memory enables language model assistants to maintain continuity across sessions and generate personalized responses, but knowing more about a user does not necessarily produce responses they prefer. Existing evaluations assess memory capabilities without directly establishing whether memory use benefits the user. To study this gap, we collect approximately 1,000 pairwise judgments from 87 participants using their own ChatGPT queries and personal memories. We find that memory-conditioned responses are not consistently preferred over responses generated without memory and in some cases, users strongly prefer the latter. Automatic evaluators also show limited agreement with users and favor memory-conditioned responses substantially more often than users do. Motivated by these gaps, we introduce a memory-aware reward model that learns from user judgments, achieving 72.8\% preference prediction accuracy and outperforming the existing baselines. Our findings motivate evaluation and optimization objectives that align memory use with the preferences of the people whose information is being used.

### [Lychee: Certified Screening of Inverted Lists for High-Recall Vector Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30697)
**Score 10/10** · `cs.IR` · 2610.30697

> Directly addresses high-recall vector search techniques essential for efficient vector database operations.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Nearest-neighbor search over embedding vectors matches faces against watchlists, transactions against known fraud patterns, and medical images against confirmed cases, where a missed neighbor is a missed threat, fraud, or diagnosis. Because an approximate index cannot report which queries lost a true neighbor, these applications require recall of 0.999 to 0.9999, at a cost far below that of exact search over billions of vectors. Inverted-file indexes reach high recall by probing more lists, but they read and score every probed vector at full precision, so that throughput falls in inverse proportion to the number of probed lists. Graph indexes saturate below these targets, whereas quantized lists must rerank their candidates with the full vectors. Our insight is to decide for almost every probed vector that it cannot enter the top k of the probed lists without reading the vector, through the certified lower bound of exact search by certified exclusion. This paper turns this insight into Lychee, a GPU inverted-file search that screens the probed lists on tensor cores under a threshold from anchors of the same lists and computes exact distances only for the few survivors. We prove that Lychee returns exactly the k nearest vectors of the probed lists, the answer of an inverted-file search at full precision, while it reads a few hundred full vectors per query regardless of the number of probed lists. On eleven datasets from one million to one billion vectors and against ten GPU and CPU baselines, Lychee is 9.7 to 117 times faster than IVF-Flat at equal recall with 200 probed lists on datasets of up to 100 million vectors. It also reaches recall levels that CAGRA and HNSW do not reach and outruns exact search by certified exclusion by 5 to 18 times above ten million vectors. On four GPUs, Lychee searches one billion vectors at recall 0.9999, 44.5 times faster than IVF-Flat on BIGANN-1B.

</details>

### [How Ground Truth Shapes Retrieval Scores: Evidence-Aware Evaluation of a Retrieval-Augmented Generation System for Breast Lesions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31606)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.31606

> Directly addresses retrieval evaluation in a clinical RAG system, highly relevant for method development.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Purpose: To describe an evidence-aware retrieval evaluation protocol for a clinical retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system over breast lesion management literature, addressing the dependence of retrieval scores on annotation coverage. Methods: An 18-document corpus and an 18-query development benchmark, with one Yes/No or WH-question per document, were used. Retrieval combined dense embeddings, BM25, and cross-encoder reranking. Chunk sizes of 128 to 1024 tokens, overlaps of 0 to 20%, and four retriever configurations were evaluated. Relevance was defined by evidence spans mapped to all containing chunks, treating overlapping chunks with equivalent evidence as relevant. Retrieval was evaluated using R-Precision, Recall@K, MRR, and nDCG@K. Separately, generation was assessed on a 512-token index at K=1 to 5 using DeepEval claim-level metrics, with deepseek-v4-pro as the LLM judge, and BLEU/ROUGE metrics. Results: The selected hybrid retriever, using 384-token chunks and 20% overlap, retrieved accepted evidence in the top three results for 17 of 18 queries. Recall@3, R-Precision, MRR@3, and nDCG@3 each reached 0.94. Ground-truth curation increased evidence spans from 25 to 37 and accepted chunk targets from 36 to 51 without changing retrieval rankings. Conclusion: Cross-encoder reranking achieved the highest retrieval scores. Expanded relevance annotations increased recognized evidence without changing rankings, highlighting annotation sensitivity. The small development set and retrieval-informed curation require independent validation, including radiologist assessment of evidence sufficiency.

### [AfterDeleteBench: Auditing Post-Deletion Information Persistence in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31366)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.31366

> Addresses memory persistence and deletion in LLM agents, highly relevant for managing long-term memory in chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Many LLM agents maintain external persistent memory and derive additional state from past interactions, such as summaries. Deleting an original memory record therefore need not remove information carried by its descendants. We introduce AfterDeleteBench, a controlled behavioral audit for this failure mode in fixed-weight LLM agents. For each synthetic secret, we compare an exposed-then-deleted agent with an otherwise matched agent that was never exposed to the secret, allowing post-deletion recovery to be attributed to the original exposure. In our primary setting, a single query recovers the deleted secret from Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct in roughly two thirds of cases, while matched never-exposed controls have zero recovery. Crucially, recovery does not require a surviving verbatim copy: when the complete secret string is absent and only separately stored components remain under a public composition rule, exact reconstruction succeeds in 62-99% of cases across Qwen, Mistral, and Phi-3. We also show that the response interface is part of the measurement system: target-bearing replies can be scored as failures when the requested format and parser are misaligned. Finally, we separate reconstruction from write-back and fresh-session recall. Provenance-aware closure eliminates observed recovery in the tested positive-baseline settings, whereas an exact-literal write filter blocks reinsertion but cannot erase information already present. Together, these results show that source deletion should be evaluated behaviorally at the level of the agent and its memory system, not only as a storage operation.

### [The Value of Memory in Language Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31303)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.31303

> Explores the value of memory in language agents, which is highly relevant for chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Memory enables language agents to carry useful distinctions from past experience into future decisions. Its value depends on which distinctions survive, whether a bounded agent can use them, and what can be recovered or revised as demands evolve. We develop a decision-theoretic framework that follows this value from raw history through persistent state, operational use, and costly recovery and revision. Original and retained value are expressed in units of avoided task loss; risk-cost frontiers describe attainable recovery and preservation across uses. Unknown demand yields value profiles, while interactive feedback changes the distribution of future work. A cost-aware simulation condition and five minimal counterexamples expose the roles of information timing, retrieval access, component interaction, feedback and demand uncertainty. In particular, a pointer with zero mutual information about the answer can still reduce the cost of exact recovery. Diagnostics on LoCoMo, a two-project update task and longitudinal cases exhibit failures of preservation, use and measurement. At fixed retrieved evidence, a saved ledger changes subsequent answers; at fixed agent outputs, an evaluator revision changes scores. The framework connects these distinctions to operational risk bounds and testable principles for allocating memory, preserving recovery options and revising evidence dependencies.

### [SCOPE: Compiling Natural-Language Policies into Neurosymbolic Predicate Programs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31062)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.31062

> Directly addresses guardrails and stability in LLMs, crucial for RAG applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Policy enforcement for LLM agents has shifted from single-turn content moderation to compound, application-specific policies evaluated over multi-turn transcripts and tool-call trajectories. Fine-tuned guardrail models are efficient but degrade on compound policies, and while LLM-as-a-judge approaches achieve higher accuracy, they incur high inference cost and tend to produce unstable predictions. Neurosymbolic methods offer a natural alternative, decomposing the policy into narrow neural decisions composed under explicit logic. However, existing symbolic layers lack the required expressiveness and are optimized against fixed data rather than the policy itself. To address this, we introduce SCOPE, which compiles a policy into an executable program of neural predicates executed by small models, deterministic extractors, and code that composes their outputs. The program is optimized jointly with its training data, using adversarial generation of counterexamples that target where the program deviates from the policy. We evaluate SCOPE on a benchmark of real-world policy tasks against prompt optimization, task decomposition, and workflow generation methods, achieving the highest average accuracy across tasks and models. Programs optimized with the weakest executor transfer to other models without further optimization. This allows them to be deployed in production on inexpensive models, where they reach accuracy comparable to frontier reasoning models. Unlike these monolithic models, SCOPE produces substantially more stable predictions, and each verdict is interpretable through the predicate outputs that produced it. Additionally, we validate the key components of our method through an ablation study. SCOPE thus enables reliable, interpretable, and efficient policy enforcement at production scale.

### [Chunk Recomputation under Real Document Revisions: Measuring Update Cost and Retrieval Quality of Fixed, Recursive, and Content-Defined Chunking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31019)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.31019

> Directly addresses chunking strategies essential for RAG applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Chunking strategies for retrieval-augmented generation are almost always evaluated on a static snapshot, yet the documents underlying a knowledge base continue to change. Because chunks are embedded independently, a boundary that moves after an edit forces unchanged text to be re-embedded. We measure this update cost on 120 pairs of adjacent Chinese and English Wikipedia revisions for three stateless chunkers (non-overlapping fixed-size, recursive paragraph-first, and content-defined chunking) under a strict byte-identical reuse rule and a frozen dense encoder. On the 116-article common-success pool, with frozen, length-calibrated parameters, fixed-size chunking recomputes 57-68% of its full new-version embedding-input workload, recursive chunking 38-56%, and content-defined chunking 43-54% (it failed on 4 of 62 Chinese articles), while at these parameters mean evidence recall under a fixed 2048-token retrieval budget differs by less than two percentage points within either language. A cost-only parameter grid shows that the ranking of the two content-aware chunkers changes with chunk-length settings, whereas non-overlapping fixed-size chunking has the highest recomputation cost in every length-matched condition. Controlled edits and a verbatim-presence diagnostic support boundary drift as one contributor to recomputation of text already present in the previous version. Update cost is therefore a substantial, parameter-sensitive dimension that static retrieval evaluations do not expose and that should be reported alongside retrieval quality.

### [The Fusion Tax: A Diagnostic Analysis of When Hybrid Retrieval Hurts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30612)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.30612

> It directly addresses the effectiveness and downsides of hybrid retrieval, critical for RAG applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Hybrid retrieval, which fuses a sparse lexical retriever with a dense neural one, is widely recommended in the software documentation surveyed in September 2026, usually with reciprocal rank fusion (RRF), on the strength of aggregate gains. We ask what fusion costs on individual queries, treating "do not fuse" as a first-class outcome, in the setting in which hybrid retrieval is deployed: a standard Anserini BM25, five dense encoders spanning 22M-596M parameters and three model generations, a learned-sparse (SPLADE) alternative, and a cross-encoder second stage, on seven BEIR datasets and three fusion functions. Even the best method, dense-weighted score interpolation, ranks relevant documents below the better of its two components on 13-48% of queries and below the deployable dense-only retriever on 8-21% (14-48% and 11-25% at a weight selected under leave-one-corpus-out validation), while beating dense-only in aggregate on every corpus with the primary encoder; the downside is significant under loss-averse risk-sensitive evaluation on six of the seven corpora. We call this loss the fusion tax. It is mostly a ranking effect that preserves recall in aggregate, it is most likely on the queries that only one retriever covers, and it persists across the encoder ladder and the SPLADE substitution. RRF carries the higher tax wherever the dense retriever is clearly stronger and only beats dense-only where the two components are balanced. Fusion failure is weakly predictable within a corpus but transfers unreliably across corpora, because the features that predict it change sign with the sparse-dense balance. A cross-encoder recovers part of the tax only where it is itself reliable. We distill these findings into guidance that keys the fusion decision to the measured sparse-dense balance of the target corpus.

### [Protection Is Not One Event: A Survey of RAG Protection Research by Measured Endpoint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30193)

> Directly addresses security and privacy risks in RAG systems, making it a must-read for protection measures.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems face security, privacy, and copyright risks including content leakage, source reconstruction, unauthorized access, data poisoning, and prompt injection. Yet RAG protection studies use incompatible evaluation metrics, datasets, and threat models. We present a systematic survey and human-coded evidence map of 118 full-text-confirmed RAG defense papers, organized by measured endpoint rather than claimed goal or defense mechanism. ### [ULME: Predictive Coupling for Mutually Reinforcing Embedding and Language Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30705)

This shift creates a new LLM vulnerability: a data-void exploit. Disinformers publish their narratives in search spaces where relevant and reliable content is scarce, i.e. data voids, then rely on the LLM's web-search to retrieve and then use, cite, or list those pages in its responses to data-void seeking prompts. Unlike classical search, where users see ranking cues and competing results, LLMs can obscure when an answer relies on sparse, unverified, or adversarial sources. We study this threat by varying the user's prompt tone - skeptical, excited, or neutral - and LLM choice - ChatGPT, Gemini, or Sonar Pro - as well as three attacker controls. The attacker can use one of five known strategies to construct and fill a data void with their narrative, host their content at varying levels of visibility from well-established, indexed domains to less established ones, and include generative-optimization engine features thought to boost citing by web-searching LLMs. We find that data-void exploits can succeed with only a single attacker controlled page. Across all conditions, attacker-content appears in LLM responses 31% of the time. User controls offer limited protection: while skeptical prompts slightly reduce success and slightly increase flagging or warnings in LLM responses, we find no evidence of large effects of prompt tone or model choice on success rates, although smaller effects cannot be ruled out. Overall, our results establish data voids as a practical inference-time attack surface for manipulating web-searching LLMs through strategically placed web content and highlight the urgent need for defenses against such exploits.
