### [The Bottleneck Is Retrieval, Not Reading: Diagnosing Evidence-Based Generation with RAG for Long Context Question Answering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26951)
**Score 10/10** · `cs.IR` · 2610.26951

> Critically analyzes limitations in RAG and introduces metrics for evidence-grounded answers.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is the dominant paradigm for grounding large language models in external corpora, but its similarity-driven, chunked pipeline has structural blind spots: it cannot anticipate which passages a question requires and discards document-level context that resolves cross-passage references. We introduce evidence-based generation (EBG), requiring models to return an answer with line-level citations, and a joint Ans+Evid metric that scores correctness and grounding simultaneously. We reformulate two corpora into evidence-grounded multi-document QA benchmarks- MultiHop-RAG and QASPER-MP, a multi-paper adaptation of QASPER with realistic short paper cues, and pair them with a controlled synthetic two-hop haystack and an evidence-augmented NoLiMa spanning 10K-1M characters. Across ten proprietary frontier and three open-weight LLMs, EBG isolates two RAG limitations that long-context models avoid: similarity retrieval surfaces any relevant chunk far more reliably than all required chunks for multi-fact queries, and chunking destroys document meta-information needed to resolve identity-bearing queries. EBG also exposes grounding failures invisible to answer-only evaluation, including incomplete citations in two-needle settings and a widening gap between Ans and Ans+Evid when context contradicts parametric knowledge. Data and code are available at https://github.com/debobanerjee/LC-EBG.

</details>

### [DeclineCraft: A RAG Abstention Benchmark for Jointly Measuring Combined Retrieval and Parametric Knowledge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28340)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.28340

> Directly relevant to RAG evaluation, establishing a framework for assessing retrieval performance.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Reliable retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) requires recognizing when neither retrieved documents nor parametric knowledge supports an answer. We introduce DeclineCraft, a dynamic abstention benchmark whose unanswerable questions target Minecraft feature additions after a corpus cut-off, with audits of possible parametric exposure. Later cut-offs support evaluation of future models. Across 27 LLMs, we measure Correct Abstention Rate (CAR; how often models acknowledge insufficient knowledge on unanswerable questions), Answerable Attempt Rate and Answer Accuracy. We find that (1) single-shot retrieval significantly raises CAR in 38 of 40 configurations (model and thinking setting) versus no retrieval. (2) Iterative RAG, in which the model self-assesses its context and can request up to two further retrieval rounds, significantly lowers CAR versus single-shot RAG in 30 of 40 configurations (mean 46.4%), with little change in Answer Accuracy (68.5% to 69.3%). Adding query rewriting and reranking to hybrid (dense & sparse) retrieval lowers CAR for all 12 models tested. (3) Larger models answer more accurately (Spearman $ρ= +0.60$ with total parameters), but neither model size nor release date shows a significant association with CAR. (4) Without retrieval, enabling thinking on the same weights lowers CAR significantly for 8 of 13 models (by 14% points on average), and the responses shift from acknowledged uncertainty to premise denial.

</details>

### [Contextual Candour: Role-Aware Graduated Disclosure for LLMs over Mixed-Sensitivity Contexts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28067)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.28067

> Focuses on graduated disclosure in LLM responses, critical for safety in LLM applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

LLMs increasingly operate over mixed-sensitivity contexts, where they must reason over information ranging from public to confidential while disclosing only what the user is authorised to receive. Existing approaches enforce access control as a binary decision: either fully answering or refusing, without regulating how much or what kind of information is revealed if access is granted, leading to over-refusal or unintended leakage. We introduce graduated disclosure, an access-control formulation in which the level of detail and sensitivity in a model's response is calibrated to the user's authorisation rather than treated as a grant-or-deny decision. We model graduated disclosure as a disclosure-aware generation problem and propose a reward-based optimisation framework that shapes responses toward level-appropriate abstractions while penalising unauthorised factual leakage, including paraphrases and approximations. We evaluate our approach on enterprise and clinical benchmarks and compare it with prompt-based, gating, and supervised baselines. Experiments show that our method substantially reduces information leakage while preserving correctness and utility. These results suggest that disclosure control must be integrated into the generation process itself to enable secure yet useful LLM deployments.

</details>

### [Memory as Code: Declarative Retrieval over Codified Long-Term Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27700)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.27700

> It discusses long-term memory systems and their retrieval mechanisms, directly relevant to conversational memory in chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

In long-term memory systems, memories are constructed from interaction histories and retrieved in response to queries, enhancing the reasoning ability of LLM agents. In complex scenarios, facts are scattered across sessions and their relationships evolve over time, making it difficult for existing memory systems to compose them to adapt to diverse queries. Motivated by the modularity and composability of code, we propose Memory as Code (MaC), a unified memory framework that jointly designs memory construction and retrieval through a shared code abstraction. MaC couples codified construction, which establishes executable memory abstractions, with declarative retrieval, which translates queries into structured retrieval programs. Specifically, codified construction builds a three-layer executable memory structure that progresses from an atomic function layer, through a relational layer, to a modular composition layer. Declarative retrieval recasts retrieval from relevance-based fact selection into declarative query processing over memory. Experiments on LoCoMo and LongMemEval show relative gains of up to 15.91% in overall LLM-judge accuracy over the strongest baseline in each setting, while substantially reducing token consumption, highlighting the effectiveness of memory as code for long-horizon memory tasks.

</details>

### [SHAM-Bench: A Span-level Hallucination Benchmark Across Multiple Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27370)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.27370

> The paper discusses hallucination detection in language models, which is directly relevant for RAG evaluation.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Span-level hallucination detection pinpoints unsupported content in LLM outputs, enabling actionable feedback and targeted mitigation. Existing evaluations for span-level hallucination detection methods are fragmented across tasks, annotation formats, and metrics, and most span-level datasets cover only a single NLG domain, making it difficult to assess whether a detection method actually generalizes across tasks and domains. In this paper, we introduce SHAM-Bench (Span-level Hallucination Benchmark Across Multiple Tasks), a unified benchmark for span-level hallucination detection spanning six NLG tasks, aggregated from existing human-annotated datasets totaling 6,461 examples under a common schema and evaluation protocol. Using SHAM-Bench, we evaluate existing hallucination detection methods and find that fine-tuned detectors often perform well in-domain but degrade under distribution shift, whereas prompt-based methods with reasoning are more robust across tasks. Our analysis further shows that subtle unsupported content remains difficult to detect and span-level evaluation is still challenged by ambiguous span boundaries and noisy labels. We release the dataset, evaluation code, and a public leaderboard to support future research on broadly generalizable hallucination detection.

</details>

### [GARRET: Generative Feedback for Region Retrieval in Visual Documents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27829)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2610.27829

> The GARRET framework for region retrieval in visual documents relates closely to document chunking and retrieval in RAG.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Multimodal retrieval-augmented generation over visually rich documents requires evidence at a finer granularity than entire pages. Existing visual retrievers typically index or return full pages, whereas training region-level retrievers often requires costly query-to-region annotations or noisy model-generated labels. We introduce GARRET, a region-retrieval framework trained from (page, question, answer) triplets through differentiable generative feedback. A shared context-preserving retriever produces page and region multi-vector representations. During training, their retrieval scores define a soft routing distribution over candidate region crops. An auxiliary vision-language model attends to each crop separately, aggregates crop-specific contexts using the routing weights, and predicts the question followed by the answer under a next-token prediction objective. Gradients through the routing distribution assign query-conditioned credit to the indexed region representations, without requiring query-to-region annotations. At inference, the auxiliary model is removed. GARRET also reuses the contextualized region representations, together with page summary tokens, to construct a compact page representation. We introduce ViDoRe-V3-Region, a corpus-wide benchmark containing 200,682 fixed candidate regions across eight domains. GARRET achieves a macro nDCG@10 of 0.4126, outperforming the strongest visual baseline by 2.94 points. On page retrieval, it remains comparable to ColQwen2.5-v0.2 while indexing 47.8% fewer vectors per page.

</details>

### [Joint Constraint Aggregation for Qualifier-Sensitive Retrieval-Augmented QA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27114)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2610.27114

> Directly addresses RAG performance issues and improves candidate answer selection process.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented QA can fail even when the correct answer is in the retrieved evidence. This can happen when a competing answer satisfies some, but not all, of the question's requirements and is mentioned frequently in the retrieved documents, which can make it appear to be the best-supported answer. To test whether this retrieval skew actually affects the decision, we manipulate it directly: increasing the prevalence of passages that favor the competing answer makes the model more likely to select it. This points to a decision problem that candidate-level support alone cannot resolve. We therefore introduce Joint Constraint Aggregation (JCA), which compares candidates against the same question-derived requirements using the retrieved evidence. Across three evaluation settings and three instruction-tuned backbones, JCA improves overall accuracy, and its advantage grows when retrieval favors a competing answer. Together, these results suggest that when partial matches and retrieval prominence pull models toward a competing answer, comparing candidates against the question's full requirements can make selection more reliable.

</details>

### [DocLayout-YOLO: Axis-Attentive Document Layout Detector with Mesh-Guided Synthetic Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28171)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CV` · 2610.28171

> Directly relevant for document parsing and layout analysis in RAG applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Accurate and efficient document layout analysis is critical for modern document parsing systems. However, although the YOLO framework represents a leading paradigm for the real-time detector, its adaptation to heterogeneous documents remains underexplored. To address this gap, we propose DocLayout-YOLO, a document-specialized real-time detector that integrates diverse synthetic document pretraining and page-axis context modeling. From the data perspective, we introduce MeshLayout-1M, a synthetic pretraining corpus generated by the proposed Mesh-candidate BestFit algorithm, which provides large-scale and diverse document layouts for pretraining and improves adaptation to heterogeneous document scenarios. From the model perspective, we propose Page-Axis Layout Attention (PALA), which captures long-range dependencies along the horizontal and vertical page axes while remaining computationally efficient. Extensive experiments demonstrate that DocLayout-YOLO achieves state-of-the-art performance on four heterogeneous document benchmarks, obtaining 72.4%/75.5%/96.0%/73.1% mAP50:95 on D4LA, M6Doc, HJDataset, and PRIMA-LAD, respectively. Compared with existing real-time detectors, DocLayout-YOLO establishes a superior accuracy-efficiency trade-off in document layout analysis. Code, model, and data are available at https://github.com/opendatalab/DocLayout-YOLO/tree/release-v2.

</details>

### [MAS-Guard: Unified Guardrails from Individual Agents to Multi-Agent LLM Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28397)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.28397

> Relevant to guardrails for LLM applications, which is crucial for production systems.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Agentic AI extends large language models (LLMs) from generating responses to planning and executing complex workflows, substantially expanding their utility while exposing applications to unsafe actions, information leakage, and adversarial manipulation. Multi-agent systems further expand these capabilities through specialization and collaboration, but distributed authority, heterogeneous roles, and opaque or potentially compromised participants make safeguarding even more challenging. Existing defenses address important individual threats but provide fragmented coverage across local execution, system-level interactions, and enforcement mechanisms. We introduce MAS-Guard, a unified framework that closes this gap for safeguarding single- and multi-agent AI applications containing both instrumentable and opaque agents. Within instrumentable agents, the proposed local Agent Guardrail (A-Guard) layer applies task-specific experts to assess agentic events. At the system level, developer-owned Agent Charters and a Control Plane combine deterministic structural checks of routes, agent capabilities, and message formats with semantic assessments of roles and message content. These complementary layers enable enforcement through a shared set of interventions: allow, reflection, confirmation, and block. Evaluated across six benchmarks covering diverse single- and multi-agent scenarios with three agent LLMs from different providers, MAS-Guard reduces the overall attack success rate by 71.5-86.3% relative to unguarded agents, while meeting the utility reference criterion on benign tasks.

</details>

### [Temporal Evidence-Parameter Co-Alignment for LLM Reasoning over Temporal Knowledge Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28176)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.28176

> Addresses temporal grounding and enhancing LLMs' reasoning, relating to RAG evaluation and retrieval.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large Language Models (LLMs) often struggle with temporally grounded reasoning because their static parametric memory is misaligned with the dynamic evolution of real-world knowledge. Although RAG and Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) provide practical ways to enhance LLMs, they remain insufficient for temporal reasoning. Existing retrieval methods typically prioritize semantic relevance while overlooking temporal validity, event order, and evolutionary dependencies, resulting in evidence that is relevant in isolation but temporally fragmented. Meanwhile, conventional PEFT methods usually employ fixed and input-agnostic low-rank updates, making it difficult to capture time-dependent entity semantics and relation drift. To address these limitations, we propose Temporal Evidence-Parameter Co-Alignment (TEPCA), a framework for LLM reasoning over TKGs. TEPCA aligns LLM reasoning with temporal knowledge at both the evidence and parameter levels. At the evidence level, it introduces time-constrained Dynamic Relevance-Guided Path (DRGP) pruning to preserve globally coherent temporal paths, together with Mutual-Information (MI)-based pruning to retain query-informative local facts under temporal constraints. At the parameter level, it integrates Dynamic-Gated LoRA (DG-LoRA), a timestamp-conditioned low-rank adaptation mechanism that dynamically modulates the effective adaptation subspace according to temporal context. This design enables LLMs to better capture fine-grained entity evolution and time-dependent relation semantics while preserving parameter efficiency. Experimental results on two TKG benchmarks demonstrate that TEPCA achieves state-of-the-art performance. Additional evaluations of DG-LoRA on eight commonsense QA datasets further verify its general input-adaptive capacity, scalability across LLM backbones, and interpretability.

</details>

### [Polarity Blindness in LLM Safety: A Systematic Study of Negation, Antonym Substitution, and Adversarial Framing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27908)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.27908

> Investigates safety challenges in LLMs, directly relevant to guardrails for LLM applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Negation remains a persistent challenge for language models across NLP tasks and modalities. We show that this weakness can undermine LLM safety guardrails through a structural vulnerability we term polarity blindness: the failure to recognize harmful intent after polarity-altering linguistic transformations. We study two forms of polarity inversion: grammatical negation and antonym replacement, and use synonym replacement as a polarity-preserving control. We further test whether an adversarial textual wrapper amplifies these failures. Using a 260-prompt benchmark across 10 state-of-the-art LLMs and 3 languages (English, Spanish, Czech), stratified by harmfulness potential and evaluated on a validated 0-5 harm scale, we find that polarity blindness affects most evaluated models, with mean harm scores reaching 4.12 for the most vulnerable model. The effect persists across typologically differing negation systems, suggesting that polarity blindness reflects a broader limitation in semantic robustness rather than a language- or grammar-specific failure.

</details>

### [ABench: Adversarily Benchmarking Large Language Models across Taxonomized Values](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27611)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.27611

> This paper investigates alignment of LLMs with cultural values, which relates to safety and guardrails for LLM applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Ensuring the alignment of Large Language Models (LLMs) with diverse cultural values is critical for their safe global deployment. However, existing alignment benchmarks are predominantly English-centric or confined to simple discriminative tasks. Moreover, they often fail to effectively evaluate model robustness against complex, adversarial inductions within specific cultural contexts. To address these limitations, we propose ABench, a fine-grained, handcrafted benchmark specifically tailored to taxonomized values in Chinese. Grounded in a rigorous hierarchical taxonomy comprising 158 derived value subcategories, ABench consists of 2,895 open-ended questions. These questions employ techniques such as role-playing and logical traps to probe the stability of models' stances in adversarially induced contexts. Furthermore, we propose a weighted automated evaluation framework that utilizes question-specific scoring criteria for nuanced assessment. Extensive experiments on 16 mainstream LLMs reveal that most models exhibit significant vulnerabilities when subjected to sophisticated adversary attacks on values (scoring below 60). This underscores the need for more robust, culturally specific alignment strategies. Our data will be publicly released to promote further research on taxonomized value alignment.

### [Detecting Errors in Large Language Model Responses: The Incremental Predictive Value of Hidden States](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27455)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.27455

> It addresses hallucination detection in LLM responses, aligning well with evaluation metrics for grounded QA.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Although large language models support a wide range of tasks, hallucinations remain a major obstacle to user trust. When a model generates an answer, both its internal processing and final output vary with the question. Internal states are often expected to offer richer signals for hallucination detection. However, studies using these states typically report their standalone discrimination performance, while output-side comparisons often use simple baselines with weaker modeling capacity. We integrate output-side information available at inference time into a combined baseline and compare detection performance before and after adding hidden states under identical data splits and evaluation rules. Experiments cover open-ended generation and constrained multiple-choice tasks across different models. Adding hidden states improves AUROC by approximately 0.004 and 0.039 on the two tasks, respectively. Hidden states thus provide additional predictive information, but strong standalone detection performance can still yield only limited additional gains.

### [AutoSim: A Self-Improving User Simulator for Conversational Search Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28054)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.IR` · 2610.28054

> The user simulator for conversational systems could enhance context management and conversation summarization.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

User simulators promise to evaluate conversational search systems at a scale human studies cannot reach, but only if their behavior is realistic enough to trust. We study AutoSim, a conversational-search user simulator whose relevance judgments rank a controlled pool of retrieval systems in agreement with NIST across three TREC iKAT years (Kendall's $τ$ of $0.66$, $0.91$, and $0.91$; all $p{\le}0.002$), an agreement that survives removing its persona. AutoSim self-improves against a held-out signal from real users, and we find that fine-tuning its weights makes it behave like real users, which in turn gives a more accurate estimate of how well a system serves them. Reaching that behavior takes more than prompting: when discriminating between real and simulated user sessions, no prompt we tried lowers a held-out real-versus-simulated classifier below AUC $0.85$, whereas fine-tuning the weights drives it to $0.45$, where neither the classifier nor a human reading full dialogues can tell it from a real user. That added realism is what makes the evaluation more accurate: where real users retrieve little, a prompted simulator over-estimates retrieval effectiveness by $+0.136$ nDCG and returns a relevant passage on $57\%$ of the turns where real users find none, enough to change a deployment decision. Only the weight-level simulator escapes this bias. We release the framework and code.

### [UIBK-Embedding: Effective Text Retrieval by Unifying Dense and Late-Interaction Retrieval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27195)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.IR` · 2610.27195

> Unifies dense and late-interaction retrieval, relevant for improving document retrieval in RAG applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Dense bi-encoders and late-interaction retrievers are usually treated as alternatives, yet they fail on different queries: one vector captures what a text is about, token-level matching finds its exact entities and phrases. Using both today means two models, two indexes and a score-fusion step. We present UIBK-Embedding-v1, a 179M-parameter English encoder that produces both representations in one pass and scores them with one unmodified MaxSim. The dense embedding is written into the list of token vectors as three extra vectors, and a one-dimensional tag keeps the two kinds of vectors from matching each other, so that plain MaxSim equals the token-level MaxSim plus a weighted dense cosine. The model therefore runs in a standard late-interaction index without changes; we test it with PLAID. A shared 18-layer trunk with a separate 4-layer top per head, and a cross-encoder distilled into the fused score, turn the two heads into one retriever. On BEIR, UIBK-Embedding-v1 reaches 59.79 nDCG@10. This is 2.57 points above LateOn, the strongest model of its size, and 2.10 points above it when both models read documents of the same length; UIBK-Embedding-v1 is ahead on all 15 datasets. On a decontaminated version of BEIR it stays ahead on average (61.55 against 60.36) and on 8 of 12 datasets. Among the 156 models with complete BEIR results in the MTEB results repository, only models with at least 1.5B parameters score higher. Code, model weights, and a technical blog post are available at https://github.com/DataScienceUIBK/uibk-embedding , https://huggingface.co/DataScience-UIBK/uibk-embedding-v1 , and https://huggingface.co/blog/abdoelsayed/uibk-embedding-v1 .

</details>

### [GRaSP: Latency-Aware Optimization for Geo-Distributed Retrieval-Augmented Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26938)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.IR` · 2610.26938

> Improves RAG by addressing latency in geo-distributed settings, relevant for performance in production.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is increasingly served across geo-distributed cloud and edge infrastructures, where wide-area latency, cross-region egress cost, and data-residency rules compete with retrieval quality. Conventional pipelines rank by semantic similarity alone and ignore where content lives, inflating tail latency and cost. We formalize the Geo-RAG Selection Problem (GRaSP), a drop-in re-scoring layer that decays relevance by latency and adds an optional cost penalty under a cardinality budget and per-region caps, with no retraining. We first settle its complexity: the base formulation is a linear objective over a partition matroid, solved optimally by an O(N log N) greedy algorithm, rather than NP-hard as a knapsack framing suggests, with hardness entering only under an additive egress budget. On a five-node CloudLab testbed emulating a four-region WAN over the Natural Questions corpus (2.68M passages), GRaSP's benefit is placement-dependent: under uniform placement it cuts fetch latency at negligible quality cost; under topical (residency-like) placement it traces a recall-latency Pareto frontier that dominates relevance-only, nearest-k, and a latency-budgeted baseline. At a moderate latency setting, GRaSP lowers P90 fetch latency by 41% while retaining 78% of relevance-only's recall, with sub-millisecond selection overhead, stable to 200 queries/s. Findings generalize across four public BEIR benchmarks. In end-to-end question answering, GRaSP reduces context-fetch latency by 43% while remaining within 0.062 absolute EM of relevance-only retrieval.

</details>
