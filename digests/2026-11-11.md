### [OpticalRAG: Pixel-Space Compression for Token-Efficient Retrieval-Augmented Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13675)
**Score 10/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.13675

> Presents a novel RAG approach using visual tokens for efficient retrieval, essential for the user's project.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is bottlenecked by the token budget of large language models: feeding long documents into the context window is expensive, while aggressive textual compression discards fine-grained information that is irreversibly lost in a one-dimensional token sequence. We argue that this bottleneck is not fundamental to information density but to the choice of compression \emph{modality}. Modern visual encoders can pack the contents of a rendered text page into roughly a hundred visual tokens with little semantic loss, opening a new compression-fidelity tradeoff that purely textual methods cannot reach. We present \textbf{OpticalRAG}, a framework that renders documents as page images, encodes them with a frozen vision encoder, and distills the retrieved visual tokens into as few as $16$ injected tokens before passing them to the LLM. Two ingredients make this work without expensive cross-modal training: (i) \emph{Encoder-Bridged Retrieval}, which projects text-query states into the visual feature space of the frozen encoder, through a projection shared with the compressor, to retrieve visual tokens from a text query, with no contrastive training; and (ii) \emph{Query-Driven Distillation}, a lightweight cross-attention compressor that condenses the retrieved visual tokens into a small set of query-conditioned prefix tokens. On LongBench-v2, BAMBOO, and LooGLE-v2, OpticalRAG achieves the best compression-aware accuracy (CAP) on all three benchmarks while injecting only $16$ tokens, and improves the closed-book Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct backbone by $5.17$ points on LongBench-v2.Code is available at https://github.com/brighterr/OpticalRAG.

### [Word-Level Alignment of OCR and Ground Truth under Zoning and Reading-Order Errors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13752)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.13752

> Focuses on OCR and ground truth alignment, highly relevant for document ingestion in RAG.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

OCR post-processing needs OCR text aligned word by word with its ground truth. Data sets rarely provide that mapping, and a monotone aligner can lose its place when zoning errors permute the reading order. We revisit this problem, the Zoning Problem of our 2019 paper, with a protocol that tests correctness under such permutations. We apply OCR-side reading-order permutations with construction-time word-level gold and score correct-match F1, cascade length and recovery. We compare the 2019 location-aware aligner, a re-anchoring extension of it, a RETAS-style baseline (RETAS-lite), a multi-region aligner that combines unique-word and unique n-gram anchors with chunk-to-window assignment, genalog's RETAS and the original Java RETAS. Experiments use synthetic character noise with block moves, paragraph swaps and column interleaving, historical newspaper reading-order permutations, geometry-derived gold for Tesseract OCR, and a small word-box set. Synthetic tests show where local tracking fails after large displacements and where unique-anchor methods collapse when unique anchors are scarce. On our synthetic boilerplate page the 2019 method had the highest F1 among those tested. Historical newspaper and geometry-derived evaluations carry the limitations of approximate gold and the article-order convention. The first multi-region version has a quadratic scaling cost. Local tracking remains fragile under large reading-order changes; the tested anchor-based methods handle many of those cases but fail when unique anchors are scarce. Approximate gold, the article-order convention, missing residual annotation, and the lack of modern multi-column word-level ground truth remain limitations.

### [From Search to Decision: How Post-Training Shapes Information-Seeking Capabilities in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14149)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.14149

> Examines information-seeking capabilities essential for enhancing RAG systems and retrieval effectiveness.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Information seeking in large language model (LLM) systems increasingly involves decisions about when to acquire information, where to search, and how to evaluate and use evidence. Recent post-training methods have improved several such behaviors, but their objectives and evaluations span different tasks and system settings. This paper organizes research around six interrelated information-seeking capabilities and examines the learning signals, optimization methods, update loci, and credit-assignment mechanisms used to develop them. We synthesize empirical progress in targeted behaviors, end-to-end performance, search control, and generalization. Existing studies report gains in retrieval routing, query refinement, multi-tool exploration, evidence-grounded reasoning, and resource-aware search, although the scope of behavioral evidence varies across capabilities. We identify open challenges in estimating prospective retrieval utility, coordinating information-seeking decisions, and adapting to unfamiliar retrieval environments, and discuss evaluation practices for interpreting future progress.

### [DocFS: File Systems as Agentic Reasoning Environments for Complex Multimodal Document Question Answering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12957)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.12957

> Directly addresses complex multimodal document interaction for question answering, aligning closely with RAG objectives.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

VLM-based agents solve complex tasks by iteratively interacting with environments, and their effectiveness depends heavily on how the environment exposes information and provides tools for acting on it. However, constructing an effective environment for complex multimodal document question answering remains challenging: documents usually span many pages, with multimodal contents distributed across hierarchical sections and linked by cross-modal associations, while existing methods organize documents into structured representations that address only the information side of the environment and lack tools aligned with the structure, limiting agents' ability to fully exploit it. We observe that file systems naturally combine structure organization with aligned tools: directories and files encode document structure, while file operations provide rich tools to access file contents. Building on this insight, we propose DocFS, a file-system-based agentic framework that reorganizes raw documents as file systems, where directories mirror the section hierarchy and explicit links connect text with figures, tables, and equations. Based on well-established file system tools such as navigation, search, and reading, agents can iteratively explore the document structure, locate relevant evidence, and understand fine-grained local information. Paired with Qwen3.5-Plus, DocFS achieves state-of-the-art performance on MMLongBench-Doc and FinRAGBench-V, outperforming the strongest baselines by 2.9 and 4.3 percentage points, respectively, and is up to 10.3x faster with up to 10.1x fewer tokens than the strongest baseline during reasoning.

### [TAMEing the Open-World Personalization: Towards Open-Set Personalized MLLM Assistant](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14118)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CV` · 2611.14118

> Directly addresses personalization in MLLMs, which is relevant to long-term memory and conversation management in chat systems.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Personalizing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) as intelligent assistants has recently emerged as an active research topic, where an MLLM is expected to go beyond generic, one-size-fits-all replies, and instead generate responses grounded in user-specific objects, entities, and concepts encountered during interactions. Nevertheless, existing studies mainly focus on personalizing MLLMs on a static, predefined set of personalized concepts and require users to manually and frequently extend this set to accommodate newly emerging concepts, which is infeasible in dynamic-world scenarios. To address this limitation, we recast conventional MLLM personalization as an Open-Set problem, and propose TAME-O, the first long-context open-set personalized MLLM assistant. Specifically, TAME-O introduces a training-free, plug-and-play Concept Reconciliation Skill (CR Skill). It enables the assistant to automatically unlock novel concepts during prolonged human-MLLM interactions while preventing confusion with previously unlocked ones, allowing the MLLM to continually grasp more and more concepts and progressively refine the modeling of known ones. Extensive experiments under the open-set personalization setting underscore the efficacy of our method.

</details>

### [FERN: Cross-Benchmark Table Retrieval for Text-to-SQL with Frozen Retrievers and Learned Ranking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14477)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.14477

> Directly relates to retrieval strategies in Text-to-SQL, applicable to document ingestion for RAG.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Text-to-SQL systems over large pooled catalogs must first retrieve the tables a question needs. We compare against the published table recall of RASL, which uses Claude 3.5 models to extract keywords and select tables, on pooled BIRD, Spider, and Fiben catalogs. FERN (Feature-Engineered Ranking with No generative LLM) instead fuses three frozen retrievers into 100 candidates, describes each with up to 120 features, and reorders them with LightGBM rankers fitted once on BIRD's training questions. On Spider, where it was not fitted, its candidate pool alone reaches RASL's published recall at fifteen tables (98.0), and the ranker raises the share of questions with every gold table in the top five (complete@5) from 83.9% to 95.6%. At five tables, FERN's recall is within one point of RASL's on Spider (96.7 vs 97.0) and on BIRD with its evidence hints (96.6 vs 97.5), and 8.2 points below on BIRD without them (89.3). On 121 real BEAVER questions, without BEAVER fitting, its recall at fifteen (82.1) falls between two BEAVER-trained retrievers (80.8, 84.5), but its complete@15 is below both (49.6 vs 54.5 and 62.0). On Fiben, it falls far short at fifteen tables (54.2 vs 69.2, different question sets), although its recall at fifty is numerically close to RASL's question-only retrieval result (80.7 vs 79.4). On BIRD dev with evidence, non-retrieval features raise complete@5 by 7.7 points over retrieval signals alone. On BIRD dev, median latency is 208 ms per question on one A100, without hosted model calls.

</details>

### [StaRSE: Compact Russian Sentence Embeddings with a Sign-Coded Static Encoder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14236)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.14236

> Concerns compact sentence embeddings, useful for efficient vector storage in RAG applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Transformer sentence encoders provide strong retrieval quality, but their inference cost becomes a bottleneck when embeddings must be produced on CPUs or refreshed frequently. We introduce StaRSE, a compact Russian static sentence encoder that removes Transformer inference at deployment time: token directions are stored as sign bits, token norms are stored separately, and sentences are encoded by mean pooling over norm-scaled sign directions. StaRSE is initialized from ruBERT-base, fine-tuned on Russian adjacent-sentence and task-shaped pairs, and post-trained with a contrastive stage that directly optimizes the sign-projected token table. On RuMTEB, StaRSE reaches a task mean of $51.16$, $7.98$ points above the strongest included static baseline. The packaged model occupies $11.3$ MiB and has a mean CPU latency of $0.08$ ms per sentence, compared with an estimated $55.7$ MiB 16-bit parameter footprint and $1.43$ ms mean CPU latency for the closest-quality contextual encoder. Ablations examine adjacent-sentence supervision, token norms, and direct sign-projected post-training.

### [POEM: Output-Relative Block-Sparse Attention for Long-Context Prefilling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13578)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.13578

> Discusses defense mechanisms which are essential for safe LLM app deployment.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large language models (LLMs) have shown strong capabilities across diverse applications, making both safety and utility essential for reliable deployment. Jailbreak attacks use diverse forms of adversarial prompting to bypass alignment safeguards and induce LLMs to generate harmful responses. Some representation-based defenses regulate internal activations using predefined steering directions or learned safety regions. However, adapting intervention to each input without a predefined representational target while limiting over-refusal remains a challenge. In this paper, we propose Target-free Entropy-guided Residual Modulation (TERM), an inference-time defense that uses prompt-calibrated attention entropy deviations to guide adaptive modulation of the model's own residual updates. Attention patterns vary across prompts and decoding steps, making raw entropy values difficult to interpret as a consistent signal for intervention. We therefore calibrate attention entropy against each prompt's own prefill baseline to establish an input-specific reference for reducing unnecessary intervention on benign requests. Adaptive scale normalization and temporal accumulation then account for differences in deviation scale and persistence to determine intervention strength. The resulting signal controls bounded scaling of the model's own residual updates, without relying on a predefined safety direction. We compare our method with seven defenses on seven jailbreak attacks and two over-refusal benchmarks across Llama, Qwen, and Gemma models, including Qwen3 models from 0.6B to 32B. Results show our method improves jailbreak robustness while maintaining low over-refusal on benign safety-sensitive requests with modest inference overhead.

### [BioMem: Agentic Memory for Biomedical LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13426)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.13426

> Focuses on conversational memory and retrieval quality in LLMs, relevant for memory management in chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated considerable potential in biomedical applications, while agentic memory systems have emerged as a promising approach to preserving and utilizing information across tasks and interactions. However, a fundamental question remains: how effectively and reliably do these systems retain, retrieve, update, and utilize biomedical information? In this study, we systematically investigate agentic memory in the biomedical domain, focusing on task effectiveness, retrieval quality, information fidelity, and long-term robustness. We evaluate five memory approaches across four LLMs in three representative settings: biomedical knowledge reasoning, clinical prediction, and long-term conversational memory. Our analyses reveal how memory construction, evidence relevance, and conversational history shape downstream biomedical performance, highlighting the distinct challenges of retrieving relevant information, preserving its fidelity, and effectively utilizing it for reasoning. Building on these findings, we introduce Hybrid Memory Retrieval and Utilization (HMRU), a training-free framework that jointly enhances evidence accessibility and task-aware utilization to improve biomedical reasoning and long-term conversational memory. HMRU achieves an average relative improvement of 8.6% across biomedical question-answering and conversational memory benchmarks, with additional gains in selected clinical prediction tasks. It further reduces conversational answer errors by 24.9% and closes 48.0% of the performance gap to full-context inference. Our findings provide a systematic characterization of biomedical agentic memory and practical insights into developing more effective and reliable memory-augmented LLM systems.

### [Activation-Guided Graph Retrieval for Cognitive Memory Reconstruction in Language Model Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13127)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.13127

> Proposes an architecture for retrieval-augmented systems with a focus on memory and evidence retrieval.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented language model systems generally select context by query-conditioned relevance. We argue that long-lived AI systems require a second operator: activation-conditioned memory availability. We specify an architecture in which request and goal cues seed an authorized memory graph, activation and fan attenuation allocate a bounded traversal frontier, retrieved entities recursively emit cues, and a typed evidence graph is reconstructed before generation. The specification yields five architectural consequences about candidate selection, traversal control, graph regularization, evidence reconstruction, and reinforcement. We derive implementation-level cost estimates and define ablations, failure conditions, and a reproducible evaluation protocol. The central claim is narrowed or falsified if a simpler graph reranker matches full traversal on causal and temporal recovery under equal budgets. This architecture-and-analysis preprint reports no benchmark measurements. If the claim holds, retrieval returns the evidence path that makes a memory available, not only a ranked list.

### [Ontology-Guided Knowledge Graph Construction and Robust Evidence Retrieval for Indian Knowledge Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14050)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.14050

> Discusses knowledge graph construction and evidence retrieval relevant to document ingestion in RAG.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieving evidence from Indian Knowledge Systems is complicated by transliteration variants, ambiguous names, and relations distributed across passages. We present an ontology-guided knowledge graph (KG) pipeline that links typed entities and relations extracted with Qwen3-32B to original textual evidence. Retrieval combines sense-aware grounding, bounded graph traversal, lexical anchors, relationship-vector search, and passage reranking. We evaluate KG alone, single-vector retrieval (SV), and KG unions with SV and Entity Notes on 320 questions from Dhara Bench, matching the per-question candidate count across internal systems under BGE and Qwen rerankers. Standalone KG has the highest Precision@5 under both rerankers and the highest MRR@20 in Counting, Composite, and Multi-hop among the evaluated internal configurations. With Qwen, KG achieves Multi-hop MRR@20 of 0.8722, compared with 0.7622 for the fully reranked KG + SV union. The KG + SV + Entity Notes union instead provides the highest overall Recall@20 (0.5134), compared with 0.4347 for KG. On the 15 Multi-hop questions, Qwen KG achieves complete-evidence MRR@20 of 0.4651 versus 0.3372 for SV, while both full unions complete ten questions within twenty results compared with nine for KG. Gemini 3.7 Flash, evaluated through manually reviewed source mappings, re mains stronger on overall MRR and nDCG, while internal systems have category-specific advantages. All comparisons are descriptive. A separate deployment timing study measures mean retrieval times of 20.374 s for KG and 0.646 s for SV. The findings support selecting KG or its unions according to the required balance of early relevance, evidence completeness, and retrieval cost.

### [Beyond the Document: Compiled Context and Governed Agents for Any Work Product](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13055)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.13055

> Discusses packaging work products with structured context, relevant to knowledge management in RAG systems.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

A document preserves the visible result of professional work but often discards the research, calculations, assumptions, evidence, and authorization boundaries behind it. We study a different unit of delivery: a Portable Agent Format (PAF) package that carries a work product together with compiled context, evidence, provenance, and rules for subsequent interaction. To test whether this context remains useful beyond the process that produced it, we evaluate four new packages, created after the hosted and offline readers were fixed: an investment proposal, fund research, financial-statement analysis, and an engineering incident review. Two packages were produced by specialized agents and two through a general-purpose assistant's packaging skill. Across 400 frozen questions and 12,000 evaluated responses, a hosted governed reader succeeds on 96% of questions versus 86% with instructions plus raw-file retrieval (Holm-adjusted p < 10^-7). On the 260 answerable questions per condition, success is 94% versus 79%; the gap varies by package. Source-material chat and instructions alone reach 48% and 74% overall. A separate 0.8-billion-parameter offline reader succeeds on 69% overall, but 54% on answerable questions. Its -1 response rate is 2.9%, combining materially wrong and unauthorized answers, versus 56% with the source and instructions alone. Ablations suggest advantages for the integrated selector and package-adapted writer, without isolating representation from training effects. The same run exposes a failure: offline retrieval resolves none of the 40 historical-record questions in one financial-data package. These findings support treating compiled knowledge as part of the delivered work product, while leaving independent standards interoperability and end-to-end governance conformance unproven.

### [DisasterAgent: An Evidence-Driven Agent for Verifiable Disaster Report Generation From Remote Sensing Imagery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13776)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CV` · 2611.13776

> Relevant to generating reports from data, addresses evidence grounding which can apply to document ingestion in RAG.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Remote sensing disaster assessment is moving from simple damage classification toward comprehensive report-level analysis. In this setting, responders need not only damage descriptions but also verifiable evidence to guide response and recovery. Recent vision-language models (VLMs) and tool-augmented agents offer strong multimodal reasoning and perception, but they struggle to provide the evidence required for high-stakes verification. To address these limitations, we propose DisasterAgent, an evidence-driven agent that uses a Perception-Augmentation-Generation workflow to generate verifiable disaster reports from bitemporal optical imagery. DisasterAgent first invokes a specialized vision expert toolkit in the perception phase to build a Spatiotemporal Perception Graph (STPG), serving as a structured memory to store and organize disaster-induced changes across space and time. Evidence-based Augmentation (EA) then extracts evidence from the STPG, attaching inline citations that form a closed evidence loop from each statement to the supporting visual evidence. Extensive experiments on the DisasterM3 and RSCC benchmarks demonstrate that DisasterAgent consistently outperforms general-domain VLMs, remote sensing-specialized models, and existing remote sensing agents. Notably, compared with the original model, it improves the average score for disaster report generation on DisasterM3 by 13.5\% and ST5-SCS on RSCC by 15.34\%, providing a more accurate and verifiable solution for disaster emergency response. The code will be available at https://github.com/ZhijianLi2003/DisasterAgent.

</details>
