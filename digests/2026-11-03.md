### [Re$^3$Rank: Listwise Reranking with Reasoning-Conditioned Retrieval Heads](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02550)
**Score 10/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.02550

> The paper presents a novel reranking system that enhances retrieval quality, crucially relevant to improving RAG architectures.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Attention-based rerankers score each candidate by the attention mass that retrieval heads route to it from the question, but the question, serving as a fixed query, carries limited information for locating relevant evidence. We propose Re$^3$Rank, a listwise reranker built on reasoning-conditioned retrieval heads (RRHeads). A generator first writes a reasoning trace over the question and the candidates, and a scorer then uses the trace tokens as the attention query of a few selected RRHeads, reading relevance as continuous scores. We present a straight-through estimator to propagate the ranking loss through the discrete trace, so that the generator and the scorer can be trained jointly for ranking. Averaged over 9 benchmarks, Re$^3$Rank outperforms 11 baseline rerankers: our 4B variant exceeds the strongest baseline by 3.11 NDCG@10 points on average, and even our 0.6B variant surpasses rerankers with up to 32B parameters.

</details>

### [EviCast: Forecasting Retrieval Gains Through Shared Response Calibration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02387)
**Score 10/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.02387

> Offers a framework for evaluating and optimizing RAG retrieval gains, a must-read.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) extends large language models with external evidence, yet relevant evidence does not ensure that a generator can use it. Indiscriminate retrieval can degrade task performance while adding retrieval and long-context inference costs. Existing methods neither determine before large-scale execution whether a generator, task distribution, and retriever configuration is worth deploying nor diagnose multiple candidate retrievers under a shared budget. We introduce EviCast, a framework that forecasts retrieval value through interventional evidence-state decomposition. EviCast measures a generator's response to controlled evidence states once and combines this shared profile with the offline evidence-state distribution of each candidate retriever, yielding the end-to-end net utility and its 95% lower confidence bound without executing every RAG configuration. A state-refinement analysis characterizes when its binary and topology-aware instantiations are exact and when shared calibration is statistically more efficient than per-retriever calibration. In frozen evaluations, EviCast correctly decides all 14 HotpotQA retrieval configurations with a forecast error of 2.75 F1 points while reducing diagnostic generation calls by 80%, and it attains the lowest forecast error among seven predictors in the low-budget regime. Its topology-aware instantiation, EviCast-T, captures the complementarity between terminal and bridge evidence, correctly deciding all 16 configurations on MuSiQue and HotpotQA and 23 of 24 after prospective transfer to a third dataset. These results show that retrieval deployment depends on both evidence availability and the generator's ability to convert evidence structure into stable net utility. Code is available at https://github.com/YiboWang2002/EviCast.

### [Dhārā Bench: An Evidence-Oriented Retrieval Benchmark for Indian Knowledge Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03196)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.03196

> Presents a retrieval benchmark specific to knowledge systems, directly applicable to RAG evaluation and document chunking.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Dhārā Bench is a retrieval benchmark for Indian Knowledge Systems (IKS), with 325 questions in 12 categories and 60,121 text chunks from 15 source groups. The categories were developed based on 4,500 questions submitted to the Dhārā application; the evaluation set combines corpus-aligned application queries with expert-contributed questions. The annotations comprise 7,578 question-chunk relevance links involving 6,847 distinct corpus chunks. For 34 composite and multi-hop questions, 401 conjugate groups specify alternative sets of passages that jointly support each question. The category results expose particularly weak verse retrieval, while group scores reveal gaps between finding individual passages and completing their support. The corpus and evaluation code are available at https://github.com/Avinash2236/Dhara-Bench.

### [DR Pixel: Page-Native Deep Research with Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03097)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.03097

> Directly focuses on enabling document retrieval and reasoning, highly relevant to your project needs.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Complex documents such as scientific papers contain rich information in tables, figures, and equations; however, existing deep research (DR) agents typically reduce papers to OCR-extracted text. We instead ask whether vision-language models (VLMs) can enable page-native deep research: directly retrieving and reasoning over rendered document page images. We introduce DR Pixel, the first open-source end-to-end multimodal DR agent that uses rendered page images as its sole retrieval and reasoning unit, operating over a corpus of 1.06M pages. To evaluate this setting, we construct DRPixelBench, a benchmark on scientific papers requiring reasoning over text, figures, tables, and equations. Surprisingly, page-native DR already works well without training, outperforming OCR-based baselines across state-of-the-art models like GPT-5.4, Gemini-3.5-Flash, and Qwen3.6-27B, while requiring fewer tool calls. We next turn to DR training. A key challenge is that teacher trajectories for agentic supervised finetuning are noisy, mixing critical reasoning and action steps with redundant or failed attempts. To address this, we introduce counterfactual credit reweighting, a fine-grained credit assignment method that weights individual reasoning and action steps by their counterfactual contribution to the final answer. Our training improves retrieval recall by about 15 points and final accuracy by over 10 points on DRPixelBench, and likewise outperforms the untrained baseline on four existing benchmarks. On out-of-distribution ViDoRe-V3, DR Pixel comes within 1.8 points of an oracle that is given the gold pages directly. Our results suggest that the conventional OCR-then-retrieve pipeline is not only unnecessary but suboptimal: directly retrieving and reasoning over visual document pages makes complex DR both more effective and more efficient.

### [An Experimental Study of Hybrid Retrieval, NLI and Open-source LLMs for Scientific Claim Verification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03093)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.03093

> Investigates hybrid retrieval methodologies with implications for scientific claim verification, closely aligned with RAG interests.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Claim verification is the task of deciding whether a source text supports a claim, contradicts it, or lacks sufficient evidence. Natural Language Inference (NLI) models can judge whether a single sentence supports or contradicts a claim, but they struggle when the evidence is spread across several sentences. Conversely, Large Language Models (LLMs) can reason across sentences, but may draw conclusions the text does not support. In this experimental study, we test whether a conservative fusion rule, labeling a claim Support or Contradict only when both models agree and defaulting to Not Enough Evidence otherwise, combines their strengths. We construct a benchmark of 2,035 claims from SciFact and CliniFact (858 Support, 152 Contradict, 1,025 Not Enough Evidence). We retrieve the top five most relevant sentences per claim using a hybrid of semantic and lexical (BM25) retrieval, which slightly outperforms either method alone. These retrieved sentences are then provided to every verification model. We then compare a DeBERTa-v3-base-mnli-fever-anli NLI baseline against Llama 3.1 8B, Qwen3 8B, and Mistral 7B. Qwen3 achieves the highest Macro-F1 (0.7937), followed by Llama 3.1 (0.7204) and Mistral (0.6388), while the NLI baseline reaches only 0.3157. Contrary to our hypothesis, fusion degrades performance, dropping Macro-F1 to 0.7392, 0.6730, and 0.5991, respectively. To understand this decline, we analyze agreements, disagreements, and common error patterns between the NLI and LLM verifiers. Our error analysis shows that NLI and LLM disagreements often result in correct LLM predictions being overturned.

</details>

### [TRIO: Task Refinement with Individual Optimization for Retrieval-Augmented Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02810)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.02810

> The paper focuses on improving RAG through task refinement and optimization, directly aligning with user interests.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

This paper studies retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), which leverages external knowledge to improve the performance of language models. Despite the remarkable progress, current RAG approaches are still far from satisfactory due to inadequate retrieval and potential hallucinations. Towards this end, we propose a novel approach named Task Refinement with Individual Optimization (TRIO) for RAG. The core idea of our TRIO is to equip three well-designed agents with an iterative refinement paradigm by aligning their individual optimization objectives. Specifically, we first expand ambiguous queries with a query extension agent and quantify the reward based on the improved answer quality, which can be utilized to refine our agent for higher expansion efficiency. To enhance the retrieval quality, we include a unanimity voting to annotate the expanded query as insufficient or sufficient, and their generative outcomes are utilized as ground truth to optimize the judge agent. To further improve evidence utilization and answer accuracy, an answer agent is optimized with dynamic matching-based rewards with curriculum learning for final outputs. Extensive experiments across multiple benchmark datasets validate the effectiveness of the proposed TRIO in comparison with state-of-the-art approaches. Our code is available at https://github.com/yingtaoren/TRIO.

### [Scalaris: A Scalable Enterprise Memory Architecture for Conversational AI Assistants](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02612)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.02612

> The paper discusses a long-term memory architecture for conversational AI, directly relevant to context management in chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Enterprise conversational systems require memory to personalize interactions, retain information across sessions, and maintain conversational coherence. At enterprise scale, however, memory must not only retrieve relevant information accurately but also provide it efficiently to the answer-generation model. Existing memory systems face a trade-off between retrieval accuracy and context efficiency. Retrieving more context can improve recall but increases inference cost and latency. We present Scalaris, a scalable enterprise memory architecture that addresses this trade-off through a unified long-term and short-term memory design. We propose that user interactions and assistant responses possess distinct characteristics and roles in a conversation: user interactions capture evolving knowledge about the user and provide value across sessions, whereas assistant responses must be stored efficiently so that only information relevant to the current query is retrieved. When integrated with our proprietary closed-source AI assistants, Scalaris improved overall response quality by 36 %. Together, these results establish Scalaris as a cost-effective framework for building scalable, personalized conversational AI systems.

### [Chain on Demand: Deferred Evidence Organization for Long-Term Conversational Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02480)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.02480

> Directly addresses long-term conversational memory, crucial for chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Long-term conversational memory faces a timing mismatch: write-time summaries and graphs organize history before a future query is known, while independent retrieval can allocate a limited evidence budget to redundant or fragmented turns. We introduce Chain on Demand (CoD), a query-time evidence organization framework over an unchanged turn-level memory. Given a broad candidate pool, CoD constructs a temporary evidence chain by allocating a fixed anchor budget according to query relevance, lexical diversity relative to selected anchors, and newly covered query terms, followed by deterministic restoration of each selected anchor's same-session neighborhood. The resulting chain is used only for the current query, while the underlying conversational memory remains unchanged. In a paired comparison over all 1,986 LoCoMo questions, CoD achieves 82.2% accuracy on 1,540 non-adversarial questions and 76.3% on all 1,986 questions. On LongMemEval-S, one fixed configuration that indexes both user and assistant turns obtains 86.6% across all 500 questions. These results show that long-term memory can preserve the original conversation while deferring evidence organization until the information need is known.

### [LEANGUARD: Lean-Verified Guardrails for Tool-Using Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02332)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.02332

> This paper presents LeanGuard, which addresses input/output filtering and prompt injection defense, essential for LLM application safety.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Tool-using language-model agents often execute actions that their domain policies forbid. We present LeanGuard, a runtime that checks every proposed tool call against a policy written in Lean4 before the call reaches the environment. Policies combine state predicates with scoped past-time temporal operators over the execution history, evaluation is fail-closed, and the decision procedure is proved equivalent to a LeanLTL semantics. A denied call can be corrected in two ways. With feedback, the agent receives the violated rule and a hint; with proof-guided repair, Lean metaprogramming searches for a nearby call that preserves the user's intent and proves that the policy admits it, without the model. On Tau-Bench with five Qwen models (0.8B-27B), unguarded agents execute at least 33 calls that the Lean policy forbids, while LeanGuard executes none; with feedback, task success does not change significantly. On DTap, LeanGuard reduces direct prompt-injection success from 57.9% to 5.9%, and in telecom, where benign tasks need no writes, benign success is unchanged. Feedback reduces repeated blocked calls and raises task success for four of five models over a bare denial. On replayed denials, repair proves an admissible edit for 88 of 252 denials caused by argument values, in a median of 2,ms.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Existing approaches support such retrieval through explicit graph structures or iterative query reformulation, introducing graph-construction or query-time overhead. We present Evidence-Guided Adaptive Graph Retrieval (EAGR), a framework in which accumulated evidence dynamically determines which semantic directions to explore next, and which requires no dedicated graph database and no explicit graph materialization. ### [Federated Vector Retrieval across Heterogeneous VectorDBs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01514)

> Directly addresses federated retrieval across heterogeneous vector databases, relevant for vector search.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Vector databases are now core infrastructure for AI retrieval systems, yet many real deployments must serve queries over multiple independently constructed vectorDBs built with different embedding models. This creates a systems problem of federated retrieval over heterogeneous vectorDBs: vectors and similarity scores are not directly comparable across embedding spaces, while fully resolving this incompatibility through corpus-wide re-embedding and re-indexing is often operationally prohibitive. Existing methods therefore face a difficult trade-off between retrieval quality, construction cost, and query throughput. Across these settings, HVMap consistently achieves a better quality--cost--throughput trade-off than baselines, achieving up to 3.59X higher nDCG@10, up to 2.01X higher query throughput, and construction time as low as 0.3% of baseline methods. These results show that HVMap makes heterogeneous vectorDB federation practical at scale.

### [Characterizing When Language Models Take Harmful Actions to Protect Their Assigned Goals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03374)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.03374

> The investigation into harmful actions relates to safety measures in LLM applications, particularly relevant for guardrails.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Recent language model (LM) safety failures have largely been studied as disparate phenomena. In agentic misalignment, a model blackmails an executive to avoid being replaced. In alignment faking, it complies with harmful requests during training to keep its values from being modified. We argue that these behaviours share a common decision structure: a model assigned a goal must either take a harmful action to preserve it or refuse and lose it. We formalize this as a goal-harm conflict and present the same conflict under various elicitation protocols that range from compact forced-choice prompts to simulated agentic environments. These protocols span two perspectives on LM decision-making, which we term (i) the logit view, reading preferences from answer-token probabilities, and (ii) the sampling view, classifying open-ended responses with an LM judge. Under the logit view, we show that goal importance and harm severity additively shape how a model resolves these value conflicts. Under the sampling view, we derive two factorizations of the harmful-action probability that safety evaluations commonly report. The first measures how well a preference read from answer tokens carries over into behaviour, such as making a harmful tool call. The second attributes a low harmful-action rate either to refusal or to responses that reach no resolution. We then show how fictional context changes a model's preference under the logit view, and how tool use and partial observability -- two features of agentic evaluations -- change its harmful-action rate under the sampling view. Our work offers a descriptive framework for studying how LMs resolve such value conflicts and for estimating low-probability harmful actions at a finer granularity than the aggregate harm rates commonly reported in safety evaluations.

### [CREDO: Evidence-Conditioned Credit Assignment for Temporal Reasoning Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01643)

### [R$^2$-Omni: Reducing Structural and Precision Redundancy in Omni-LLMs for Long-Form Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03247)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CV` · 2611.03247

> The focus on optimizing token constructions in language models may have implications for long-form generation and contextual query processing.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Omni-modal large language models (Omni-LLMs) typically process large numbers of multimodal tokens from audio-visual inputs, incurring substantial computational costs and a large memory footprint for the key-value (KV) cache. Aggressive token pruning can alleviate this problem while maintaining competitive performance on multiple-choice tasks, which require only a single option as output. However, we observe substantial quality degradation when generating long-form, open-ended responses. We attribute this gap to query-guided selection, which can retain sufficient evidence for answer selection while discarding details needed for comprehensive generation. This motivates reducing overlapping information across tokens, termed structural redundancy, while preserving diverse content. However, the retained representations may still contain precision redundancy due to unnecessarily high numerical precision in their cached values. To address these two levels of redundancy in token representations, we introduce a training-free framework named R$^2$-Omni. First, Structure-Aware Token Consolidation (STC) consolidates redundant representations using spatiotemporal similarity in video and local continuity in audio. Then, Semantic-Sensitivity Guided Precision Allocation (SSPA) allocates value precision to the retained representations according to the estimated reduction in attention-output error under quantized keys, with query attention and audio-visual alignment providing a semantic prior. Extensive experiments on long-form generation benchmarks demonstrate an average 5.0$\times$ reduction in the memory footprint while preserving 97.98% of baseline generation quality and achieving a 1.48$\times$ end-to-end speedup, highlighting the benefits of jointly reducing structural and precision redundancy.

</details>
