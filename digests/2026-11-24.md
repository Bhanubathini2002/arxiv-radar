### [LLM-Based Assistant for Dog Care Guidance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29286)
**Score 10/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.29286

> Explores RAG architecture and performance in pet care advice, highly relevant to the user's interests.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

This study investigates whether domain grounding through Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and parameter-efficient fine-tuning using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) can enhance the accuracy and reliability of pet care advice generated by large language models (LLMs). We curated a dataset of breed-specific dog care information and used it to develop two systems: a RAG pipeline based on TinyLlama-1.1B-Chat-v1.0, and a fine-tuned LLaMA-3.2-1B model adapted using LoRA. Both systems were evaluated on 15 dog care questions. The RAG model achieved a perfect average score of 2.0, outperforming the fine-tuned model (mean = 1.47). Statistical analyses, including Welch's t-test, Mann-Whitney U, and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (all p < 0.05), confirmed the significance of the performance gap. Our findings suggest that retrieval-based domain grounding leads to more accurate, consistent, and trustworthy responses than standalone fine-tuning in specialized LLM applications.

### [DM-Lab at TREC 2026 RAG: Multi-Query Retrieval, Ensemble Judging, and Claim-Pack Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28233)
**Score 10/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.28233

> Directly addresses RAG architecture and evaluation through a detailed retrieval framework for evidence-grounded generation.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We present DM-Lab-RAG-2026, a framework developed for the TREC 2026 Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) track. The framework integrates large-scale evidence retrieval and citation-grounded answer generation over the 553-million-document ClimbMix-400B corpus. The retrieval architecture combines multi-query BM25, reciprocal rank fusion, ensemble relevance assessment, listwise reranking, and adaptive retrieval depth. The generation pipeline employs a claim-first strategy to extract evidence-linked atomic claims, construct cited responses, and verify source support. Across 22 development topics, the retrieval system achieved a mean nDCG@30 of 0.604. In the official evaluation, it attained a set precision of 0.455 and a manual nugget gain of 0.910. Additional experiments examine the influence of reranking, evidence selection, and citation verification on system performance. The evaluation demonstrates the potential of diversified lexical retrieval and language-model-based relevance assessment for large-scale evidence discovery. It further identifies evidence preservation and structured answer construction as key considerations for reliable RAG. This work provides a reproducible framework for investigating retrieval effectiveness and evidence-grounded generation.

### [Uncovering the Tells: Efficient Black-Box Audits for LLM Data Contamination](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30639)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.30639

> Proposes a black-box auditing method for LLMs which is crucial for guardrails and safety in LLM applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Existing methods for assessing training data contamination in Large Language Models achieve high accuracy, but fundamentally rely on gray-box access, rendering them inapplicable to fully black-box environments. This limitation forces auditors to rely on full-sequence sampling or rigid heuristics, both of which incur prohibitive inference costs and struggle to isolate the underlying memorization signal. To overcome this, we propose a generation-based black-box framework that infers context-induced information gain from targeted token discrepancies. We introduce a budget-constrained auditing regime, building on the empirical observation that the membership signal is highly sparse and concentrated in a few highly informative tokens. We formalize this sparsity, showing why uniform sampling needs many more samples than a policy that targets informative positions. Empirically, our approach achieves state-of-the-art black-box separation accuracy while needing about 3x fewer queries than uniform position sampling. Finally, we apply this methodology to closed frontier models, exploring its capacity and limits in surfacing potential training data overlaps on commercial APIs.

### [Retrieval-Augmented Generation in the Agentic Era: Tradition, Present, and Prospect](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30181)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.30181

> Directly addresses RAG frameworks, evaluation, and orchestration workflows.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) couples frozen language models with external, updatable knowledge, so that generation is grounded in retrieved evidence rather than in parametric memory alone. This work maps RAG in the agentic era by joining them on a single lifecycle axis, from offline indexing through pre-retrieval, retrieval, post-retrieval, and orchestration over these stages. ### [SHAKTI: Evidence-Grounded Conversational AI for Nutrition and Health](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29988)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.29988

> Directly relevant to RAG with a focus on grounded conversational AI and metrics for evaluation.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large language models (LLMs) answer many diet and health questions but can state unsupported claims and cite sources that do not support them. We present SHAKTI (Smart Health Advisor for Knowledge-based Tailored Interventions), a conversational assistant that answers only from retrieved scholarly papers and exact food-composition data, and evaluate it with retrieval, generation, grounding, efficiency and contextawareness metrics. We indexed 484,368,086 OpenAlex works, searched exactly on a GPU for evaluation (10,065 NFCorpus, BioASQ, SciFact and PubMedQA questions) and through a compressed 230-million-abstract index in deployment. On 1,000 stratified questions, we compared single-shot, multi-hop, corrective and deep-research retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) with Claude Sonnet. SHAKTI adds conversational query rewriting, USDA FoodData Central lookups, source-link checks and online verification of quotes and numbers. It was tested in 36 scripted conversational turns, a 248-question memory-depth test and a 38-question load test. Claims were checked by a Claude Opus judge against exactly the text the system saw. MedCPT reranking of hybrid retrieval gave the best MRR (0.21) and MAP@100 (0.14). Across existing RAG methods, faithfulness was 0.95-0.96, claim hallucination 0.5-0.8% and context precision up to 0.98; the deep-research agent answered 73.2% of BioASQ questions correctly. In conversation, SHAKTI reached a context-awareness score of 97.1%, faithfulness 95.5%, 1.9% unsupported claims, no contradicted claims and 229/229 working source links; it resolved references to the first question after 16 unrelated questions. An answer cost 14,716 tokens (US$0.06) with a median latency of 24 s. Large-scale retrieval with agentic evidence selection and mechanical verification yields auditable, low-hallucination nutrition answers.

### [Training-Free Interventions for Hallucination in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29909)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.29909

> This paper directly addresses hallucination intervention in LLMs, crucial for ensuring faithfulness in answer generation.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

A frozen language model can give a wrong answer because a fact is unavailable, a reliable passage is ignored, or an unsupported claim survives generation. We review 116 works on inference-time hallucination intervention and its foundations. We trace the development from retrieval and controllable generation to methods that act on input evidence, token scores, internal representations, completed claims, or the decision to answer. For each family we compare its source of evidence, model access, preparation cost, and the task on which a correction can be established. For activation steering, we distinguish estimating a subspace from applying a state-dependent edit and separate interventions on the prompt from those on the generated prefix. Published comparisons show why faithfulness to a source, factual correctness, informativeness, and answer coverage must be measured separately. Applications in time-sensitive question answering, document synthesis, and visual grounding identify when source authority, evidence availability, or capability preservation limits a reported gain.

</details>

### [Where Do Fact-Checking Errors Arise? Separating Retrieval, Search Control and Judgement in Evidence-Grounded Verification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29697)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.29697

> Addresses the separation of retrieval and judgement in fact-checking which is important for grounded Q&A and retrieval performance.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

An evidence-grounded fact-checker can fail because it does not find the evidence or because it does not use it; end-to-end accuracy does not tell the two apart. We present a protocol that follows the reference evidence from the store to the verdict and separates retrieval, search control, judgement and verdict validation. We assess 293 claims from FEVER, FEVER 2.0 and AVeriTeC with one open-weights 9B model under two evidence conditions: small stores of annotated evidence, where discovery is easy, and the full source collections. In the control condition a single hybrid retrieval pass delivers complete reference evidence for 226 of 228 evidence-bearing claims, and adaptive controllers and a tool agent spend two to nine times as many model calls without a consistent gain. Of 34 decisive claims labelled as lacking evidence, 33 have the complete evidence in the packet: in 15 the judge abstains, and in 18 validation rules overturn a decisive verdict. On AVeriTeC these rules lower single-pass accuracy by six points, to 65.0% (balanced accuracy 57.6%). Ten judges from eight model families also show abstentions and validation conversions on fixed packets. Against the full stores hybrid single-pass accuracy falls by 17 to 29 points. Wikipedia evidence recovery declines; AVeriTeC recovery changes from annotated answers to cited pages and is measured separately. No controller recovers the accuracy loss. The agent also shows no resolved gain on 714 further Wikipedia claims. Evidence recovery, judgement and validation should be measured separately before an error is attributed to search.

</details>

### [SuSi-RAG: A Hybrid Graph RAG for Equipment Maintenance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30588)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.30588

> Directly discusses a hybrid RAG architecture, aligns well with project goals.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

In the manufacturing domain, equipment maintenance tasks depend on expert knowledge. Their documentation is often stored in heterogeneous formats and sources. The enhancement of LLMs with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is promising for bridging the scattered knowledge, but traditional vector-based RAG shows weaknesses with two characteristics which are common in real maintenance queries: complex causal chains and cross-domain synthesis. To address this, we propose SuSi-RAG, a hybrid Graph RAG solution, that matches a user query and each maintenance record using subgraph similarity. This work shows an in-use system: SuSi-RAG has run on an active Bosch production line for more than eight months, handling 10,664 question-answer interactions and demonstrates upper bound answer accuracy of 96.25% (confirmed error rate 3.75%). In a proof-of-concept, SuSi-RAG improves top-1 retrieval accuracy from 61.5% to 96.2% and expert-rated answer quality from 44.9% to 84.1% against a vector-based baseline. We present an integrated operational feedback loop to update SuSi-RAG.

### [VistaTree: Question Answering over Long Multimodal Documents with Image-Derived Summary Trees](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29419)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.29419

> Directly addresses question answering over multimodal documents, crucial for grounded generation in RAG.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Answering questions over long multimodal documents requires locating relevant evidence across pages containing text, tables, and figures. When the document exceeds the model's context window, selective retrieval becomes necessary. We introduce VistaTree, a document question-answering agent that builds a summary tree directly from rendered page images. VistaTree segments documents into contiguous page spans, recursively groups them into a topic hierarchy, and refines sibling summaries to distinguish neighboring sections. At query time, the agent uses node summaries to select relevant nodes and reads the corresponding pages, using hierarchical navigation when the full outline exceeds the context budget. Experiments on MMLongBench-Doc and LongDocURL evaluate answer quality and retrieval cost using a locally hosted vision-language model, and quantify the performance gap between global and hierarchical access to the index.

### [Zarc: Exact Nearest Neighbors over a Billion Vectors in Seconds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29252)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.29252

> Offers enhancements in vector search, pivotal for indexing efficiency in RAG applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Exact k-nearest-neighbor vector search lies on the critical path of machine learning systems, from sparse attention in the latest large language models (LLMs) to long-context memories and training-data deduplication. Exhaustive GPU search scans the whole dataset for every query, while approximate indexes return inexact neighbors. Recent methods skip almost every vector with a cheap half-precision proof that it lies too far away, but only on one GPU that holds the whole dataset. Our insight is to keep only the short rows of these proofs in GPU memory and to fetch the few remaining full vectors from host memory. Our system, Zarc, answers Euclidean, cosine, inner-product, and filtered queries exactly under updates. On four H100 GPUs, it answers 10,000 exact queries over one billion vectors in 1.1 seconds instead of five minutes. On eleven open LLMs, it retrieves the exact top-10 attention keys 2.2x to 13.1x faster than exhaustive search. In sparse attention over one million tokens, it runs 3.1x faster than bf16 selection with FlashInfer, which misses the exact top-64 for most queries.

</details>

### [GERF: A Token-Efficient Multimodal Retrieval Framework for Schema-Guided Scientific Information Extraction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28295)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.28295

> Directly relevant to document ingestion with a focus on multimodal retrieval for scientific information extraction.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Scientific information extraction (IE) is a fundamental component of AI for Science, transforming experimental knowledge embedded in scientific literature into structured and traceable data for evidence synthesis, scientific database construction, and data-driven discovery. However, critical experimental details are often distributed across text, tables, figures, captions, and surrounding discussion, making them difficult to recover using conventional text-only extraction. Existing LLM-based and multimodal RAG pipelines typically rely on full-document context or coarse page-level retrieval, introducing substantial irrelevant evidence and high textual and visual token costs. We propose GERF, a Graph-based Evidence Retrieval Framework for token-efficient, schema-guided multimodal scientific IE. GERF represents each article as a heterogeneous evidence graph that connects schema fields, text chunks, figures, and figure-derived content through semantic and structural relations. Using schema fields as retrieval anchors, GERF combines direct semantic retrieval, Random Walk with Restart, and null-aware evidence expansion to construct compact, field-specific multimodal evidence packages. Visual interpretation is applied only to graph-selected figures, avoiding indiscriminate processing of the entire document. We evaluate GERF from two complementary perspectives: evidence retrieval quality and pipeline-level input-token consumption. On the expert-annotated SOFC-Exp corpus, GERF retrieves gold-supported evidence for 94.55% of the evaluated field instances, providing broad field-level evidence coverage for downstream extraction. In the token comparison, GERF reduces average input-token usage by 73.40% relative to full-context processing and uses fewer tokens than the evaluated page-level pipelines.

### [DKnown-Jev-2: Article-Level Answer-Evidence Classification and Efficient Inference with a Pre-trained Language Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31056)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.31056

> Focuses on evidence selection in question answering, closely related to retrieval and grounded information.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Evidence selection in normative knowledge question answering requires determining whether a candidate article contains content that directly supports an answer under the applicable conditions of the question. We present DKnown-Jev-2, an article-level answer evidence classifier designed for evidence integration across multiple paragraphs. The model jointly encodes question conditions, article metadata, and paragraph content, and uses a pointer-style classification head to predict Answer Evidence (AE) and Non-answer Evidence (NE) in a single forward pass. Ona manually annotated test set of 972 evaluation instances, DKnown-Jev-2 achieves 91.77% accuracy and 91.28% macro-F1, outperforming Jev by 2.16 and 2.37 percentage points, respectively, while reducing both evidence misses and non-evidence false positives. The test set contains 317 multi-paragraph instances, with a maximum body length of 9,322 characters. Shared article encoding and non-autoregressive classification support low-latency inference. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method for article-level evidence selection in normative knowledge question answering.

Trallie is an open-source framework that leverages large language models (LLMs) to perform information extraction (IE) from natural language sources with or without user guidance. Unlike conventional approaches that rely on extensive labeled datasets or domain-specific training, Trallie enables the conversion of unstructured data into structured formats with minimal or no representative examples. ### [MERIT: Experience-Guided Memory for Long-Horizon Persona-Specific Persuasive Dialogue](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29679)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.29679

> Addresses conversational memory and dynamic adaptation in user interactions, relevant for chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Effective long-horizon persuasion in recurring goal-oriented settings requires more than factual memory: it must track how a user's trust, negotiation behavior, and strategic receptiveness evolve across interactions. Existing persuasive dialogue systems are largely single-session, static, and unimodal, while cross-session memory systems, though temporally aware, retain only factual content and overlook persuasion-specific dynamics. We propose MERIT (Memory-augmented Experience-Rationale for Iterative Temporal persuasion), which pairs a graph-based memory of four persuasion-specific memory types with a per-persona experience rationale library that records which strategies succeed for each user and why. MERIT surpasses memory and experience-based baselines on every metric across two policy backbones, reaching persuasive-appeal scores of 4.10 and 4.74 (out of 5).

### [Adaptation or Poisoning? Security Risks of Conversational Test-Time Learning in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29532)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.29532

> Addresses security risks relevant to LLM safety in conversational applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Test-time learning (TTL) enables large language models to adapt to unlabeled inputs during deployment. The model updates a lightweight adapter by minimizing the next-token prediction loss on the input it receives. When these updates persist across conversation turns, every user query serves both as the request answered in the current turn and as adaptation data for later turns. By choosing the queries used for adaptation, an attacker can influence parameter updates and weaken safety alignment through ordinary interactions with a TTL chat service. To demonstrate this risk, we introduce Progressive Test-Time Adaptation Poisoning (P-TAP), a query-only multi-turn attack that jailbreaks the model by poisoning its session adapter through the service's own updates. A sequence of escalating adversarial queries shapes the adapter turn by turn; the final harmful request is then answered under the accumulated state without itself being used for adaptation. Across four open-weight instruction-tuned models, enabling updates raises attack success rates on average by 13.00 percentage points under greedy decoding and by 22.25 points under best-of-10 sampling, relative to matched sessions receiving the same queries without updates. Answering adversarial queries but adapting on unrelated text or on benign text about the same topic removes most of this increase. Among existing safeguards, screening incoming queries flags every attack stream before the final request, while a perplexity-based detector of unsafe adaptation flags none of the successful attacks.

</details>

### [Structure-Exploiting Hierarchical Retrieval for Long Narrative Documents: A Precision-Oriented Alternative to Learned Abstraction Trees](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29966)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.29966

> Works on hierarchical retrieval for long documents, highly relevant to RAG systems.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented generation over book-length documents must locate a small, coherent set of passages inside tens of thousands of tokens. Flat dense retrieval ignores document structure; RAPTOR instead builds a hierarchy by recursively clustering and summarizing with a language model. We study a complementary design that exploits the native structure of a document: a two-level retriever that (i) selects chapters with a hybrid BM25+dense match over chapter recaps, (ii) runs a chapter-filtered dense search, and (iii) expands top passages into contiguous, section-aware spans under a token budget, plus content-hash-gated delta ingestion to keep the index cheap to update. We evaluate on five public-domain books (113 chapter-localization queries) and on QuALITY and NovelQA (200K+ token novels). In-house, we find a seed-stable precision/ranking tradeoff: with section expansion off, hierarchical retrieval returns a higher fraction of chunks from the correct chapter (+3.3pp, Wilcoxon p=0.014); with expansion on, ranking improves (MRR, p=0.040). Accuracy differences are not significant. On QuALITY, hierarchical retrieval beats flat top-k (81.0% vs 70.4%, p=0.0019) and matches the full article (p=0.74). On NovelQA, hierarchical and flat retrieval are statistically tied (55.7% vs 56.8%, p=0.24); hierarchy helps aggregation questions while flat wins on localized facts. Against a faithful RAPTOR baseline we find a complementary tradeoff, not superiority. Delta ingestion reuses 94 to 99.7% of embeddings on incremental edits. We make no claim of semantic chapter-boundary inference and scope claims to English narrative prose.

</details>

### [Look Before You Leap: Re-Architecting Disk-Resident Vector Search with TriVS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29948)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.29948

> Focuses on optimizing vector search methods relevant to efficiency in RAG applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Disk-resident approximate nearest-neighbor search (ANNS) scales vector search beyond DRAM, yet state-of-the-art systems remain I/O-bound, even with fast SSDs and sophisticated I/O execution. To understand this bottleneck, we conduct an I/O-centric analysis that breaks each query down into an approach phase that locates a query-near region and a converge phase that resolves the final top-k. This analysis reveals complementary I/O tradeoffs: cluster-based search circumvents approach-phase I/O through in-memory routing but reads coarse-grained, full clusters during convergence, whereas graph-based search employs fine-grained, vector-level I/O throughout, enabling efficient convergence at the cost of inevitable I/O during the approach phase. To combine the complementary I/O benefits of the two granularities, we present TriVS, a new paradigm that re-architects disk-resident vector search as a trilogy: approach coarsely, distill progressively, and converge exactly. Its key innovations come in two parts. First, it decouples the approach and converge phases from a single search granularity, employing cluster-based search to avoid approach-phase I/O and leveraging vector-level I/O for efficient convergence. Second, to bridge the two granularities, it introduces a new in-memory distill phase between the original two, progressively reducing routed clusters to a vector-level shortlist using compact quantized vectors before disk access. Moreover, coarser clustering shrinks the centroid-routing index, allowing TriVS to reallocate existing memory to distillation rather than expand its memory budget. Our evaluation on five datasets spanning diverse vector types and scales shows that, at Recall@10=0.9, TriVS achieves on average 3.42X PipeANN's throughput, 51.2% lower latency, and 63.7% less disk I/O, while using less memory and storage than PipeANN.

</details>
