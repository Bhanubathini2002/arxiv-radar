### [Typhoon OCR 2: From Thai Document Transcription to Multilingual, Multi-Task OCR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27494)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.27494

> Focuses on OCR and document parsing, which is essential for document ingestion in RAG systems.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Typhoon OCR 1.5 focused on reading Thai documents. However, in real-world workflows, users often need to extract specific information or preserve the structure of an entire page for further processing. Typhoon OCR 2 handles both tasks in a single model, with prompt-driven extraction and structured full-page parsing. Full-page parsing covers Thai, English, and Chinese. We release compact 2B and 0.8B models adapted from open document parsers through supervised fine-tuning on curated Thai and public multilingual data, followed by Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with task rewards and a rule penalizing unfinished responses. Among evaluated open models, Typhoon OCR 2 2B scores highest on Thai MDPBench (80.7 versus 68.9 for Typhoon OCR 1.5) and on ThaiOCRBench (59.91 versus 40.45), which covers recognition, extraction, and document understanding. The 2B model remains close to its base on English olmOCR-bench (84.7 versus 84.4) and on OmniDocBench, which evaluates English and Chinese together (90.97 versus 91.03). On MDPBench, GRPO reduces the mean share of pages with repetition loops for the 2B model from 3.2% to 0.7%, with a smaller and statistically uncertain score gain for the 0.8B model. We release the model weights, Python package, and evaluation suite.

### [ERA: Embed, Route, Adapt for Cold-Start Cross-Model Latent Retrieval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26919)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.26919

> This paper proposes a method for cold-start cross-model retrieval, directly relevant to RAG architectures and document retrieval.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Production retrieval systems are tied to the embedding model that indexed their corpus. Moving queries to a different encoder normally requires re-embedding the whole corpus, and at cold start there are no query logs while every document embedded by the new encoder incurs an API or compute cost. We present ERA (Embed, Route, Adapt), a system for cold-start cross-model latent retrieval. ERA spends a small anchor budget on maximally informative documents, selected from the existing target index via Cosine Farthest Point Sampling, trains a lightweight adapter with a Manifold Alignment Loss that couples directional alignment with orthogonal regularization to prevent representation collapse, and pairs the adapter with a deterministic router that escalates the rare, hard queries that define perceived retrieval quality. Across five BEIR datasets, ERA matches or outperforms point-wise L2 and embedding-conversion baselines, with relative gains of up to 35% nDCG@10 on Arguana, and on clustered corpora its anchor selection also improves prior methods under a fixed anchor budget. With 1.2 ms of adapter and router overhead on top of a ~20 ms local encoder, ERA at a 20% nominal escalation budget reduces end-to-end query latency from roughly 160 ms to 38-86 ms while concentrating escalations on the queries where the projection fails most.

</details>

### [Search From the Start: Search-Native Pretraining for Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26809)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.26809

> Directly proposes a method for enhancing LMs with search capabilities, highly relevant to retrieval.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Modern language models (LMs) are increasingly deployed with access to search tools at inference. Yet pretraining remains predominantly focused on predicting static text, with search capabilities typically introduced during post-training. We propose Search-Native Pretraining as a practical route to teach search capability during pretraining from ordinary text corpora. Specifically, we augment pretraining corpus at scale with web-search queries and retrieve web response using a local index. Training on this augmented corpus teaches models to generate search calls and incorporate retrieved evidence during language modeling. Across Wikipedia and DCLM-Edu corpora and 360M to 3.3B parameter scales, search-pretrained models ("QueryLM") show improved performance on knowledge-intensive tasks and higher steerability toward the retrieved evidence. Crucially, their advantage persists after supervised fine-tuning of both search-pretrained and conventionally pretrained models, indicating that the tested post-training procedures do not fully recover the capabilities developed during pretraining. Throughout our experiments, we find that a 1.4B QueryLM consistently beats or matches the performance of a FLOP-matched 3.3B baseline model across all knowledge intensive tasks. These results establish search-pretraining as a practical way to develop foundational search capabilities during pretraining. We release the training artifacts, including model checkpoints and data.

</details>

### [When Visually Rich Documents Meet Multi-Vector Retrieval for Region-level Retrieval-Augmented Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27542)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CV` · 2611.27542

> Directly addresses region-level retrieval in Vision-RAG, crucial for document handling.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Visually rich documents (VRDs) convey knowledge through textual, visual, and layout information that text-centric Retrieval-Augmented Generation (Text-RAG) struggles to fully exploit. Recent Vision-RAG approaches enable retrieval and generation directly from document images, retaining this multimodal information. However, earlier Vision-RAG methods commonly adopt coarse retrieval representations and full-page visual contexts, which can obscure local evidence and introduce substantial redundancy and computational overhead. To address these challenges, we propose MV-VisRAG, a region-level Vision-RAG framework that uses semantically complete regions for both document representation and visual augmentation. Specifically, MV-VisRAG first constructs these regions through a ContentAdaptive Regionalization module. It then encodes them into local region vectors and a page-level global vector using a VLM-based retriever trained through multimodal reconstruction pretraining and retrieval fine-tuning. With this compact multi-vector representation, MV-VisRAG performs late-interaction retrieval and estimates region contributions from the resulting matching scores. These contributions guide the selection of relevant regions, which are then rearranged into a compact visual context for generation. Experiments across five benchmarks demonstrate retrieval and generation gains over corresponding baselines, while region-level augmentation substantially reduces visual tokens and computational cost. Our code will be made publicly available at https://github.com/xandery-geek/MV-VisRAG.

</details>

### [MemOnce: A Scalable Memory Architecture for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27973)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.27973

> Focuses on memory architectures for LLMs, pertinent to long-term memory in chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Deploying large language models (LLMs) in long-horizon and continual interactions requires maintaining reusable memory states for future inference, posing challenges for existing architectures. Standard Transformers accumulate layer-wise, token-level KV caches, incurring linear GPU memory growth and quadratic computational complexity as the context expands. In contrast, linear-time architectures compress history into fixed-size states, but struggle to retain fine-grained details. We introduce MemOnce, a foundation architecture that learns scalable memory states for language models. MemOnce is based on two core designs: (a) Memory encoder-decoder: it caches only a small subset of final-layer hidden states as compact memory representations and dynamically decodes them back into KV caches for attention layers; (b) Memory sparse attention: for each continual input, MemOnce adaptively retrieves relevant memory representations, with the sparse indices computed only once across layers. Coupling these two designs enables efficient on-the-fly memory loading and KV cache decoding, drastically reducing the GPU memory footprint and achieving linear-time complexity. We conduct extensive experiments to show that MemOnce outperforms Transformers and other architectures across varying model sizes, context lengths, and training paradigms (pretraining and post-training adaptation). In particular, adapting Qwen3.5-4B with MemOnce reduces memory state cache by 1313$\times$ and prefill latency by 6.9$\times$ at 1M context, and improves throughput by 3.5$\times$ and overall memory performance by +5.77%. Finally, MemOnce generalizes zero-shot to downstream representation tasks, achieving competitive results against specialized models.

</details>

### [LongCat Recall Sparse Attention: Co-Designing Key Organization and Retrieval for Long-Context Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27233)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.27233

> Substantially discusses retrieval methods relevant to RAG, focusing on efficient key organization.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-based sparse attention, exemplified by DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA), reduces attention computation by selecting a small subset of key-value entries. However, selecting these entries can still require searching the entire visible history. For long sequences, retrieval becomes a major bottleneck, limiting the efficiency gains from sparse attention. To address this problem, we propose LongCat Recall Sparse Attention (LRSA), which combines fine-grained representatives with paged retrieval in an incrementally maintained index. LRSA partitions the sequence into time buckets that can be indexed independently as new keys arrive, without regrouping existing history. Within each bucket, it organizes indexing keys into capacity-balanced directional groups and packs a fixed number of complete groups into each page, retaining a separate center for every group. These centers provide fine-grained representatives for page selection, after which refinement scores members of the selected pages together with sink, sliding-window, and visible unindexed candidates to select the final Top-$K$ logical positions. To execute this retrieval process efficiently, we store members in reordered pages, fuse construction stages according to group size, and share storage for overlapping fixed candidates across queries during chunked prefill. These choices support regular member access while reducing kernel launches during construction and repeated candidate transfers. Moreover, we integrate LRSA into SGLang to maintain the index during inference, overlapping asynchronous index construction for new buckets with subsequent computation and reusing compatible bucket indices and member pages after a prefix match in the original key-value pool. With a 1024K-token context, LRSA accelerates key selection by 4.70$\times$ over DSA and combined selection and attention by 35.60$\times$ over full attention.

</details>

### [Reasoning in Two Dimensions: Quantifying Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness and Efficiency](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27100)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.27100

> Addresses faithfulness in chain-of-thought reasoning, relevant to grounded and faithful generation in RAG.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning exposes intermediate steps, but a correct answer does not establish that an LLM relies on the dependence expressed by those steps or reasons efficiently. We operationalize CoT faithfulness and efficiency within a common framework. Specifically, we represent a CoT as a semantic reasoning graph and decompose the model's predictive dependence along two complementary axes: dependence explained by semantic support versus additional reasoning history, and dependence allocated to answer-reaching versus off-path reasoning. This decomposition yields global and step-level measures of unfaithfulness and inefficiency. Controlled interventions show that the proposed metrics respond to hidden dependence and induced off-path reasoning as intended. Across mathematical and multi-hop reasoning tasks, answer-level unfaithfulness is consistently associated with greater predictive uncertainty, whereas both unfaithfulness and inefficiency exhibit weak or inconsistent associations with answer correctness. We further derive theoretically grounded, graph-aware fine-tuning objectives that improve the targeted reasoning properties without directly optimizing the computationally expensive metrics or compromising answer accuracy. Results indicate that accuracy and brevity provide incomplete assessments of reasoning quality: reliable CoT evaluation should also consider whether the LLM's predictive dependence is faithfully represented and efficiently allocated.

</details>

### [Rethinking Full-Weight LoRA with Domain and Task Adapter Scaling for Question Answering over Portuguese Well Drilling Reports](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26833)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.26833

> Relevant to RAG through domain adaptation techniques for question answering.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Adapting large language models (LLMs) to specialized domains remains challenging, particularly for technical documents such as Portuguese well drilling reports, where annotated data are scarce and domain adaptation may compromise instruction-following capabilities. Although Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is widely used for parameter-efficient adaptation, its scaling after training remains insufficiently explored. This study investigates the impact of LoRA adapter scaling on question answering over Portuguese drilling reports using Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.1-8B. Domain-adaptive pretraining (DAPT) is performed either directly on instruction-tuned models or on their base counterparts, with subsequent adapter transfer, while task-specific adapters are trained on WellQuest, an open-source question-answering dataset derived from Portuguese drilling reports. The two adapters are also combined using complementary weights. Scaling factors ranging from 0.1 to 1.0 are evaluated across multiple training checkpoints using BERTScore. Results show that fractional scaling consistently improves DAPT performance over full-weight application, increasing F1 from 68.8\% to 71.9\% for Qwen2.5-7B and from 63.2\% to 68.5\% for Llama-3.1-8B. QA adapters perform best at high but fractional weights, while base-to-instruct transfer yields better results than direct DAPT on instruct-tuned models. Adapter merging provides a modest improvement for Llama-3.1-8B. Overall, these findings demonstrate that post-training adapter scaling can improve domain adaptation without additional data or training.

</details>

### [Show Me the Answer: Visual Grounding on Dashboards](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27355)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CV` · 2611.27355

> Relevant to document query grounding and visual evidence retrieval in dashboard contexts.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Natural language interaction has become an increasingly important paradigm for accessing complex visual information systems. In visual data analytics environments, such as dashboards, a key capability is to ground user queries to specific visual elements, enabling systems to identify the visual evidence relevant to the query. However, existing visual grounding research has primarily focused on natural images, while grounding in dashboards remains underexplored due to their complex multi-chart layouts and fine-grained visual elements. In this paper, we study the task of Visual Grounding on Dashboards (VGD): Given a dashboard screenshot and a natural language query, the goal is to localize the visual element corresponding to the query using a bounding box. We propose VGD-Net, a two-stage grounding framework that reflects how users typically locate information in dashboards, by first performing query-aware chart retrieval and then fine-grained in-chart grounding. To systematically evaluate VGD-Net's performance on this task, we construct VGD-Bench, a benchmark specifically designed for VGD, covering three complementary settings: chart retrieval, in-chart grounding, and overall VGD. Experimental results show that our two-stage VGD-Net outperforms strong multimodal baselines on the VGD task, achieving a mean IoU of 57.06 and a success rate of 66.82, while also delivering superior performance over existing methods in both stages of chart retrieval and in-chart grounding. These results highlight the value of stage decomposition for reliable and interpretable VGD, and its potential to support query-driven visual analysis.

</details>
