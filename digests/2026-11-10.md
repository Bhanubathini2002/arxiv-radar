### [MIRIX-2.0: Multi-Agent Memory System](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12068)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.12068

> Directly addresses long-term memory and efficiency in chat assistant contexts, relevant to conversation management.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Long-term memory lets LLM agents personalize, stay consistent, and reuse what worked before. Parametric memories need training, consolidation can lose details for good, and keeping every record leaves a growing store to search. We present MIRIX-2.0, a multi-agent memory system designed to be practical, accurate, and efficient at once. It is the most accurate of the compared systems on four of five QA benchmarks. It reaches 91.4% on LoCoMo and 72.6% on LongMemEval-S, where it leads the strongest baseline by 14.3 points, and with AutoDream disabled it gains 31.0 and 22.0 points over HyperMem on single-hop and multi-hop document QA. As procedural memory, it yields the highest mean success among the compared memory methods on ALFWorld and WebShop and reaches 49.2% on MetaClaw-Bench, against 44.0% for MetaClaw's skill library. Six typed memory agents keep complete records as the system of record, and a provenance hypergraph indexes episodic and semantic records with n-ary frames that cite their rows. Retrieval runs under explicit budgets, so the evidence a question inspects does not grow with the history. AutoDream merges duplicate anchors outside the write path without rewriting any record, and it distills task sessions into skills. With AutoDream disabled, MIRIX-2.0 answers faster than HyperMem on all five timed benchmarks. It needs no fine-tuning, runs with closed and open models, and its code is public.

</details>

### [Search-by-Answer Bias in Retrieval Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11396)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.11396

> Directly addresses search-by-answer bias in retrieval models, important for RAG quality.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Dense retrievers are meant to select passages that match a query's semantic specification. We show they suffer from search-by-answer bias: they favor passages that mention answer objects the model knows (scores as likely), over passages that support valid alternative answers. We test this with controlled factual contrasts from LAMA/TREx. For each retriever, we measure how strongly the model scores the canonical object over an alternative object using a token-prediction head, and ask whether this score predicts passage rankings. Across eight retrievers, including DPR, Spider variants, Contriever, E5, and Qwen3-Embedding, larger token-prediction scores consistently predict higher retrieval scores for passages containing the corresponding object. This holds when passages differ only in the answer object, and even when the passage that mentions the high-scoring object expresses a wrong relation. Dense retrieval thus exhibits search-by-answer bias during passage selection, shaping which evidence is retrieved.

</details>

### [Calibrating Document-Local Boundaries for Contextual Chunk Retrieval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12097)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.12097

> Addresses contextual chunk retrieval, vital for implementing efficient document chunking in a RAG system.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Late Chunking encodes an entire document before pooling at chunk boundaries, preserving document context in local retrieval units. Chunk-level post-training improves context-dependent retrieval, but shared context can make semantically similar same-document negatives difficult to distinguish. We study the local decision boundary between a gold chunk and its hardest sibling. Starting from InSeNT, we analyze InSeNT's within-document gradients and show that the entropy of the conditional negative allocation decreases monotonically with the scaling factor at fixed logits. We propose a two-part framework for calibrating document-local chunk boundaries. The first component adapts the discriminative scale from within-document margins, while the second exposes chunk partitions to attention during document encoding. Experiments on ConTEB and NanoBEIR show that the resulting model improves contextual chunk retrieval across the evaluated encoder settings while retaining competitive passage-level retrieval quality; ablations further characterize the distinct roles of adaptive scaling and chunk-aware routing.

</details>

### [PCG-RAG: Progressive Compositional Grounding for Retrieval-Augmented Generation in Complex Web Question Answering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11113)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.11113

> The paper directly proposes a RAG architecture addressing complex question answering, making it highly relevant.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Complex Web question answering requires retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems to identify target entities from multi-clue descriptions and connect evidence scattered across long Web pages. Individual pages often support only some of the required semantic constraints, while clues discovered on one page guide subsequent searches on others. However, premature reliance on partially supported entity hypotheses can divert retrieval from the original information need. RAG systems must therefore use partial evidence to guide further searches while seeking the evidence needed to establish the intended entities. We propose PCG-RAG, a novel RAG framework based on Progressive Compositional Grounding (PCG). Progressive Evidence Acquisition directs subsequent searches toward missing evidence for candidate attributes and relations. Compositional Reasoning separates candidate-guided retrieval from evidence-based answering along a directed acyclic graph (DAG) of subquestions. Provisional names make dependent searches specific, while answering tasks use accepted results and unresolved identifying conditions, assessed against the original question. New passages connect the identifying clues to the requested relations, supporting evidence-based downstream answers. Evidence Grounding combines identifying and relational source passages with supported intermediate answers, connecting evidence across Web pages to the original conditions during final generation. On BrowseComp-Plus and WebWalkerQA, PCG-RAG improves accuracy over the leading compared baselines by 5.43 and 5.15 percentage points, respectively.

</details>

### [Lightfoot: Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Exact, Model-Free Document Deletion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11010)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.11010

> Addresses a RAG framework for multi-hop QA, making it crucial for users interested in retrieval mechanisms.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Graph-based retrieval-augmented generation (GraphRAG) improves multi-hop question answering, but deployed systems face two costs that research comparisons rarely measure: building the graph, and removing documents from it. We present Lightfoot, a GraphRAG system in which every entity, link, fact and synonym edge is a deterministic function of per-passage extractions carrying chunk-level provenance. Indexing makes one language-model call per passage, retrieval makes none, and deleting a document is a database operation with no model calls. On the full 11,402-passage MuSiQue corpus we verify that, after deleting 10% of the documents, the graph is identical to a fresh index of the surviving documents. We compare Lightfoot with HippoRAG 2, LightRAG and fast-graphrag under identical extraction, answer and embedding models on MuSiQue, 2WikiMultiHopQA and HotpotQA. Lightfoot builds the cheapest index on all three datasets, 26-31% below HippoRAG 2 and 3-6x below LightRAG and fast-graphrag. Against HippoRAG 2 it is significantly better on MuSiQue retrieval (+2.8 points recall@5 and +4.3 recall@10 on a held-out split) and on HotpotQA answers (+7.7 points exact match), and significantly worse on 2WikiMultiHopQA (-3.9 recall@2, -8.4 F1). Deleting the same documents took LightRAG 2,187 model calls and 3.7 hours; fast-graphrag has no deletion path. Code and per-question results: https://github.com/udayallu-ml/Lightfoot-GraphRAG

</details>

### [Yuanbao-Reranker: Document-Grounded Synthesis and Preference Distillation for Text Reranking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10405)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.10405

> Directly addresses text reranking for RAG systems, making it highly relevant.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

High-precision text reranking is a critical component in modern search, recommendation, and agentic retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems. We present Yuanbao-Reranker, a family of general-purpose dense rerankers spanning 0.6B, 4B, and 8B parameter scales built upon the Qwen3 backbone. To construct diverse and reliable ranking supervision at scale, we pair answer-first and query-type-guided document-grounded synthesis with a three-stage curation funnel that verifies positive support, eliminates semantic redundancy, and enforces balanced source-by-length quotas. For each curated pair, we screen real retrieval pools with a teacher score margin to filter out potential false negatives and sample Hard, Medium, and Moderate negative tiers, yielding 2.75M $1{:}3$ ranking groups. We train the models by jointly optimizing pointwise relevance cross-entropy and a bounded minimum-gap hinge objective over both positive--negative and negative--negative pairs. This formulation transfers fine-grained teacher preferences across candidate tiers without over-constraining well-separated predictions, supporting both cross-scale and same-scale distillation followed by layerwise adaptive model merging. Across five comprehensive benchmarks spanning Chinese (CMTEB-R), multilingual (MMTEB-R), English (BEIR), long-document (MLDR), and code (MTEB-Code) retrieval, Yuanbao-Reranker consistently leads every parameter tier: the 0.6B variant reaches 70.96 Avg. to surpass 1B--4B active-parameter baselines, the 4B variant reaches 73.63 Avg. to outperform the 8B teacher (72.68), and the 8B variant achieves a state-of-the-art 74.21 Avg., with substantial gains generalizing across distinct first-stage retrievers.

</details>

### [BharatSynthQA: A Context-Grounded Question Answering Dataset on Indian Temples and Religious Heritage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11952)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.11952

> This dataset is valuable for grounded QA, directly relevant to testing RAG systems with culturally-specific content.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

India's temples and other places of worship are among its richest repositories of cultural heritage, bringing together religious tradition, history, architecture, and art. Due to the widespread curiosity, people increasingly turn to Artificial Intelligence (AI) assistants for information about them. Current models are rarely trained or evaluated on this domain. We present BharatSynthQA, a passage grounded question answering (QA) dataset focused on Indian temples, which also covers other religious sites connected to them. Our pipeline crawls Wikipedia from topics seeded by authentic heritage sources, filters articles for topical relevance, and locations in India. Further it generates Boolean, factoid, and explanatory QA pairs with a fewshot prompted LLM. An LLM judge filters the pairs on answer correctness, retaining 98,677 of 131,494 generated pairs. Additionally, 300 human-annotated pairs are released separately as a reference set. Human evaluation of the subsample of this dataset showed 89.83% of the questions were formed correctly. Benchmarking five current LLMs shows that factoid token F1 is only 0.18-0.30 without the source passage but 0.73-0.77 with it, indicating that this domain is poorly captured in model knowledge. The dataset is available at https://huggingface.co/datasets/theshivam7/bharatsynth-qa to support research on culturally grounded QA and retrieval-augmented generation for Indian heritage.

</details>

### [Explaining and interrupting collective misalignment in language model systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11724)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.11724

> Addresses collective misalignment and modeling safeguards, relevant for guardrails and safety in LLM applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

In collectives, language model agents can exchange individually innocuous messages that together support a policy violation. Understanding and preventing collective misalignment requires answering the question of which messages sufficiently influenced and necessarily enabled the violation. Collective Dependence Analysis (CDA) operationalises necessity and sufficiency to explain decisions and find the fewest removals interrupting support within tested combinations. Tests covered constructed violations, unscripted agent conversations, and recorded interactions, with selected message removals reducing violations from 93% to 6% in controlled cases. Using O(N) model evaluations for N messages, CDA achieves the same violation reduction with half the deletions of a frontier LLM judge given the transcript and asked for all supporting messages in eligible operational cases. CDA also proposes the mechanism through which the collective misalignment emerged. Nonetheless, complete explanations remain difficult on unfamiliar tasks and some removals can inadvertently disrupt correct decisions. CDA unifies explanations of collective misalignment with experimentally tested interventions.

</details>

### [Safeguarding Agentic AI in Radiology Workflows: A Multimodel Evaluation of Rule-based and Large Language Model-based Verification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11541)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.11541

> Substantially about guardrails and safety mechanisms in LLM applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Purpose: To estimate how often tool-using large language model (LLM) agents record clinically relevant false or unsupported information in simulated radiology documentation and how much safeguards applied before each action reduce these errors. Materials and Methods: In this simulation study (October 2026), 48 synthetic, radiologist-reviewed base scenarios covered finding communication, tumor board preparation and follow-up management. Forty-three also had a misleading-information variant (91 variants). Six LLM agents (Claude Sonnet 5.5, GPT-6.1-Sol, GLM-5.3, MiniMax M3, Gemma-4-31B, Qwen3.8-27B) completed each variant with permissions only, deterministic rules, and rules plus a locally run LLM checker (1638 episodes). The primary outcome was at least one executed high-severity violation per episode. Risk differences were estimated per agent at base-scenario level and, exploratorily, pooled across agents, with analyses of document access and blocks. Results: With permissions only, 7.3%-27.1% of episodes had a high-severity violation (pooled across agents, 19.4%). Pooled, it was 16.7% with rules (-2.8 percentage points; 95% CI: -6.1, 0.5) and 13.2% with rules plus the LLM checker (-6.2; 95% CI: -9.5, -3.0). Adding the LLM checker to rules changed it by -3.5 (95% CI: -6.3, -0.7). No difference between agents was detected (P = .43), and task success did not decrease detectably. In misleading variants, agents opened the altered document in 83% of episodes regardless of error, and 58% of erroneous statements cited it. Most blocked actions (64%-68%) would not have caused a violation and were returned for revision. Conclusion: Layered safeguards reduced but did not prevent high-severity errors. Errors after misleading information reflected misjudged rather than missed information.

</details>

### [EASE-KV: Diagnosing the Gap Between Memory Availability and Effective Use](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11299)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.11299

> Relevant to conversational memory and leveraging past interactions in chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Reusable key-value (KV) memory avoids repeatedly prefilling retrieved text, but stored and injected memories do not necessarily become usable evidence. We present EASE-KV, a controlled framework for understanding this gap under fixed candidates and a frozen language model. EASE-KV separates three interfaces: whether required relations are preserved during encoding, whether preserved memories receive appropriate influence during allocation, and whether outcome supervision can identify useful allocations. Matched experiments show that causal encoding improves compositional accuracy by 25.0 percentage points over block-diagonal encoding, while the tested inference-time bridging methods fail to recover the lost contextual information. With unchanged stored states, diagnostic evidence-guided allocation improves LongMemEval accuracy from 31.2% to 38.4%, demonstrating that memory influence can be controlled without re-encoding. However, controllability does not guarantee learnability: binary correctness provides informative reward contrast for 53.0% of sampled query groups, compared with 73.6% under the evaluated shaped-reward setting. These findings show that effective KV memory requires more than retrieval and injection: the required relations must be encoded, preserved memories must receive appropriate influence, and supervision must distinguish better allocations from worse ones.

</details>
