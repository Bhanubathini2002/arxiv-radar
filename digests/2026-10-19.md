### [Enhancing RAG-based Medical VQA with Gated Multimodal Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23588)
**Score 10/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.23588

> Directly explores a multimodal RAG framework for medical VQA, highly relevant to RAG principles.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Medical Vision-Language Models (Med-LVLMs) have shown strong performance in Medical Visual Question Answering (MVQA), yet they may still produce factually inconsistent responses. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) can mitigate this issue by incorporating external clinical evidence; however, retrieval is often guided by a single modality, potentially overlooking the joint semantics of medical images and clinical questions. We introduce a multimodal RAG framework that learns a joint query representation through a Gated Multimodal Unit, enabling evidence retrieval to leverage information from both modalities while keeping the underlying Med-LVLM frozen. We evaluate the proposed approach on benchmark datasets. Results show that the multimodal query representation consistently outperforms a reference Med-LVLM and remains competitive with state-of-the-art methods. Our findings demonstrate the potential of multimodal query representations for more effective evidence retrieval in Medical VQA.

### [SIT-Eval: Situation-Based Interactive Turn-by-Turn Evaluation of Conversational Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23612)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.23612

> Focuses on evaluating conversational agents, relevant to context management and RAG quality.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The rapid proliferation of conversational AI agents necessitates evaluation methods that assess performance across diverse dialogue scenarios, utilizing multiple situational criteria, as appropriate behavior depends on context and encompasses various aspects. Existing dialogue benchmarks typically use static natural conversations, which underrepresent rare situations---a critical safety gap, as low-probability events become frequent failures at production scale. However, limited sample sizes force a choice: either maintain natural dialogues for realism or turn to adaptive situation sampling for comprehensive yet statistically reliable coverage. We introduce SIT-Eval, an adjustable framework for evaluating situation-based conversational agents. We formalize an agent's desired behavior as situational rules: if situation is P, follow rules Q. At each conversation turn, an LLM-interlocutor enacts a uniformly sampled situation. A context-aware LLM-judge leverages all conditionally applicable rules as criteria to evaluate the agent's reply for that situation. The judge is validated using human-verified synthetic golden dialogues. Benchmarking 8 LLMs on 1292 conversation turns reveals failure modes hidden by aggregate metrics: models with similar overall scores vary widely in their adherence to individual rules. All models struggle most with ambiguity clarification and Socratic questioning.

</details>

### [MuninAI: A Self-Hosted Agentic Framework for Independent Research Groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23480)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.23480

> Directly related to building self-hosted models grounded in user documents, critical for the RAG chat application.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Research groups that want a language model grounded in their own literature face a choice between hosted services that receive their unpublished work and self-hosted deployments that keep the data local but give the model no access to the corpus. Munin is an open-source framework for a self-hosted scientific assistant that indexes a group's corpus and its citation graph, and wraps whichever open-weight model the group can serve in an agent harness with specialised reading, search, and computation tools. We claim that the harness, rather than the specific model, is what makes the system useful on domain literature, and we ship the evaluation suite that tests this claim with the software. On 199 paired literature questions, the harness more than doubles the accuracy of the base model, from 0.387 to 0.874 (+0.487, 95% CI [0.407, 0.568]), while naive retrieve-then-answer scores below the unaided model on every backbone tested. Under the harness, roughly twice as many answer claims trace to a passage the model read itself as with fixed top-5 retrieval. When the source paper for a question is removed from the corpus, the system abstains on about nine in ten of the questions it had answered correctly on the Qwen backbones and eight in ten on gpt-oss-20b, and it produces no fabricated local citation across 300 fabricated references. The pattern holds on three backbones from two developers, including a 20-billion-parameter model that runs on a single consumer-grade GPU. With all outbound network access disabled, both Qwen backbones still exceed the published PaperQA2 accuracy. The evaluation suite runs as a single command, so an adopting group can re-run it on its own corpus and model.

</details>

### [What LLMs Do in the Shadows: Reading Context Reliance from the Unembedding Dark Space](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23182)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.23182

> The paper focuses on detecting hallucinations in LLMs, crucial for ensuring faithfulness in chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Detecting faithfulness hallucinations in large language models (LLMs) is crucial for their safe deployment in high-stakes domains. Recent approaches address this by contrasting contextual against parametric knowledge utilization, yet measuring context reliance typically demands noticeable overhead, such as materializing attention maps, running additional forward passes, or training auxiliary probes. In this work, we show that context reliance can be estimated by projecting hidden states onto the dark space - directions that compose weakly with the unembedding matrix. Beyond its recognized function as an initial-token attention sink, we show that this subspace actively mediates context processing, and that its share of hidden-state norm - termed the Dark Matter Ratio (DMR) - provides a direct score of context utilization. Pairing DMR with a parametric knowledge score yields DarkLens, a lightweight faithfulness detector operating strictly within a single forward pass. Evaluated across three QA and summarization benchmarks on six open-weight LLMs (4B-13B parameters), DarkLens matches or outperforms a wide range of baselines without requiring extra forward passes or probe training.

</details>

### [SAFE-Mem: Secure Agentic Memory with Fine-Grained Authority and Governance-Aware Retrieval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22959)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.22959

> Directly addresses memory governance and retrieval concerns relevant to long-term memory in chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large language model (LLM) agents increasingly rely on persistent memory to support long-term reasoning and personalized interactions. However, information collected from external sources can introduce security risks when untrusted claims (i.e., atomic statements) are later treated as authorized preferences, policies, or permissions. Existing defenses typically rely on content-based filtering or coarse-grained provenance tracking, which either fail to prevent unauthorized memory use or block legitimate information, sacrificing memory utility. To address these limitations, we propose SAFE-Mem, a fine-grained memory governance framework that improves security while preserving legitimate memory use. First, SAFE-Mem annotates each claim with three governance labels (i.e., kind, authority, and scope) to capture its provenance and permitted use. Second, governance-aware retrieval matches query requirements with claim permissions to block unauthorized influence while retaining legitimate information. Third, a governance-enabled memory graph connects source sentences, claims, and entities to support provenance tracking and efficient retrieval. Across four LLM backbones on MPBench and LoCoMo, SAFE-Mem attains the lowest attack success among defenses that retain legitimate memory, at least 77% below the strongest of them, and the highest answer accuracy of all evaluated defenses, improving it by up to 15%. The code is available at https://github.com/LVenum/SAFE-Mem.

</details>

### [Bengali Hallucination Detection with Retrieval and Programs: A System Study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22892)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.22892

> Directly relevant to hallucination detection in grounded QA, which is a critical aspect of RAG applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Bengali hallucination detection must distinguish unsupported factual claims from incorrect reasoning, often without a supplied reference passage. We present and characterize an inference-only system that combines response-conditioned query rewriting, hybrid retrieval, and task-specific language-model judging. Six retrieval routes search question-answer and passage collections; a router directs each example to a 27B textual judge or a 14B agent that can compare retrieved answers, reason directly, or generate and execute Python. We characterize the system using version-specific code, saved predictions, execution traces, and corpus metadata from the Olikbochon challenge. Kaggle associates a displayed private macro-F1 of 0.956 with Version 2; its saved run processes 2,516 examples in 220.99 minutes on two T4 GPUs. Of 253 math-routed examples, 88 terminate through candidate matching, while execution traces expose 49 stderr-bearing run events across 35 examples. A later run changes six predictions despite identical core worker code. A bounded audit of 1,090 benchmark-related corpus records finds exact question or context overlap for 38 processed evaluation inputs, precluding an unqualified claim of corpus-independent generalization. The contribution is a concrete retrieval-and-computation judging architecture with an auditable operational characterization, not a controlled estimate of component gains. We distinguish the reported leaderboard result from independently recomputed diagnostics and identify the evidence needed for stronger effectiveness and reproducibility claims.

### [Veil-Then-Reveal: Protecting PII Privacy via Reversible Pseudo-Anonymization in Zero-Trust User--LLM Interaction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22800)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.22800

> Addresses privacy concerns in LLM interaction, crucial for guardrails in LLM apps.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large language models (LLMs) increasingly process sensitive documents and personal information, yet sending prompts containing personally identifiable information (PII) to remote providers exposes data subjects and organizations to risks from logging, retention, model training, and unintended disclosure. Existing anonymization methods often mask or remove PII, while conventional encryption eliminates the lexical and structural cues needed for effective LLM processing. We present Veil-then-Reveal (VtR), a user-side PII pseudo-anonymization framework for privacy-preserving LLM interaction under a zero-trust threat model. VtR treats remote LLM providers as untrusted and sends only pseudo-anonymized prompts, keeping plaintext PII on the user. VtR first detects PII and replaces it with realistic, same-type, in-vocabulary surrogates before prompts leave the user's device, preserving their linguistic and structural properties. It combines format-preserving encryption (FPE) for structured PII, such as phone and credit-card numbers, with a keyed-hash dictionary and secure vault for semantic PII, such as names, locations, and organizations. Session-specific mappings provide cross-session unlinkability, while user-side restoration reversibly replaces surrogates with their original values in LLM responses. We further introduce a rank-class FPE strategy for dates that preserves format and ambiguity while reducing cipher evaluations. Evaluation on three datasets containing 11,322 annotated PII instances achieves 97.9% detection recall, 95.2% protection of detected values, and 99.9% round trip; 100% of echoed surrogates end-to-end round-trip restoration.

</details>

### [NoveltyScope: A Multi-Agent Research Assistant for Evidence-Grounded Novelty Assessment of Scientific Papers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22703)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2610.22703

> NoveltyScope's evidence-grounded assessment has direct implications for RAG applications and evaluation methodologies.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

NoveltyScope is a prototype that compares a scientific paper with retrieved prior work and produces a structured report and a five-level novelty score. The system extracts the core task and at most two author-claimed contributions, then searches a closed local corpus using BM25 and dense retrieval with reciprocal rank fusion. Contribution comparisons request paired passages for potential refutations, and quotations are checked against cached source text. Four scoring roles (Prosecutor, Defender, Judge, and Critic) use the resulting evidence, with at most one revision of the Judge's score. Evaluation uses 50 class-balanced RINoBench samples whose idea-level labels are retained for full-paper input. Mean absolute error is 1.48 and 1.26 for single-LLM and multi-agent scoring with Gemini, respectively, and 1.22 and 1.16 with Qwen3. The vector-similarity baseline has an error of 1.68. A HiCLIP case study illustrates the contribution comparisons and paired evidence passages. Overall, the results demonstrate the feasibility of evidence-grounded novelty assessment and show that the multi-agent configurations achieve lower scoring errors than their single-LLM counterparts across both evaluated models.

</details>

### [MARS: Memory-Aware Routing for Session-Level LLM Assistants](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23583)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.23583

> Explores memory-aware routing for LLMs, relevant to conversational memory and context management.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Long-term LLM assistants must repeatedly decide what prior context to expose and which model should answer as an interaction evolves. Existing memory systems and model routers usually make these decisions separately, although memory construction changes the evidence, distractors, and difficulty of the resulting input. We introduce MARS, a memory-aware routing framework that uses session state as a shared control interface between memory construction and model selection. A Query and Topic Manager (QTM) estimates compact decision-relevant state that guides a fixed state-conditioned memory policy and an online router, enabling each turn to receive an appropriate memory view and a suitable model under a quality-memory-cost trade-off. We evaluate MARS on RealMem and MultiChallenge with 16 system configurations each, covering long-history memory-driven interaction and bounded sessions with structured context interference. On RealMem, MARS preserves 98.2% of full-context GPT-4o's QA while using only 14.5% of its generation API cost, including both generation and controller calls for MARS, and achieves the strongest measured memory-recall and retrieval scores among compared systems. On MultiChallenge, MARS trails the strongest static system overall but approaches its performance at lower recorded cost on a mechanism-focused subset. Together, these results support a favorable quality-memory-cost trade-off for long-history, memory-dependent interaction.

### [Self-Reflective Chain-of-Thought (SRCoT): Evidence-Based Text Generation via a Closed-Loop Retrieve--Reflect--Revise Pipeline on Edge LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23311)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.23311

> Introduces a RAG-like pipeline with elements of retrieval and revision, relevant to grounded generation.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large language models (LLMs) can generate fluent outputs that lack source support or conflict with local operational constraints. We introduce Self-Reflective Chain-of-Thought (SRCoT), a modular architecture that organizes generation as a closed Retrieve--Reflect--Revise loop. SRCoT separates environment state, persistent knowledge, accumulated experience, and short-term memory into provenance-labeled stores. BM25 retrieval supplies local context; subsequent passes expose intermediate drafts, missing-information markers, follow-up queries, and optional claim--evidence mappings. These artifacts inform retrieval, revision, clarification, or abstention decisions. We examine the architecture through qualitative, trace-based case studies in two fully synthetic settings: emergency medical services and Munich-Sim, an emergency-operations simulation. Local prototype runs on an Apple M4-family laptop with 128 GB memory illustrate source-labeled documentation, explicit gap marking, and structured dispatch-style outputs. The traces also reveal provenance-assignment errors, incomplete evidence coverage, and unsafe assumptions that remain possible despite prompting constraints. These cases demonstrate report-level inspectability and local execution, not comparative superiority, hallucination reduction, clinical validity, or operational safety. The architecture separates retrieval and control from the model interface, but behavior across different models remains to be evaluated. SRCoT is a research proof of concept requiring human review, with its principal contribution lying in an inspectable control procedure rather than a new model.

### [Ignored, Not Refused: What Low Prompt-Injection Success Rates Hide in Small Tool-Using Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23254)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.23254

> Addresses prompt injection issues which are critical for safety in LLM applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Tool-using agents read untrusted external content, which exposes them to indirect prompt injection (IPI), where hidden instructions hijack the agent's tool calls. Benchmarks measure IPI security by the attack success rate (ASR), which treats every failed attack as a successful defense. To test this assumption, we audit 33,000 trajectories from eleven open-weight agents (2-14B parameters) on InjecAgent and AgentDojo. A label-blind LLM judge shows that most failed attacks are ignored (76-80%) rather than refused (17-18%). With identical model weights, changing only the system prompt shifts InjecAgent ASR from 0.2% to 43%. Our Attack-Task Outcome Matrix (ATOM) further shows that in 21.7% of AgentDojo episodes, the agent stayed safe but did not complete the user's task. A low ASR therefore mostly reflects agents silently ignoring injections, not refusing them. Evaluations should report how attacks fail and whether the user's task is completed, alongside ASR. We release the labeled corpus, judge prompt, and analysis code.

</details>

### [Can AI interview? Comparing AI-led to Human-led Chat Interviews](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23149)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.23149

> Explores the use of AI for interviewing which intersects with conversational memory and context management.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large language model chatbots have been proposed as AI-interviewers for collecting qualitative interview data at scale, potentially bridging social science's longstanding divide between the breadth of surveys and the depth of qualitative interviews. This paper provides the first direct empirical comparison of data quality in AI-led and human-led interviews. In a randomized experiment (N = 41), university students were interviewed via synchronous text chat by either trained non-expert interviewers or a locally hosted AI system; we evaluate exposure, specificity, and relevance. Median interview duration was similar across conditions, but AI-interviewers asked more than twice as many questions per interview and elicited roughly 65 percent more respondent text per interview. Per-question responses were longer with human interviewers, though not after multiple-testing correction; only AI-condition participants skipped questions. Responses were almost uniformly on-topic in both conditions; for specificity we detect no difference, but the design cannot exclude one of up to three quarters of a point. These results were obtained with a small, locally hosted open-weight model (7B parameters), which addresses concerns about data security, GDPR compliance, and scientific reproducibility. Our findings support the viability of AI-interviewing for large-N qualitative data collection.

### [ECHO: Replacing the Compliance Proxy with Response Harmfulness in Optimization-Based Jailbreak Attack](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23030)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.23030

> Directly engages with jailbreak attacks in LLMs, relevant to guardrails and safety in retrieval applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Despite extensive safety alignment, large language models remain vulnerable to jailbreak attacks. Existing optimization-based attacks share a single objective: maximize a token-level proxy of victim compliance---such as the likelihood of an affirmative prefix---computed on the prompt rather than the decoded reply. This proxy cannot distinguish a cautious hedge from a fully actionable harmful answer, leaving the search blind to what the model actually says. We propose ECHO (Evaluation-Conditioned Harmfulness Optimization), which replaces the compliance proxy with response-level, continuous harmfulness scores from HarmSig, a 1.7B regressor grounded in actual harmful capability. ECHO couples HarmSig with five word-level perturbation operators and Keyword-Anchored Beam Search with global top-m memory, under a strict black-box threat model. Across five open-weight victims on AdvBench, ECHO achieves up to 0.898 StrongREJECT mean score and 89.23% HarmBench success. Our code is publicly available at https://github.com/STANWYW/echo.

### [Identify, Classify, Act: Per-Instruction Controlled Compliance for LLMs over External Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22874)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.22874

> Discusses prompt injection defenses relevant for safety in LLM applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

LLMs over external data face a per-instruction tension: a single document or tool return often interleaves legitimate directives with adversarial prompt injections, and the model must follow the former while rejecting the latter. Existing defenses suppress or trust all external instructions, collapsing the safety-utility frontier and forcing a choice between over-refusal and over-compliance. We argue the missing capability is \textbf{per-instruction credit attribution} and formalize it as External-Data Instruction Classification, an identify-classify-act decision over each embedded directive. We propose \textbf{EDIC}, a decomposed reward framework for per-instruction compliance. A scene-grounded pipeline injects mixed beneficial and harmful instructions with counterfactual filtering, while a generative judge is initialized by SFT and refined with GRPO to attribute compliance at the instruction level. The judge then produces a reward that separately encourages beneficial compliance and penalizes harmful compliance for RL training. Across two backbones and five benchmarks, EDIC improves both safety and utility over the strongest prior defense, lifting harmful-rejection by up to 7.7\% and Arena-Hard-Auto utility by 12.5\% on Qwen3-4B. The code repository is hosted at https://github.com/yingyingxia666/EDIC.

### [Prune the Leaky Neurons: Mitigating Private Data Leakage in Fine-Tuned LLMs via Joint-Embedding-Guided Pruning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22792)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.22792

> Presents a pruning framework to mitigate privacy leakage in LLMs, highly relevant to safety.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) on proprietary records enables domain adaptation but introduces two challenges: (i) privacy leakage of memorized private data, such as personally identifiable information (PII), and (ii) balancing private data removal with utility preservation. Existing defenses, including data sanitization and differential privacy, often degrade performance, while privacy-neuron editing lacks a PII-free reference to distinguish identity-specific neurons from those supporting useful text. We propose JEPA-Prune, a post-hoc neuron pruning framework guided by a model trained exclusively on PII-free views. Alongside the fine-tuned private model, JEPA-Prune, trains a public model on surrogate and masked views of the same records using a joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA), capturing domain knowledge without identifiers. A cross-model representation probe quantifies deviations between private representations of leaked records and this PII-free reference. Integrated gradients and a scale-invariant specificity score then identify PII-responsive neurons, which are pruned without retraining. Experiments show that pruning just 1.6% of neurons reduces PII inference accuracy by up to 7.4x and PII extraction recall by up to 13.9x, while keeping unseen-text perplexity within 16% of that of the unpruned model for most LLMs. In contrast, privacy-neuron editing increases perplexity by at least 26x.

> Discusses multi-stage retrieval and reranking, closely tied to RAG evaluation metrics.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

This paper presents the overview of the runs related to the Ad-hoc Video Search (AVS) and Video Question Answering (VQA) tracks of TRECVID 2025 on behalf of the CERTH-ITI team. For the AVS track, we introduce a two-stage framework built on foundation models. In the first stage, multiple vision-language models (VLMs) encode both the input query, augmented through LLM-generated rephrasings, and the candidate video shots, producing weighted similarity scores for initial retrieval. For the Multiple-Choice (MC) task, instead of voting, we use mean pooling on the logits assigned by the fine-tuned model to each candidate response. Through the combination of fine-tuning and frame subset ensembling we achieve the highest score across 3 metrics in the VQA AG task and the second highest in the VQA MC task.

### [EgoP$^{2}$C: Internalizing Plan-Perceive-Compose for Spatial Reasoning in Egocentric Video](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22681)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CV` · 2610.22681

> Discusses internalizing spatial reasoning in VLMs, relevant for context management in RAG applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Answering complex spatial reasoning questions from egocentric video requires determining what evidence is needed, acquiring targeted observations, and combining them through multiple reasoning steps. Existing vision-language models (VLMs) struggle to perform this process reliably. Agentic systems address this challenge by querying external vision and geometry tools, but training their reasoning leaves these tools unchanged, so inaccurate observations can still lead to incorrect answers. We introduce EgoP$^{2}$C, a framework that internalizes a plan-perceive-compose process within a single VLM. The Planner teacher supervises which evidence-seeking questions to ask, when to stop gathering evidence, and how to compose the final answer. The Perception teacher supervises the video-grounded answers to those questions. At inference, the student executes this adaptive workflow end-to-end, without external spatial tools. Across three spatial reasoning benchmarks, EgoP$^{2}$C students built from Qwen3-VL-8B and SenseNova-SI-1.3-8B outperform their base models by an average of 9.2 and 7.5 percentage points, respectively.
