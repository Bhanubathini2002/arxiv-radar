### [Natural Language Programming: Rethinking Language, Memory, and Cognition in the Era of Generative AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23106)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.23106

> Discusses concepts of conversational memory and dialogic interaction, directly relevant to chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Generative Artificial Intelligence is transforming the role of natural language in computational systems. In classical Natural Language Processing (NLP), language was treated as an object of computation: something to analyse, classify, translate, or generate. Large Language Models (LLMs) challenge this perspective, enabling natural language to function increasingly as an executable cognitive medium through which humans structure reasoning, orchestrate problem solving, and regulate hybrid human/AI workflows. This paper introduces Natural Language Programming (NLPg) as a theoretical and epistemological extension of traditional NLP. Rather than focusing on language understanding alone, NLPg conceptualises prompts as dynamic cognitive artefacts that externalise goals, constraints, assumptions, validation criteria, and intermediate reasoning states. Prompting is thus not merely a technical interaction technique, but a form of dialogic cognitive programming operating within distributed human/AI memory architectures. Building on theories of distributed cognition, extended mind, metacognition, and dialogic learning, the paper proposes the notion of Dialogic Working Memory, describing how prompts and conversational states function as temporary external memory structures supporting reasoning continuity and reflective regulation. We further map existing memory architectures (BTMA, SKGE, RMR) onto this framework, illustrating how episodic, semantic, and metacognitive layers support long-horizon conversational coherence. Finally, implications for computational linguistics and AI/mediated knowledge systems are discussed, suggesting a post symbolic and post interface paradigm in which natural language operates simultaneously as communication medium, executable specification, cognitive scaffold, and distributed memory architecture.

### [StitchRAG: Constructing Compact and Evidence-Preserving Contexts for RAG with Diffusion-Based Stitching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22766)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.22766

> Directly addresses RAG architectures and the efficiency of constructed contexts.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) improves factuality by grounding large language models (LLMs) in external evidence. However, long retrieved contexts incur computational overhead and can even hurt performance due to noisy or irrelevant content. We propose \textsc{StitchRAG}, a framework for constructing compact context in RAG. \textsc{StitchRAG} builds a faithful, information-dense context while dynamically compressing the retrieved passages. We adapt the size reduction ratio to each passage by formulating a utility maximization trading-off the retained information and computation costs. We then extract answer candidates and query-related keywords from passages as anchors and collect passage sentences containing anchors under the derived size constraint. Finally, we connect or stitch the collected sentences together using text diffusion to form a coherent and natural context. Experiments on five open-domain QA benchmarks show that \textsc{StitchRAG} consistently outperforms diverse multi-step and compression baselines. Extensive ablations show the effectiveness of dynamic size allocation, anchor preservation, and diffusion-based stitching.

### [EGS: A Complementary Safety Framework for Educational LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22203)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.22203

> It presents a safety framework for LLMs, relevant for input/output guardrails in RAG applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large Language Models (LLMs) offer strong potential for personalized education, but adolescent-facing deployment raises risks of toxic, biased, misleading, or developmentally inappropriate content. We argue that educational safety should move beyond binary refusal: student queries are often ambiguous or curiosity-driven, requiring contextual guidance rather than simple blocking. We introduce the Educational Guardrail System (EGS), a lightweight, model-agnostic runtime framework that complements intrinsic alignment through query moderation, scenario-aware pedagogical guidance, and response moderation. EGS triages inputs by risk level, redirects gray-zone queries with category-specific educational interventions, and audits outputs before delivery. Experiments across general and education-specific benchmarks show that EGS improves moderation performance, refusal quality, and jailbreak robustness across diverse LLMs. These findings suggest that defense-in-depth guardrails can strengthen safety while preserving the pedagogical utility required in educational LLMs. Our code and model weights will be publicly available to support community adoption.

</details>

### [GauntletScore: Auditable Fact-Checking That Matches the Best Published Checkers on LLM-AggreFact](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22027)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.22027

> Directly addresses methods for evaluating grounded text generation and checking strengths, highly relevant.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Checking whether a generated sentence is supported by its source document is a standard safeguard for AI-written text, and the strongest published checkers are single models. We describe GauntletScore, an evidence engine whose few rules and thresholds were set on the benchmark's development split. It puts typed questions to independent readers from different providers and records each answer as a measurement in an evidence ledger. It combines the measurements with a fixed Bayesian rule that counts a contradiction only when two readers agree. On the full LLM-AggreFact test split (29,320 claims across 11 datasets), following the leaderboard's approach, GauntletScore achieves 79.51 balanced accuracy, above every average published on the leaderboard as of 15 November 2026. That places it level with the best of them, nimble-v4-max (79.45 from its published dataset scores; the engine's 95% interval, 78.47 to 80.53, contains it). On the same claims it beats two of its three readers used alone (Readers A and B) by 1.66 and 2.17 points (95% intervals 0.80 to 2.53 and 1.29 to 3.06). Without AggreFact-CNN, described below, the leads are 0.90 and 1.32 and both intervals stay above zero. The engine also records the evidence behind every verdict, which a single model does not. Its largest margin is on full-length news articles checked against multi-sentence summaries (AggreFact-CNN), a format that resembles much regulated writing. There it scores 76.45, the best on the leaderboard, against 70.1 for the next best and 63.9 to 67.8 for the six systems with the highest averages. The test split was run once, on an engine frozen before the first test claim, under a stopping plan committed in advance. We release per-claim predictions and the scoring script.

### [TUSI: A Citation-Grounded Graph-Based Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21823)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.21823

> Directly addresses evaluation benchmarks for RAG systems, crucial for assessing performance and grounding.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Evaluating retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems in Persian law remains challenging because benchmarks lack realism, grounding, and coverage of legal reasoning. Existing resources are either synthetically generated from statutory texts, lacking natural information needs, or derived from informal legal question--answer sources without explicit linkage to authoritative legal documents, making them unsuitable for rigorous evaluation of grounded legal reasoning. To address this gap, we introduce TUSI-Bench, a structured Persian legal benchmark designed for the evaluation of RAG-based systems. TUSI-Bench is built upon a multi-layered legal knowledge framework containing 7,261 legal articles and integrating codified statutes, legal commentaries, doctrinal writings, and Supreme Court unification rulings. We also construct a legal knowledge graph linking articles and concepts for structure-aware retrieval. Based on this foundation, TUSI-Bench provides two complementary evaluation tasks: (i) TUSI-QA, an open-ended question answering benchmark containing 1,999 questions that requires grounded responses with explicit legal citations, and (ii) TUSI-Bar, a multiple-choice benchmark containing approximately 500 four-option questions derived from Iranian Bar examination questions. Furthermore, we propose a graph-based retrieval baseline that leverages relational structure within the legal knowledge graph. Experimental results demonstrate that incorporating graph-based relations improves retrieval recall by up to 8 percentage points compared to standard baselines, highlighting the importance of structured legal representations for RAG systems. The proposed benchmark provides a standardized resource for evaluating grounded legal reasoning in Persian and enables future research on structured legal intelligence.

### [GuardAgent: A Memory-Augmented Agentic Framework for Adaptive Detection of Adversarial Promotions in LLM-Based Recommendation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22274)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.22274

> Directly addresses safety and detection in LLm-based systems, key for RAG applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

In traditional recommender systems (RSs), textual information typically serves as auxiliary signals for representation learning. In contrast, in LLM-based RSs, textual information becomes the primary medium for recommendation generation through semantic understanding and reasoning. This shift fundamentally changes the attack surface, making promotion-driven manipulations more feasible, effective, and harder to detect. However, effectively detecting such attacks remains largely unexplored. To fill this gap, we present the first systematic study on detecting promotion-driven attacks against LLM-based RSs, aiming to safeguard next-generation recommendation platforms. Specifically, we propose GuardAgent, a novel LLM-powered agentic detection framework that operates through a loop of Perception, Action, Reflection and Memory. In contrast to conventional LLM-based detectors that function in a static, one-off, and opaque fashion, GuardAgent continually learns and self-evolves to adapt to unseen threat patterns while generating explainable reasoning traces and outcomes. Extensive experiments on real-world datasets demonstrate that GuardAgent achieves state-of-the-art generalization performance, laying the foundation for robust and trustworthy recommendation in practical settings.

### [Retrieval-Augmented Correction for Generalist Medical Image Segmentation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23189)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CV` · 2611.23189

> Discusses retrieval-augmented correction methods for segmentation, relevant to document ingestion strategies.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Automated segmentation is routinely used as the first step in quantitative image-based clinical workflows, where predicted masks are subsequently reviewed and corrected by human operators. Automated correction of the residual structural errors is currently addressed one anatomy at a time, so the number of trained correctors scales with the number of segmentation targets. We present GRACE, a retrieval-augmented refiner that edits an input mask under context drawn from a shared knowledge base of verified prior cases. The refiner is trained once on a multi-organ corpus spanning compact organs, cavities, branching vessels, and tumour lesions. In the held-out dataset-transfer evaluation, three held-out datasets query a fixed knowledge base built from training data only. A separate leave-one-anatomy-out experiment tests parameter-free index extension: verified cases of an unseen solid organ enter the knowledge base, and the refiner stays frozen. A light shape-aware projection head on a frozen multimodal encoder maps embedding similarity to the shape agreement of the masks, so the knowledge base returns cases that the refiner can use as a shape prior. We evaluate GRACE on 18 public datasets spanning CT, MRI and ultrasound, using nine refinement configurations and seven upstream segmentors that span weak, mid, and strong operating regimes. We report each change with the DSC of its input mask. On the weak tier, the input DSC is 0.386 and GRACE adds up to +0.35 DSC. On a strong segmentor, the input DSC is 0.932 and the mean change is small. Transfer to an imaging modality that is absent from training and from the index is partial and depends on the anatomy, and remains an open research direction. Our results suggest retrieval-conditioned refinement is a practical alternative to anatomy-specific correction models. Code: https://github.com/robustml-eurecom/grace

### [Mind the Gap: A Deployed Multi-Agent System for Documentation Maintenance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23142)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.23142

> Relevant to documentation systems for chat applications but not specifically about RAG architecture.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The "AI revolution" has enabled teams across the technology industry to deliver software features at unprecedented velocity. While code evolves faster than ever, customer-facing documentation often struggles to stay up to speed. As users increasingly rely on LLM-based agents that consume documentation as their primary interface, the impact of this documentation gap worsens: stale information propagates and is treated as authoritative. Signals for identifying where documentation has fallen behind already exist in abundance, both explicit (e.g., resolved support tickets revealing what documentation lacks) and implicit (e.g., code-documentation discrepancies detectable from automated loops). We deploy a multi-agent system, built around a triage agent we call "TriAgent," that continuously extracts these signals and repairs documentation drift. Over 43 days of shadow-mode operation, our system triaged 499 tickets and surfaced 92 gaps in the documentation. Using a panel of LLM judges from independent model families, 81.1% of TriAgent's verifiable assertions were correct (245 of the 302 assertions the human resolution addresses; majority of three judges); on length-comparable pairs the panel preferred TriAgent's response in 70.5% of pairs and the human engineer's in 23.2% (the rest had no judge majority). Importantly, agent- and human-sourced replies tended to be complementary, with the human often surfacing insights that were either buried within the existing documentation or missing from it entirely; our system turns each such insight into a documentation gap and, after human review, a fix. Repairing a gap measurably improved the agent's later answer: when the agent re-answered the same ticket after the repair, blinded judges preferred the post-repair answer over its own pre-repair answer 71.4% of the time (n=35). A blinded human audit confirms strong alignment on self-improvement.

### [LMMR: Learned Multigranular Memory Retrieval for Small Language Model Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22425)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.22425

> Describes a memory retrieval method that could improve RAG performance in handling multi-hop questions.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Small language model agents have limited space to read the memory needed for multi-hop questions. Relevant facts are often scattered across records whose surrounding text consumes much of that space. We propose LMMR, a learned multigranular memory retrieval method that selects both the next source and how much of it to read. LMMR links paragraphs and sentence fragments to their original sources and pairs views that supply the same missing support during training. Relevant distractors and repeated information provide contrasting examples. At inference, the reranker scores what each candidate adds to an anchor passage, and a selector chooses the evidence within a token budget. On 22,398 official validation questions from HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, and MuSiQue, a frozen 2B answer model reaches 61.47, 43.26, and 30.58 F1 under a 400-token budget. The gains over query expansion are 3.06, 2.14, and 4.55 points, while memory input decreases by 23.1-26.9%. LMMR also uses 23.5-27.7% fewer input tokens than a coarse retriever trained on the same questions. Against that retriever followed by LLMLingua-2 at comparable input lengths, F1 gains reach 3.99 points and are significant on HotpotQA and 2WikiMultihopQA after Holm correction. All eight small answer models evaluated on HotpotQA improve over query expansion and MMR in both F1 and exact match.

### [SIMQ: A Single Graph-based Index for Multi-Vector Query](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22079)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.22079

> Focuses on indexing and efficiency in vector query retrieval, relevant for vector search strategies.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Multi-vector retrieval models such as ColBERT achieve state-of-the-art effectiveness by representing documents as sets of token-level vectors and scoring via MaxSim, but their high computational cost limits scalability to large corpora. Existing approximate methods face fundamental bottlenecks: centroid interaction matrix approaches require computing a full matrix at every query and rely on GPU-accelerated clustering, while data-oblivious compression methods yield unstable recall that requires repeated compensation. We propose SIMQ (Single graph Index for Multi-vector Query), a framework that bridges single-vector graph indices and multi-vector retrieval through data-aware sampling-based clustering and per-token graph navigation, simultaneously bypassing all aforementioned bottlenecks. SIMQ organizes all token vectors into fine-grained clusters via data-aware sampling, builds a single graph index over cluster centroids independently of PLAID's residual representation, and approximates the MaxSim score through per-token graph search without materializing any interaction matrix or relying on GPU acceleration. We provide theoretical analysis showing that, under a manifold assumption and successful cell retrieval, data-aware partitioning has a smaller expected approximation error than a data-oblivious partition. Building on this foundation, SIMQ+ further introduces count-based scoring and a dynamic candidate set to improve robustness across varying query conditions. Experiments on five benchmarks compare recall, throughput, and construction time against several baselines, primarily on CPU; large-scale PLAID k-means centroids are built on a separate GPU machine. SIMQ has been integrated into VSAG, an Ant Group's open-source vector search library.

### [Reading Scene States: Uncovering Safety Failures in Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23206)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CV` · 2611.23206

> Addresses safety and vulnerabilities in vision-language models, pertinent to guardrails for LLM apps.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Vision-language models (VLMs) can provide harmful assistance by interpreting visual events, even without an explicit request for the underlying harmful objective. We identify this \textbf{scene-state safety vulnerability} and introduce \textbf{SceneSafetyBench}, comprising 375 image--question pairs spanning Initial, Medium, and Ultimate scene states and five visual presentations. Across eight VLMs, scene-state inputs elicit harmful assistance with attack success rates (ASRs) of 45.6--72.3\%. Comparative analysis across three VLMs shows that scene-state inputs most closely resemble QR-related images in their layer-wise safety-related signal profiles, while exhibiting lower average late-layer readouts and distinct signal dynamics during generation. To turn this vulnerability into a systematic attack, we propose \textbf{S3TA}, which translates harmful objectives into scene states, queries image--question pairs independently, and assembles the responses. On seven MM-SafetyBench categories across eight open-weight and commercial VLMs, S3TA achieves a model-average ASR of \textbf{71.2\%, exceeding the strongest baseline by 6.1 percentage points}. Text-side filters and visual moderation provide limited protection. These findings highlight the need to safeguard the assistance models infer through scene-grounded reasoning, beyond screening input content.

</details>

### [Transfer Once, Ask Locally: Compact Audio-Visual Memory for On-Device Question Answering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23085)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CV` · 2611.23085

> Discusses a compact memory framework for audio-visual QA, which has potential relevance to retrieval and efficiency in RAG.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Audio-visual question answering on lightweight user devices requires balancing speed, memory usage, privacy, and communication. One approach is to run audio-visual encoders on a server and a lightweight language decoder on the user device, keeping questions local while offloading media encoding. However, transferring and processing the full multimodal representation remains costly. We propose a training-free, query-agnostic token pruning framework that constructs a compact, reusable memory before transmission. We define video spatio-temporal blocks and aligned audio windows as players and estimate their importance through Monte Carlo Shapley valuation. We use an embedding coverage objective that combines query-independent utility with cross-modal redundancy calibration and rewards unimodal diversity through marginal coverage gains. We retain the original token embeddings of high-scoring players under a limited token budget and transfer the resulting memory once for reuse across subsequent local questions. Experiments on Video-MME, Daily-Omni, and Video-Holmes using video-SALMONN 2+ (3B) and video-SALMONN 2 Pro (4B) show that our method achieves the best or tied-best QA accuracy among the compared compression methods across all evaluated settings. On Daily-Omni with video-SALMONN 2+ (3B) at 80\% pruning, our method improves accuracy by 12.86--14.62 percentage points over these methods while achieving the lowest device-side LLM prefill and decoding latency, with up to $1.92\times$ decoding speedup at comparable embedding memory usage.

</details>
