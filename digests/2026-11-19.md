### [Jailbreak Detection on Large Language Models via Contrastive Prototype Structures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24936)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.24936

> The paper focuses on jailbreak detection, aligning well with guardrail and safety interests for LLM applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Jailbreak attempts and harmful prompts remain a challenge for the safe deployment of large language models. We study whether contrastively learned representations can support safety detection without training an additional downstream classifier. We propose Jailbreak Detection with Prototype Inference (JDPI), which uses binary safety labels to construct harmful and benign prototypes and makes predictions from relative prototype distances. JDPI uses only binary safety labels for encoder adaptation, prototype construction, and validation. In our setup, it uses fewer labeled examples than the classifier baseline (Method A) by avoiding the separate split used to train the downstream classifier. Across four benchmarks covering different input formats, contrastive fine-tuning improves JDPI's detection accuracy by 9.1-18.0 percentage points. Using the same encoder, JDPI achieves slightly higher ROC-AUC on one benchmark and stays within 0.017 of the baseline across all four. With dataset-specific adaptation, JDPI exceeds the released Llama Guard 3-8B baseline by 0.173-0.310 in ROC-AUC across the four benchmarks. In multi-turn replay on TomGibbs, JDPI uses accumulated user history as the conversation unfolds, achieving higher conversation-level accuracy than when it uses the current turn alone. Across 12 directed transfers, JDPI outperforms the classifier by an average of 1.59 percentage points in accuracy, demonstrating stronger cross-dataset robustness.

### [A Hybrid RAG Framework for Deceptive Review Detection: Integrating RoBERTa-LoRA, FAISS-Based Retrieval, and LLM Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24049)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.24049

> Directly relates to RAG architecture using vector retrieval and LLM reasoning for review detection.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The growth of deceptive online opinions poses a critical risk of misinformation and fraud. This paper proposes a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) system to automatically distinguish spam reviews. The architecture integrates RoBERTa with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) classification, FAISS semantic vector retrieval, and a Large Language Model (LLM) for final reasoning. The system was evaluated across balanced and severely imbalanced real-world scenarios. Results demonstrate that five epochs of fine-tuning enable robust domain transfer, achieving an F1-score of 0.90 in cross-domain evaluation, outperforming baselines (OpenAI Detector,F1 = 0.82) and demonstrating competitive performance against specialized in-domain SOTA models (fakeRoBERTa,F1 = 0.97). The proposed architecture provides evidence-backed explainability and statistically significant precision gains while maintaining comparable overall detection performance.

</details>

### [A Mechanistic Analysis of In-Context Learning and Out-of-Context Hallucination](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24000)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.24000

> Examines hallucination in context learning, essential for grounded QA evaluation.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large language models can infer and follow specific rules through in-context learning (ICL), yet may fail to remain faithful to provided evidence, giving rise to out-of-context (OOC) hallucination. Existing work has largely investigated the underlying mechanisms of ICL and OOC hallucination along separate lines. However, this separation obscures the mechanistic basis for these seemingly opposing outcomes. In this work, we study this contrast through the unified view of functional roles in dynamic context. The key observation is that role information can be internally represented without reliably constraining output. We derive a first-order decomposition to characterize the role effect, showing that its substantial influence depends on two necessary conditions, namely sufficient contribution magnitude and coherent signed aggregation. Causal analysis further shows that role span contributions affect key token choices involving response structure, content selection, and factual values. We further show that role-consistent choices depend on sufficient role span support at these key decisions, while failing choices can be reversed when this support overcomes the deficit. These findings support a common mechanistic view of ICL and OOC hallucination: each functional role defines the pattern of output decisions over which role span contributions must remain effective, and differences in this organization account for the seemingly opposing outcomes.

</details>

### [Dreaming Up Authors: Factual Prevalence and LLM Hallucinations in Academic Authorship Attribution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23530)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.23530

> Investigates hallucination rates in LLMs, directly relevant for evaluating faithfulness in grounded QA.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large Language Models (LLMs) are widely used for question answering but are prone to hallucinations, yet the underlying causes, particularly the role of factual prevalence in training data, remain underexplored. In this work, we study this through academic authorship attribution, hypothesizing that hallucinations are more likely for less prevalent papers. Using citation count as a proxy for factual prevalence, we curate a dataset of 1.92 million papers across eight scientific disciplines with stratified samples by citation level. Experiments on three state-of-the-art LLMs confirm our hypothesis, revealing a robust inverse relationship between hallucination rate and citation count across all models and disciplines, with hallucination rates exceeding 98% for rarely cited papers and falling below 37% for highly cited ones. Our in-depth analysis further shows that these failures primarily reflect retrieval limitations rather than knowledge absence, and hidden-state probing of open-source models shows that factual prevalence is encoded in pre-generation representations, providing convergent mechanistic evidence for the prevalence-hallucination relationship. The code and data are available at https://github.com/quinnzhong1/dreaming-up-authors.

</details>

### [Can Programs Replace LLM Judges? Executable Program Judges for Scalable and Efficient Relevance Labeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24849)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.24849

> It proposes a method for automatic relevance labeling, ensuring reliability in retrieval evaluation, pertinent to retrieval benchmarks.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large language model (LLM) judges have become a common way to create large-scale relevance judgments for retrieval evaluation. However, each judgment requires a potentially expensive call to a frontier model, and the judgments are hard to interpret and may not be reproducible. In this paper, we ask whether an LLM can write a readable program for each topic that replaces its per-document judgments. To address these issues, we propose ExecJudges, a method for generating fast, inspectable and scalable judges for automatic relevance labeling. Specifically, ExecJudges uses a frontier LLM to turn assessment guidelines into executable programs, which rely only on lexical matching, standard NLP libraries like parsers, and pool statistics. An ExecJudges-L variant also uses examples of human labels if available. We evaluate ExecJudges on seven TREC collections by comparing the produced labels with trained NIST assessors, frontier LLM judges, and recently proposed per-topic classifiers. Our results show that ExecJudges agrees with NIST assessors at the level of the Claude Sonnet 5.5 LLM judges on TREC DL 2019-2021. With a small number of human labels per topic, the ExecJudges-L variant agrees with NIST more than per-topic classifiers with the same labels on all seven collections, and either exceeds the agreement of LLM judges provided the same labels or is within about 0.05 of it in Krippendorff's alpha. The labels rank the official TREC DL runs with Kendall's tau of 0.87 to 0.94, and writing the programs incurs a one-time cost of $0.17 to $0.49 per topic, with no additional costs for grading. Our results suggest that ExecJudges offers a reproducible, inspectable, efficient and cost-effective alternative to LLM judges for labeling deep result pools for system evaluation or ranking improvements.

</details>

### [MIRAGE: A Framework for Multi-hop Reasoning under Dynamic Knowledge Injection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24058)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.24058

> Discusses multi-hop reasoning which is relevant to multi-hop retrieval and grounded generation.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Multi-hop Question Answering (MQA) requires large language models (LLMs) to retrieve and synthesize multiple pieces of evidence into a coherent reasoning path, which is essential for complex tasks. However, prior work on knowledge updating for MQA has largely focused on simple fact edits, overlooking a more challenging and realistic setting---\emph{Knowledge Injection}---in which the model must simultaneously accommodate both conflicting updates and entirely novel information. Under this setting, a hop-level answer is often wrong simply because the model trusts the wrong knowledge source: it clings to outdated parametric memory when the injected knowledge conflicts with it (K2), and it cannot answer when the required fact is entirely new (K3). To investigate this, we introduce NEXUS, the first benchmark tailored for multi-hop reasoning under Knowledge Injection, which explicitly covers both conflicting (K2) and incremental (K3) knowledge. We further propose MIRAGE, an efficient, training-free framework whose core component, the Correlation-aware Dual-path Reasoner (CDR), resolves such per-hop source-trust errors via P(True)-based confidence routing, complemented by a lightweight template-guided corrector for residual structural deviations. Extensive experiments across four LLM families and two benchmarks demonstrate that MIRAGE consistently improves both final-answer accuracy and hop-level reasoning accuracy, establishing a new state of the art in this challenging setting.

</details>

### [FutureBench: A Renewable Framework for Evaluating Knowledge-Context Conflict in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23601)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.23601

> Introduces a framework for evaluating knowledge-context conflicts, relevant to RAG evaluation and grounded responses.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Asking a model to check retrieved text can also tell it which source to trust. We introduce FutureBench, a renewable framework for evaluating knowledge-context conflict using timestamped questions, controlled passage triplets, and no-context probes. Across 3,629 questions and six models, Verify (a prompt combining checking with passage priority) raises false-answer adoption in all five models tested on pre-cutoff events, mainly by displacing answers that were correct under Baseline, which allows both passage evidence and model knowledge. On post-cutoff events, only o4-mini increases adoption (+13.7 percentage points). A four-prompt comparison on 902 post-cutoff questions finds that passage priority without checking raises adoption, while moving to Verify lowers it in all five models. Adversarial-Aware, which warns of misleading passages and prioritizes model knowledge, restores 82.5-98.9% of displaced pre-cutoff answers with under one point of change in true-passage accuracy. FutureBench supports repeated evaluation on fixed cohorts and extension to new events as models change.

</details>
