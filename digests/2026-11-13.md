### [RMIT-ADM+S at TREC RAG 2026: Research Agents, Retriever Ablations, and Open-Weight Replication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16425)
**Score 10/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.16425

> The paper directly discusses retrieval strategies and quality within a RAG context, making it essential.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We describe the RMIT-ADM+S team's participation in the TREC 2026 Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) track using the ClimbMix corpus. We submitted seven research-agent runs using proprietary OpenAI and open-weight models, exploring different retrieval strategies, writer models, and agent workflows. Pre-submission ablations identified writer selection and adjacent-chunk expansion as the main factors affecting answer quality. In the official NIST evaluation, six of seven runs exceeded the track median across all three rubric/nugget collections. Our best-performing run, brief-hybrid, achieved the highest all-item coverage among our submissions and tied the track maximum (0.892) for Batch-1 vital coverage. Human side-by-side preferences differed across evaluation batches: the open-weight owa-glm5 led Batch 1 despite lower automated coverage, while sol-research-first led Batch 2. These findings highlight differences between automated coverage measures and human preferences, as well as the potential of lower-cost open-weight models for research-agent systems.

### [Faithful in Steps: Improving Generalization and Citation in RAG via Query Decomposition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15538)
**Score 10/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.15538

> The paper directly addresses enhancing citation and generalization in RAG systems, making it essential for building RAG applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augment generation is a prevalent strategy to mitigate hallucinations of LLMs. The attributable RAG (RAGQ) generates quotes for its answers. The quotes indicate which input contexts support the RAG to derive the answers, enhancing the answer's verifiability and trustworthiness. However, existing RAGQs exhibit significant degradation when dealing with questions that require multi-hop reasoning and multi-modal understanding, suffering from over-citation, implicit entity identification failure, and poor generalization. In this paper, we propose a novel RAGQ framework, namely QDRAG. QDRAG breaks down the input question into atomic subquestions to identify the implicit entities. Then, the reranker prunes context distractors to eliminate the downstream over-citation. To facilitate query decomposition, we propose two zero-shot approaches: QD-C and QD-R, which guide the QD MLLM to decompose the question based on context knowledge and retrieval rewards, respectively. One interesting finding is that finetuning on the QD task shows better generalizability compared to directly finetuning on the downstream RAGQ task. Experiments on four multi-modal QA benchmarks demonstrate QDRAG's efficacy in grounding answers and generating faithful citations. The framework significantly outperforms all the baselines on both in-domain and out-of-domain tests, even surpassing Gemini-Pro.

</details>

### [Active Learning of Approximate LLM Ranking for Energy-Efficient Retrieval of Long Queries](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16488)
**Score 10/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.16488

> Proposes LLM Active Approximation Model (LAAM) for energy-efficient retrieval, directly relevant to retrieval strategies in RAG.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Search engines and Large Language Models (LLMs) offer complementary strengths for document retrieval: lexical retrievers which rely on keyword matching are fast and energy-efficient, whereas LLMs capture semantic meaning and excel at interpreting long, context-rich queries. However, this comes at a cost that is computationally and energetically prohibitive. We propose the LLM Active Approximation Model (LAAM), a hybrid retrieval framework that introduces a lightweight learned predictor which serves as an intermediary between semantic retrieval and LLM retrieval. LAAM is refined through an active learning loop where an LLM labels a small number of documents. By concentrating LLM evaluations on the most informative documents, the framework substantially reduces inference cost while preserving retrieval effectiveness. We evaluate the proposed framework on TREC Robust 2004 using Lucene with BM25 retrieval and DeepSeek v3 as the LLM, under three levels of query verbosity. For verbose queries, LAAM achieves 87% of average P@10 achieved by DeepSeek's reranking (0.459 vs. 0.526), while requiring only 3.3% of the per-query energy consumption. LAAM is statistically significantly better than BM25 and is indistinguishable from DeepSeek on average precision, though not on MAP (Friedman test, P<0.05 with post-hoc testing). Overall, our results demonstrate that selectively approximating LLM relevance judgments through active learning enables retrieval accuracy to be controlled directly through the evaluation (and consequently energy) budget. Rather than committing to either inexpensive (and less accurate) lexical retrieval or exhaustive (and expensive) LLM reranking, our framework exposes a continuous spectrum of operating points along the energy--accuracy curve, allowing practitioners to select how to trade retrieval quality with computational budget.

</details>

### [CapLite: Prompt-Injection Defense for Small Language Model Agents without Model-Written Security Artifacts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17115)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.17115

> Focuses on prompt injection defense, directly relevant to guardrails and safety for LLM apps.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Enforcement defenses against prompt injection, such as CaMeL, Progent, IPIGuard and DRIFT, ask the agent's own model to write a security artifact (a program, a privilege policy, a dependency graph or a constraint list) that code then enforces. We ask whether small language model (SLM) agents can be trusted to write these artifacts. Across 15 open-weight models, 13 of them between 1.7B and 12B parameters, they cannot: the policies, graphs and constraints that the agent's model writes admit attacks on 14 (Progent), 13 (IPIGuard) and 11 (DRIFT) of the models, and CaMeL's programs fail to run in up to 68% of episodes. We present CapLite, which derives its policy from the tool schemas, commits the write calls before any tool result is read, and admits a write only if every argument that decides whom or what it affects is stated in the user's request. This guarantees write integrity: injected text cannot add a write, and no write reaches a recipient, amount, object or destination that the user did not state. No attack succeeds in 13,935 attacked AgentDojo episodes. CapLite reaches utility under attack comparable to CaMeL's (29.0% vs. 27.2%) with fewer tokens than every other enforcement defense and than the undefended agent: 17.1k per episode, 18% of CaMeL's, and the highest utility per token of all enforcement defenses.

</details>

### [Discovering Effective Agent Memory Designs through Program Evolution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17068)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.17068

> Offers empirical insights into agent memory design, essential for conversational memory in RAG chats.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Program evolution provides a promising approach to discover agent memory designs and study the choices underlying their effectiveness. We introduce MAPE, a framework that evolves whole memory programs from minimal seeds through a standardized lifecycle interface and an iterative diagnosis-proposal loop. Experiments on LoCoMo and LongMemEval yield an absolute accuracy gain of up to 12% over manually designed baselines at 35% of the serving cost, with further evaluations supporting transfer across serving backbones and benchmarks. Empirical analyses and ablations identify and validate two recurring design patterns: retaining raw conversations as retrievable evidence preserves details omitted from extracted memories, while composing retrieved evidence according to query intent helps the answering model use it effectively. Transplanting the composition module into five existing memory systems improves accuracy across all five. Beyond these patterns, the search objective shapes where computation goes: optimizing serving cost alongside accuracy shifts computation from LLM-based memory construction to selective processing at query time while retaining competitive accuracy. Together, these findings offer empirical lessons for agent memory design. Code is available at https://github.com/ash-ding/MAPE.

</details>

### [Beyond Compressing Everything: Contrastive Memory Learning for Selective Context Compression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16384)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.16384

> The paper proposes a context compression method which is highly relevant for efficient information retrieval in RAG.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

As large language models (LLMs) advance toward increasingly long inputs, soft context compression offers a promising approach to efficient inference by converting long inputs into compact memory slots. Under limited memory budgets, effective context compression remains challenging because task-relevant evidence varies substantially across queries, ranging from localized evidence to multiple distant spans or information dispersed throughout the full document. Instead of compressing everything indiscriminately, we propose CoMem, a query-guided selective context compression framework that learns memory representations conditioned on both the document and the query. CoMem develops Contrastive Compressor Learning to achieve query-sensitive yet context-preserving memory representations. To inject a strong prior over query-dependent evidence distributions, we devise a same-context hard negative mining strategy by pairing a single document with multiple queries, requiring the compressor to selectively retain information according to each query's distinct evidence needs. To address the restricted information flow imposed by causal masking, we further enable bidirectional attention among memory slots. Experiments demonstrate that CoMem consistently outperforms existing context compression methods across compression ratios on question answering and RULER-NIAH benchmarks. Our code is available at https://github.com/scar-on/CoMem.

</details>

### [We Are What We Remember: Mental-State-Driven Memory for Long-Term Character Dialogue](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16223)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.16223

> Mainly contributes to conversational memory systems, highly relevant for chat application context management.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Shared history in human dialogue is not merely a repository of facts, but the foundation of evolving relationships and mutual understanding. However, mainstream conversational memory systems rely on passive semantic similarity over external fact stores, frequently retrieving topically similar records while missing relationally pivotal experiences. Inspired by cognitive psychology's Self-Memory System (SMS), we propose MindMem, a mental-state-driven memory framework that asynchronously consolidates shared experiences into source-linked mental states of the character, the user, and their relationship. At each turn, a specialized mental-state reasoner (Mind) contextualizes these persistent states to anticipate latent user needs, form proactive intentions, and recover supporting episodic evidence that surface-level matching overlooks. To evaluate history-sensitive adaptation beyond factual recall, we introduce MemReal-Bench, a longitudinal benchmark grounded in month-long real-user interactions across dyadic and group scenes. By pairing Direct Question Answering (DQA) with Situated Memory Realization (SMR), it tests whether agents bridge the gap between knowing past facts and naturally using shared history, world rules, and relational trajectories. On MemReal-Bench, MindMem reaches 59.03% SMR and 82.58% DQA, exceeding the strongest memory baseline by 24.19 and 9.19 percentage points, respectively, and outperforming oracle target-memory injection on SMR. Code and dataset are available at https://github.com/jiazhicheng7-ctrl/MindMem.

### [Read Once, Answer Many: Parallel Prompting for Shared-Context LLM Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15130)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.15130

> Introduces Parallel Prompting for efficient inference in document-based queries, directly aligns with user interests in RAG applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

LLM workloads sometimes ask many independent questions about the same long document, as in document QA, data labeling and LLM-based evaluation. Prefix caching removes the redundant prefill shared by these queries, but each decode step still reads the document's key-value (KV) cache once per question, so decoding is bound by memory bandwidth and scales with the number of questions. We present Parallel Prompting, which decodes P questions as P independent streams of a single request all at once, fixing this issue. In our method, every step reads the shared document once for all P streams, and a stream-id attention mask, evaluated inside the attention kernel, keeps each answer conditioned only on the document and its own question. We implement Parallel Prompting in SGLang by adding about 80 lines to three existing files and a small engine package; the mask needs no dense tensor, and every decode step has a fixed shape that is captured as a CUDA graph. With CodeLlama-7B, 8k-16k-token documents and 64-256 questions per document, Parallel Prompting is 3.7x faster than stock SGLang with its FlashInfer backend on an A100 and 5.9-6.0x faster on an RTX A6000, on two SGLang releases, including the most recent release. Timed on identical inputs, it is faster than stock SGLang in all 144 runs (2 GPUs x 2 releases x 6 workloads x 6 input sets). Code: https://github.com/jlab-nlp/parallel-prompting

</details>

### [CURATOR: Scalable Fine-grained Evidence Selection for Retrieval-Augmented Generation using Decision Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16366)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.16366

> This paper addresses span selection within RAG, directly relevant to improving model context utilization.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) provides entire retrieved passages to the LLM, even though only a handful of spans within them contain the evidence pertaining to the question. While precise selection of these spans can improve context utilization, identifying them in isolation is challenging. A span may appear irrelevant by itself, and deciding whether to retain it may require examining other retrieved information. One can evaluate each span's candidacy with the full retrieved context, but this is computationally inefficient as the same text must be processed repeatedly. Pretrained decision model can be used to efficiently evaluate multiple spans simultaneously, allowing each decision to consider the surrounding spans as well. However, our experiments show that their ability to identify relevant evidence deteriorates as the number of simultaneous decisions increases. To address this, we introduce CURATOR, a fine-tuned decision model trained to make reliable evidence-selection decisions over large candidate sets. By training a decision model to select varying numbers of supporting spans from large candidate pools, CURATOR develops the capability to select evidence for RAG while retaining the computational efficiency of joint decision-making. We observe that CURATOR maintains a strong evidence discrimination capability as the number of candidates increases, generalizes across different tasks and document domains, and improves downstream RAG performance, particularly when the available context is limited.

</details>

### [Safety as a Budget: Calibrating the Control Channel of Constrained GRPO for Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16628)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CV` · 2611.16628

> Directly addresses safety and calibration for vision-language models, relevant for guardrails in LLM apps.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Reinforcement learning can teach vision-language models (VLMs) to decline harmful image-text requests, but how safe the result becomes is set indirectly, through reward weights found by trial runs. Stating safety as budgets on the rate of harmful assistance and the rate of over-refusal, enforced by Lagrange multipliers inside GRPO with an off-the-shelf scorer as the cost, is the natural fix, and it fails twice. The multiplier compares the scorer's mean with the budget, and scorers built for ranking get the mean wrong: a fixed-point analysis shows that the mean bias, not the budget, then decides whether the multiplier settles, saturates or switches off. And standard GRPO standardizes the penalized reward within each group, which cancels the multiplier before it reaches the gradient. Two-channel constrained GRPO repairs both: the base reward keeps the raw score for the within-group comparison, the penalty and the constraint estimate pass through a monotone map fitted on judge-labeled training responses, and the advantage keeps the multiplier. On a Qwen3-VL-2B policy with a training-free readout of an open 4B model as the scorer, held-out harm on 1,037 adversarial prompts falls from 8.9% under standard GRPO to 4.8%, lower in every seed, where the uncalibrated budget gives 7.6%; over-refusal stays at most 2% on 400 benign queries and MMBench is unchanged. The gain is largest where the intent sits in the image (10.1% -> 5.1%) and grows on the unseen HADES benchmark (28.9% -> 12.8%). Order-preserving distortions of the scores reproduce each predicted controller state in every seed, and the results hold under a map fitted on another judge's labels, an image-reading judge and a human audit.

### [Label-Efficient Document-Grounded Factuality Detection via Cross-Paraphrastic Invariance Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16264)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.16264

> Substantially relevant to document-grounded factuality detection, applicable for RAG evaluation.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large language models (LLMs) can generate fluent claims that are unsupported by a given source document, motivating accurate and efficient document-grounded factuality detection. Existing LLM-based evaluators often require repeated inference and incur high computational cost, whereas compact factuality detectors are efficient at inference time but commonly rely on large labeled or synthetically generated corpora. We propose CPIL (Cross-Paraphrastic Invariance Learning), a two-stage Siamese framework that converts a small labeled set into relational supervision without acquiring additional factuality labels. CPIL constructs positive pairs from distinct label-preserving paraphrastic views of the same document-claim instance and negative pairs from opposite-label examples associated with the same source document. It further introduces quantile-aware semi-hard negative mining, which derives a boundary from the positive-pair similarity distribution and balances informative negatives with broader coverage. In the two-stage training procedure, Stage 1 learns an encoder from these task-aware pairs, and Stage 2 jointly fine-tunes the encoder and a linear classification head for binary factuality prediction. At inference time, CPIL requires no paraphrase generation, pair construction, or Siamese comparison and uses only one encoder-classifier forward pass. On the 11-task LLM-AggreFact benchmark, CPIL-2% and CPIL-3% achieve macro-F1 scores of 81.04 and 81.60 while using only 2-3% of the FactCG-released labeled pool. Both settings outperform all compared compact detectors and two vanilla LLM references. Ablations and sensitivity analyses support the contributions of task-aware pair construction, dynamic negative mining, and multi-pivot paraphrasing.

### [Fine-Grained Generative Hallucination Detection and Correction for Aerial Image Captioning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15809)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.15809

> Addresses hallucination detection in models, relevant for ensuring grounded answers in chat systems.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Aerial imagery captured by unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) plays a vital role in applications such as surveillance, disaster response, and large-scale environmental monitoring. Automatic generation of textual descriptions for such imagery is essential for reducing manual inspection overhead and enabling better downstream decision-making. However, Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), despite their impressive captioning capabilities, are prone to hallucination, producing descriptions that are inconsistent with the visual input. This problem is significantly amplified in aerial imagery due to its unique challenges such as extreme viewpoint variation, small objects, class imbalance, and ambiguous visual entities. Existing hallucination detection approaches either rely on computationally expensive large external models such as GPT-4, which perform poorly on aerial domains, or treat the problem as a rigid binary classification task, ignoring the inherent uncertainty in ambiguous cases. To address these limitations, we propose a lightweight, reference-free framework for hallucination detection and correction in free-form aerial image captioning. Our method leverages state-of-the-art MLLMs, specifically InternVL3 and Qwen2.5-VL, to detect hallucinated content in generated captions with minimal computational overhead. Detected hallucinations are corrected through a dedicated refinement module that improves the factual consistency of the original captions. Extensive experiments on the Labelled Illusion Dataset (LID) demonstrate that our framework significantly reduces hallucination rates while remaining practical for real-time deployment in resource-constrained environments. Code is available at: https://github.com/Debolena7/Hal_Detect.

### [Can Agents Relive Past Experiences? Diagnosing Event-Affect Decoupling in Affective-Episodic Retrospection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15655)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.15655

> It addresses long-term memory and context management for agents, relevant to conversational memory in chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

As memory-augmented agents increasingly support long-term and personalized interactions, reliable recall of emotional experiences while preserving their associated affect becomes necessary. However, existing evaluations usually assess emotion accuracy and factual memory grounding separately, overlooking event-affect correspondence. In this work, we formulate Affective-Episodic Retrospection (AER) as a task that explicitly evaluates the recovery of past events, associated affects, and their correspondence, and define Event-Affect Decoupling as a failure mode where the correct emotion is recalled but associated with the wrong episode. To diagnose Event-Affect Decoupling, we propose EM-TRACE, a stage-wise framework that decomposes AER into retrieval, affect recovery, and realization. On a controlled GoEmotions-based benchmark, Emotion-only retrieval achieves 28.65% affect recovery but retrieves the target episode for only 4.44% of queries, yielding a UAR of 24.21%. Further analysis reveals that event retrieval and affect assignment introduce distinct binding failures. These findings show that reliable AER requires grounded recall of emotional experiences, where episode retrieval, affect recovery, event-affect binding, and generation quality should be evaluated jointly. Our code is available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/A412/.

### [AntEmbed: Dual-Mode Multimodal Embedding Model for Efficient and Fine-Grained Retrieval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16405)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.16405

> Discusses a dual-mode embedding model for retrieval, applicable to RAG frameworks and relevant to vector search.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Discriminative embeddings encode original multimodal inputs efficiently but struggle with fine-grained semantics. Generative embeddings use reasoning to capture query intent and relevance evidence, but incur higher computational costs. We propose AntEmbed, a dual-mode multimodal embedding model that supports efficient retrieval and reasoning-enhanced fine-grained matching in a unified embedding space. First, we construct retrieval-oriented reasoning content that captures query intent and confusion-aware semantic evidence. We treat original and reasoning-enhanced candidate inputs as semantically equivalent views, and use multi-positive contrastive learning with a single embedding token to align discriminative and generative embeddings. Second, cross-mode self-distillation transfers fine-grained semantic information from reasoning-enhanced to original representations through candidate similarity-distribution distillation and query representation alignment. This strengthens discriminative embeddings without requiring additional online reasoning. Both retrieval modes share a single candidate index. On MMEB-v2, AntEmbed-8B achieves an overall score of 81.5 with reasoning-enhanced embeddings, outperforming the strongest baseline by 3.1 points.

### [REWEAVE: Online Learning for Workload-Adaptive HNSW Indexes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16060)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.16060

> Presents a method for adapting HNSW indexes which is relevant for improving retrieval quality in RAG systems.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Vector databases rely on Hierarchical Navigable Small World (HNSW) indexes for similarity search, but a graph built from data alone can be poorly matched to a skewed or changing query workload. We present REWEAVE (Reward-Guided Rewiring of Vector Graphs), an online index-maintenance method that learns edge utility from query feedback and periodically rewires the bottom layer within the original per-node degree cap. The method supports sparse exact-neighbor feedback combined with self-labels, and standard HNSW serving without learning overhead once adaptation is disabled. Under explicit assumptions we prove value-estimation and graph-stabilization guarantees. Under stationary skew, REWEAVE improves held-out recall by up to 23.3 percentage points over HNSW. In comparisons with query-aware baselines on up to 100M vectors, it achieves up to 3.3x NGFix's query throughput at recall@100 of 0.80. On Amazon-ESCI with query drift, it increases stream recall@100 from 0.745 to 0.979 with 34% fewer search distance computations than frozen HNSW. These results illustrate the potential to improve retrieval quality and reduce search effort within an existing graph-degree budget, while incurring additional work during adaptation.

</details>
