### [Hallucination-Aware Multi-Agent RAG via Internal Attention Rewards](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35056)
**Score 10/10** · `cs.IR` · 2610.35056

> This work is a must-read as it tackles hallucination reduction in RAG frameworks, which is crucial for maintaining answer faithfulness.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) grounds language models in retrieved evidence, but its outputs often contain claims unsupported by that evidence. Recent multi-agent RAG systems decompose the pipeline into cooperative decisions, yet they typically optimize all agents under a shared task reward such as EM or F1, which measures answer overlap rather than faithfulness to evidence. We propose a hallucination-aware multi-agent RAG framework that combines the task reward with an internal hallucination-risk signal computed from the generator's attention-head norms. Three role-specific LoRA adapters, a query planner, an evidence selector, and an answer generator, share a frozen backbone. A lightweight critic is trained once on TruthfulQA truthful/false pairs and then kept frozen, providing a complementary risk score that is added to the shared reward during cooperative PPO training. Across three backbones (Qwen3-8B, Llama3-8B, Llama2-13B) and three benchmarks (RAGTruth, HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA), our method improves task performance and reduces hallucination rates over the strongest multi-agent baseline. Ablations and critic diagnostics show that the TruthfulQA-calibrated signal provides useful reward shaping that transfers across RAG tasks.

### [TriageRAG: Failure-Aware Self-Training for Adapting Retrieval-Augmented Language Models to Specialized Domains](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33776)
**Score 10/10** · `cs.IR` · 2610.33776

> Directly focuses on adapting retrieval-augmented language models to specialized domains, very relevant.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) lets large language models (LLMs) answer knowledge-intensive questions from retrieved evidence, but adapting a general-purpose RAG system to a specialized domain such as medicine is difficult when only an unlabeled corpus is available. Self-training approaches such as SimRAG address this by letting the LLM write its own question-answer pairs from domain passages and fine-tuning on the pairs that survive a round-trip consistency filter. We observe that these approaches treat every retained example identically: they never ask whether the student already knows the answer, already extracts it correctly from the retrieved context, or fails - and if so, whether the failure lies with the reader or with the retriever. To tackle this, we propose TriageRAG, a failure-aware self-training approach that probes the student on each self-generated question with and without retrieved context, triages it into one of four categories (known, solved, context-failure, retrieval-failure), and routes each category to the training signal that addresses it, including a parametric route that teaches the reader to answer when retrieval misses. Probing is cheap and repeated every round, so the training mixture follows the student, and the triage histogram doubles as a label-free diagnostic of where the domain gap lies. Experiments on 11 datasets spanning three domains with a Qwen3-4B backbone, and on CS-Bench with Qwen3-8B, demonstrate that TriageRAG outperforms a budget-matched SimRAG by 1.2-6.4 points and is the only method that recovers the closed-book level in every case where retrieval hurts the base model. A single ablation identifies the mechanism: round-trip filtering removes every training context that lacks the evidence, and a reader that never sees such contexts learns to over-trust noisy retrieval.

### [SEMA-Auto: Sighted and Efficient Multi-Turn Attack with Autonomy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35743)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.35743

> Relates directly to guardrails and jailbreak detection in production systems.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Existing jailbreak attackers fall into two classes: static planners fix their single- or multi-turn adversarial plans in advance, remaining simple but losing sight of the victim's replies, wasting turns and limiting transferability; interactive generators read each victim reply to generate the next-turn attack but require in-loop evaluators for success signals or hints, incurring substantial costs and inflated performance. Bridging this gap, we propose SEMA-Auto, a sighted and efficient multi-turn attacker with autonomy, interacting with the victim model and terminating the session by itself upon success. To stabilize its behaviors and boost its capabilities, SEMA-Auto is trained in two stages. Synthetic bootstrap tuning installs the notion of attack targets and conforming decisions by fine-tuning the base model with bootstrapped trajectories. Decision-supervised reinforcement learning then optimizes two objectives jointly yet separately, reinforcing the attack with higher jailbreak reward while supervising the decision at each turn against an absolute target. Our method achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) attack success rates (ASR) across multiple datasets, victims, and judges, outperforming all static and interactive baselines, within almost the fewest turns, demonstrating both effectiveness and efficiency. For example, SEMA-Auto performs a $76.0\%$ ASR@1 within an average of $3.0$ turns against DeepSeek-V4-Flash on AdvBench, while the best static baseline is $54.8\%$ with fixed $7$ turns and the best interactive baseline is $43.8\%$ in $3.7$ turns. Against safety-hardened frontier models, GPT-oss-120B, GPT-5.4, Claude-Haiku-4.5, and GPT-6-Astra, our counter-trained attacker achieves an average $59.6\%$ ASR@1 on HarmBench, $13.8\%$ over prior SOTA. Our code is available at: https://github.com/microsoft/SEMA

### [CalibraRL: Confidence-Calibrated Reinforcement Learning for Mitigating Hallucinations in RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35142)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.35142

> CalibraRL provides a reinforcement learning framework to mitigate hallucinations in RAG, directly addressing quality and evaluation concerns.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large language models (LLMs) often produce plausible but incorrect answers, motivating reinforcement learning methods that reward correct answers, penalize incorrect answers, and allow abstention. However, fixed outcome-based rewards do not distinguish incorrect responses with different confidence levels or account for differences in answering reliability across queries. Consequently, such rewards may inadequately penalize overconfident errors and fail to discourage unnecessary abstention. We propose CalibraRL, a reinforcement learning framework that incorporates response confidence and query-level answering evidence into reward design. For non-abstaining responses, CalibraRL combines correctness-based utility with a Brier-based penalty that encourages agreement between confidence and correctness. For abstentions, it estimates conditional answering reliability using a Beta posterior derived from the correctness of non-abstaining responses within each sampled group. The abstention reward increases with the posterior probability that the model's accuracy on non-abstaining responses is below a predefined threshold. CalibraRL reuses training rollouts without requiring additional annotations indicating whether the model knows the answer to each question. Experiments on three QA datasets demonstrate that CalibraRL improves answer precision and reduces hallucinations compared with strong baselines. Further analysis shows that CalibraRL reduces mean expected calibration error (ECE) by 30.8\% across two backbone models and three datasets, highlighting its potential to improve the reliability of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) in web search.

</details>

### [Reinforcement Learning Done Right for Explicit Confidence Targets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35059)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.35059

> Proposes a novel reinforcement learning approach with explicit confidence targets, directly relevant to RAG evaluation.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large language models (LLMs) can produce plausible but incorrect answers when they are uncertain, which can mislead users and undermine model reliability. Training models to abstain with reinforcement learning is a natural approach, but most existing methods use a fixed error penalty and therefore cannot adapt their behavior to different confidence requirements at inference time. We study \emph{explicit confidence targets}, where a user specifies a target \(p\) in the prompt and the model directly chooses whether to answer or abstain, with the goal of achieving an accuracy of at least \(p\) among answered questions. However, learning to respond to different confidence targets with a single model has proven difficult, and prior attempts have failed to distinguish different targets. We show that this failure comes from two problems: the reward signal that distinguishes different values of $p$ is often lost during optimization, while the original error penalty grows without bound as $p$ approaches one. We propose RLEC (\textbf{R}einforcement \textbf{L}earning with \textbf{E}xplicit \textbf{C}onfidence Targets), which preserves the learning signal needed to distinguish confidence targets, introduces a stable reward family that keeps the desired answer condition unchanged, and uses reward scheduling to balance effective learning and training stability. Together, these designs enable the first successful training of a single LLM for explicit confidence target control. Extensive experiments across diverse benchmarks and model scales show that RLEC largely preserves general capabilities and achieves competitive calibration performance. RLEC also supports fine-grained target control without an external decision system or threshold calibration after training. By making explicit confidence targets part of the trained policy, RLEC opens a new and more direct path toward behavioral calibration.

</details>

### [Adaptive Retrieval, Selective Answers: Evaluating Conformal Factuality in Agentic RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34872)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.34872

> It directly addresses evaluation and adaptation in RAG, crucial for ensuring accuracy in model responses.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) can improve factual grounding without guaranteeing that every generated claim is supported. Previous work has studied claim-level conformal factuality in single-stage RAG and, more recently, multi-hop RAG. We examine its behavior in agentic RAG, where a language-model policy adaptively issues follow-up retrieval queries or terminates retrieval before response generation. We retain the retrieval-based relevance-scoring formulation and post-generation conformal filtering and evaluate six model-dataset configurations: GPT-6 Luna and Llama 3.1 8B on HotpotQA, Natural Questions, and TriviaQA. Each configuration uses 60 fixed questions, 100 seeded 30/30 calibration-test partitions, five conformal target levels, and a no-conformal-prediction baseline. Across the six configurations, higher target levels are accompanied by higher observed full-support rates and reduced answer availability. At the 95% target, non-empty response coverage ranges from 17.23% to 60.17%, claim retention from 10.72% to 43.16%, and full support among non-empty outputs from 89.14% to 95.56%. We formulate the adaptive retrieval trajectory explicitly and characterize the reliability-informativeness trade-off when conformal factuality is applied after agent termination.

### [Agent Memory as Recommendation: Recommender-System Design for Long-Term Memory of LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34673)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.34673

> Directly addresses long-term memory for LLM agents, crucial for enhancing RAG chat context.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large language model (LLM) agents that serve users across many sessions must choose, for every request, the few stored memories that enter a limited context window. This choice largely decides whether the agent answers correctly, and it runs on every request at Web scale. This selection faces three challenges: a large and growing store, a per-request cost budget, and multi-session questions that need all of their evidence. Many memory systems build notes or graphs with hundreds to thousands of LLM calls per history, and each is designed on its own, so it is unclear which component yields the accuracy. These challenges mirror the properties of recommendation, so we formalize agent memory as budgeted slate recommendation, with the request as the user, stored memories as items and the context window as a slate. The formalization places memory systems in one design space of recommender cascades and factorizes accuracy into the completeness that each stage controls. Guided by it, we propose AgentMemRec, which adapts candidate generation, learning to rank and slate construction from recommender systems and selects memories without any LLM call. On multi-session questions of LongMemEval, AgentMemRec outperforms memory and context-selection methods, including A-Mem, HippoRAG 2, DynamicRAG and T-Mem, by 10 to 31 points of accuracy. It matches an LLM reranker that makes 64 calls per query at 3.2 times lower serving cost, and on LoCoMo it outperforms Mem0 and Zep without any LLM call at write time. The factorization attributes the gains to slates that more often hold all of the evidence, so recommender-system design offers an effective and efficient foundation for the long-term memory of LLM agents.

### [Read the Route: Attention-Steered Hypergraph Retrieval for Multi-hop RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35412)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2610.35412

> Focuses on multi-hop retrieval methods essential for RAG, making it highly relevant.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Graph-based retrieval-augmented generation (GraphRAG) answers multi-hop questions by organizing a corpus into a graph and retrieving along its structure. Existing retrieval methods face two limitations. Diffusion- and similarity-based methods expand retrieval along fixed edge weights or by similarity to the question, without resolving its implicit entity references, allowing relevance errors to accumulate across hops. In contrast, exploration-based methods let an LLM reason about the next hop, but require a separate generation at each hop, leading to high inference costs. We observe that an LLM can resolve implicit entity references from retrieved passages and a multi-hop question before generating any tokens. Further analysis identifies a small set of attention heads, termed addressing heads, that capture this capability across multiple hops in a single forward pass. Building on this finding, we propose HyperAddress, which runs Personalized PageRank on a description-labelled hypergraph, using addressing heads to determine the restart distribution and transition weights, thereby guiding multi-hop retrieval without any generation at query time. On MuSiQue, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and HotpotQA, HyperAddress outperforms the strongest baseline by 3.5 points of Recall@5 and 7.4 points of All@5 on average, with gains that grow with the number of hops. It answers more than twice as fast and builds its index at half the cost.

### [Holo-NN: Holographic Nearest Neighbor Search via Compressive Sensing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33224)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2610.33224

> Relevant to privacy-preserving vector search and ANN, crucial for RAG implementations.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

In the age of AI, privacy-preserving approximate nearest neighbor (ANN) search over high-dimensional embeddings has emerged as a critical yet frustratingly challenging domain, dominated by tradeoffs between speed, privacy, and accuracy. Traditional tree and graph-based indexing structures (e.g., HNSW, ScaNN) rely fundamentally on non-linear operations that are computationally intractable under Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) or blind server-side constraints. In this paper, we introduce Holo-NN (Holographic Nearest Neighbor Search), an algebraic framework that replaces data-dependent branching with a single linear projection. Our key observation is that while distances across a database are dense, neighborhood membership is inherently sparse: only a handful of points ever lie close to a query. By pairing locality-preserving feature transforms with Compressive Sensing, Holo-NN folds the entire database offline into a compact holographic sensing matrix. At query time, encrypted search reduces to a single, non-interactive matrix-vector multiplication---requiring no FHE bootstrapping, no multi-round interaction, and no client-side index state. This turns private retrieval into an embarrassingly parallel, accuracy-tunable computation that runs out of the box on both CPUs and GPUs, scaling efficiently to production databases. We offer a comparison against other state-of-the-art Private ANN algorithms to help situate the method in the broader context of production-grade private retrieval.

By aligning conceptual motivations, computational memory mechanisms, benchmarks, and deployment concerns, the survey provides a structured roadmap for designing memory-augmented LLM agents that are persistent, adaptive, auditable, and safe to update over time.

### [PinGER: Token-Efficient Generative Retrieval with Semantic IDs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33435)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.IR` · 2610.33435

> Discusses generative retrieval which pertains to building a RAG system.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Generative retrieval replaces nearest-neighbor search with token-by-token decoding of an item's semantic ID (SID). But existing SID-based methods primarily use L levels of tokens sequentially to represent each item in the input history. As a result, a fixed context window holds L times fewer items than it would with one embedding per item. We present PinGER, a generative retriever deployed at Pinterest that retains SID outputs while making input histories compact, built on three ideas. (1) An asymmetric attention mask over interleaved SID and dense tokens lets later items attend only to the dense ones, so the model is supervised at every SID position in training yet serves one token per item while computing the same function. (2) Generation is controllable through goal-conditioned outputs, reinforcement learning, and a prefix-diversity penalty, which together steer retrieval toward surface-specific goals and diverse candidates. (3) A serving stack built for efficient wide-beam generation is paired with decoding constrained to valid identifiers and candidate resolution from a host-local index. Deployed in Related Pins, PinGER lifts saves of fresh Pins by 3.60% and overall saves by 0.62%, while reducing the surface's end-to-end latency by 0.7% and eliminating the cost of the nearest-neighbor search it replaces.
