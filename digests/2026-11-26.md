### [DRBench Pro: A Knowledge-Centric Benchmark for Professional Deep Research Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34852)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.34852

> Offers a knowledge-centric evaluation benchmark relevant for assessing the quality of RAG outputs.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Deep research agents gather information from multiple sources and synthesize it into reports on complex, open-ended questions. Existing benchmarks assess report quality along several dimensions, but lack a dedicated knowledge-centric evaluation of which research directions a report covers and how deeply it explores them. We introduce DRBench Pro, a benchmark that makes structured knowledge coverage the primary evaluation target. Our automated pipeline builds local knowledge trees from Wikipedia and uses sampled subgraphs to generate research queries and evidence-grounded rubrics. Each rubric corresponds to a relation in the tree, preserving the structure of the knowledge being evaluated. Agents receive only the research query. This structure allows us to measure breadth across major research directions and depth along related knowledge chains, with their geometric mean giving overall coverage. The benchmark includes 144 cases across 24 domains and 44,102 rubrics. Evaluating 16 deep research agents reveals different strengths in breadth and depth, with the best-performing agent reaching 57.04% overall coverage. Rubric judgments agree closely with human annotations, and agent rankings remain stable across the tested metric configurations.

### [Mitigating Hallucinations in Medical Question Answering on ESC Cardiology Guidelines: Evaluation and Retrieval Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34427)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.34427

> Addresses hallucination detection in medical QA, directly relevant to RAG evaluation.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Background: Large language models produce hallucinations in medical question answering (QA). Verify-and-revise pipelines that check atomic claims against retrieved guideline evidence are a standard mitigation, but evaluations almost universally treat the verifier's output as ground truth, an assumption rarely tested. Objective: To measure, claim by claim against human judgement, how such a pipeline fails in a guideline-grounded clinical setting. Methods: We built a dataset of 160 questions (100 patient vignettes from 89 de-identified cases, 60 fact-based) over four recent ESC cardiology guidelines, all approved by a practising internist, and a retrieval corpus of 627 hand-verified guideline records at two chunk granularities. A five-stage pipeline was run under four retrieval configurations and three independent runs (1920 arm-runs); all 606 first-pass refuted claims were manually audited, an independent judge re-judged 906 decisions, and the expert blindly reviewed 100 accepted claims. Results: Only 44.7-50.6% of flagged claims were true hallucinations. Correction loops converged in 85.2-95.1% of cases, yet 35% of genuine final errors were introduced by correction. Single-run true-hallucination recall was 35.6-41.2%, the three-run union 71-80%; verifier agreement was moderate (kappa = 0.505). The blind probe estimated the accepted-claim miss rate at 3.2% (95% CI 0.7-6.7%). Final genuine-error rate: 1.7% of answers. Conclusion: Claim-level verification reduces hallucinated content by an order of magnitude, but verifier reliability, not generation, is the limiting factor and must be measured, not assumed.

### [When Rerankers Work Together: Better and Faster Reranking without Joint Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33844)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.33844

> Directly addresses reranking in RAG pipelines, making it a must-read for improving answer accuracy.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Standalone reranker quality does not determine its value in a multistage RAG pipeline. We systematically study heterogeneous rerankers composed without joint training, spanning lexical, single-vector, late-interaction, and joint query-context scoring. Two lightweight semantic coarse scorers, Cache-Pointwise and Cache-Shapley, learn from passage relevance labels and share cacheable representations and linear-time singleton inference. Under matched candidates and budgets, we compare cascades with stage-removal references to measure evidence retention, repair and breakage, reader accuracy, and online pruning cost. On NoLiMa 32K with Gemma, inserting these scorers before CrossEncoder or Provence improves answer accuracy by 4.02-9.43 percentage points while accelerating online pruning by 1.57-1.88x relative to the corresponding reranker alone. Cache-Pointwise composed with ColBERTv2 through reciprocal rank fusion improves accuracy by 4.60 points at 1.36x speedup over direct ColBERTv2. The stage-removal comparisons reveal an asymmetry: a cascade can outperform its final reranker alone while underperforming its coarse selector alone. Two readers, a second multi-hop dataset, and three-stage extensions show that the gains depend on the reader, reranker pairing, and stage configuration. These findings demonstrate a practical route to better and faster reranking without joint training and motivate studying stage order, candidate budgets, and rank fusion as determinants of composition quality.

</details>

### [CA-RAG: Constraint-Aligned Retrieval for Structurally Faithful Long-Form Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33497)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.33497

> Directly addresses Constraint-Aligned Retrieval in RAG for compliance-sensitive generation.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) typically optimises evidence selection for semantic relevance, whereas compliance-sensitive long-form generation also requires evidence supporting explicit structural obligations. Semantically relevant retrieval may therefore omit information needed for mandatory downstream clauses. We propose Constraint-Aligned RAG (CA-RAG), a retrieval-conditioning framework that augments section-specific queries with ontology-derived structural constraints and policy-grounded knowledge-graph relations before evidence selection. Retrieval-only evaluation with manually annotated evidence shows consistent improvements across retrieval budgets. At $k=6$, CA-RAG increases Precision@$k$ from $0.5268$ to $0.5692$, pooled Recall@$k$ from $0.5402$ to $0.5950$, and F1@$k$ from $0.5389$ to $0.5672$ over standard RAG. Generation experiments further show improvements in compliance and reliability in several model--domain settings, while generation-only constraints achieve higher clause coverage but lower overall compliance and reliability. These results indicate that structurally aligned retrieval can improve evidence sufficiency in compliance-sensitive long-form generation.

</details>

### [ConTra: A Training Framework for Effective Contextual Retrieval via Prefix Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34366)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.34366

> The framework addresses contextual retrieval and chunking strategies, closely aligned with document chunking and RAG evaluation metrics.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Data management systems organize large text collections for tasks such as finding relevant studies and extracting contract clauses. Systems make long documents searchable by splitting them into independently indexed chunks. Chunking can separate text from its identifying details. The indexing challenge is to keep each chunk independently searchable while preserving the context needed to find and rank it. Existing methods either encode that context in chunk vectors or attach it as a text prefix. Context encoded only in vectors is unavailable to text rerankers. Prompted prefixes serve both vector search and reranking, but are costly to generate at indexing time and can underperform contextual encoders. We propose ConTra (Context That Travels), a reinforcement learning framework for training lightweight prefix generators. Its reward combines feedback from frozen embedding and reranking models with factual verification and length control. ConTra generates one prefix per chunk during index construction and stores the augmented text and its embedding for reuse across queries. To evaluate contextual retrieval across workloads and under interference, we introduce RealContextBench, a benchmark with five application collections and three controlled conditions that isolate the effect of distracting text. On ConTEB and RealContextBench, ConTra-2B outperforms the prompted 9B baseline using Anthropic's method and the PPLX-4B contextual encoder, with a margin of up to 3.15 points over their best overall scores, while indexing short documents 7.74 times and 2.15 times as fast as those baselines, respectively. Post-trained only on ConTEB, it leads the standalone methods on RealContextBench after reranking and is less sensitive than the prompted 2B baseline to random and topically similar distractors. Code, datasets, and trained models are available at https://github.com/SStan1/ConTra.

</details>

### [Privacy-Aware Adaptive Client Selection for Federated Retrieval-Augmented Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33890)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.33890

> This paper directly addresses privacy-aware approaches in federated RAG, key for ensuring both retrieval efficiency and privacy.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Federated retrieval-augmented generation retrieves evidence from distributed corpora. However, broadcasting queries to all clients increases the retrieval burden and query exposure. We present Privacy-Aware Adaptive Client Selection for Federated Retrieval-Augmented Generation (PACS-FedRAG), an online selector that takes into account perturbed semantic profiles along with evidence feedback, latency, payload cost, and exploration. In a ten-client setting where three clients are chosen, the archived end-to-end benchmark reports an evidence overlap of 0.372 and an answer accuracy of 0.287. The retrieval-only evaluation, on the other hand, reuses a full-broadcast baseline with a different fusion tie-breaking policy. Rank fusion across disjoint client corpora imposes an overlap ceiling of 0.375, limiting its interpretation as a relevance measure. Answer accuracy is below the fixed-label diagnostic of 0.645, and raw generator responses are unavailable for parser verification. Corrected baselines and a new answer-level evaluation are needed to assess whether quality is preserved. Selecting three clients reduces the number of query recipients by 70%. Profile perturbation does not provide confidentiality of queries or passages.

</details>

### [DocDUR-Net: Degradation-Aware Unified Document Image Restoration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34860)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CV` · 2611.34860

> Directly relevant to document processing, especially for RAG applications and image restoration.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Captured document images often suffer from diverse degradations caused by uncontrolled imaging conditions, which reduce readability and obscure fine text structures. Single-degradation restoration methods are usually trained and deployed for individual degradation types, and therefore require model selection before restoration. Unified restoration models alleviate the need to maintain multiple restoration networks by sharing one network across tasks, but representative unified document image restoration methods still depend on external task selection. This dependency limits automatic document image restoration. We propose DocDUR-Net, a degradation-aware unified restoration network that derives restoration guidance directly from the input image. It introduces a Degradation Perception Module (DPM) to extract degradation representations and predict degradation weights, and Degradation Prior-guided Adaptive Feature Fusion (DP-AFF) to combine learnable degradation priors with degradation features extracted from the input. The resulting guidance is injected into a shared restoration backbone, enabling adaptive restoration without external task selection. DocDUR-Net also supports a manual mode with explicit task selection. Experiments on document image restoration benchmarks show that DocDUR-Net achieves the best average PSNR of 29.405 dB and SSIM of 0.960 among unified methods while remaining competitive with single-degradation methods.

</details>

### [SEAM: A Self-Evolving and Adaptive Probing Framework for Automatic MLLM Hallucination Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34598)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CV` · 2611.34598

> Directly addresses hallucination evaluation in multimodal large language models, highly relevant for RAG systems.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The severe susceptibility of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to hallucinations poses a critical threat to their reliability, making a rigorous evaluation framework indispensable. However, existing evaluation pipelines largely rely on predefined test items, suffering from high construction costs, rapid obsolescence as MLLMs evolve, and ineffectiveness in probing model-specific weaknesses. To address these limitations, we propose SEAM, a self-evolving and adaptive probing framework for automatic MLLM hallucination evaluation. Specifically, SEAM introduces a composable Hallucination Probe Construction Library comprising ten hallucination-probing skills, three elicitation wrappers, and five question formats. Building on this library, SEAM establishes a dual-loop framework. In the inner loop (adaptive probing), skill-guided visual analysis, question composition, and hierarchical quality verification are coordinated with verifier and target-response feedback to refine test items and uncover model-specific hallucinations. In the outer loop (skill evolution), a skill updater distills historical probing trajectories into localized, reusable rule updates, which undergo held-out validation and are frozen before testing. Driven by these innovative structural designs, SEAM establishes an automated and self-evolving evaluation paradigm. Experiments across four target MLLMs and ten hallucination types demonstrate that SEAM substantially outperforms representative static benchmarks, achieving an 89.2\% relative improvement in mean valid hallucination discovery rate across 12 matched comparisons.

</details>

### [VulnSafe: Measuring Vulnerability-Aware Relational Safety in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34819)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.34819

> Focuses on relational safety in LLMs, relevant for implementing safe and effective chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Emotionally supportive interactions require large language models not only to avoid overtly harmful content, but also to recognize vulnerability-related risk, regulate their relational stance, preserve user agency, and provide context-appropriate support. Existing safety benchmarks often measure isolated violations, leaving unclear what underlying response competence their scores represent. We introduce VulnSafe, a Chinese capability assessment centered on Vulnerability-Aware Response Competence (VARC). VARC organizes relational safety into five complementary constructs: Risk Perception, Safety Preservation, Autonomy Protection, Supportive Response, and Boundary Management. Following an evidence-centered design, 41 scenarios elicit construct-relevant behavior through 2,050 balanced prompts and scenario-specific scoring rubrics. The assessment further separates proactive should-do behavior from prohibitive should-not behavior. Across eight representative LLMs, results show distinct competence profiles and larger disparities than prohibitive requirements. VulnSafe therefore makes relational safety interpretable as a multidimensional model capability rather than as refusal compliance alone.

</details>

### [DocSEAM: Bidirectional Cross-Page Encoding for Multi-Page Document Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34420)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.34420

> Focuses on multi-page document understanding, applicable to document ingestion for RAG.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Multi-page document vision-language models encode each page independently, creating a feature-level discontinuity at page boundaries: visual tokens are formed without access to content on adjacent pages, and the language model must reason over these page representations formed in isolation. We introduce DocSEAM (Document Selective Enrichment via Anchored Masking), a lightweight module inserted between a pretrained vision encoder and the language model that injects the inductive bias of local cross-page context into visual representations. For each page, DocSEAM forms a local triplet with its two neighbors, applies center-anchored masked self-attention with directional salience gates to fuse information from neighbor pages, and returns only the enriched center-page tokens, leaving the visual token count unchanged. After a supervised cross-page warm-up and multi-page fine-tuning with DocSEAM frozen, our 9B-parameter model achieves state-of-the-art results among the evaluated open-weight models of up to 10B parameters on five multi-page document VQA benchmarks. We further show that under controlled re-pagination, DocSEAM reduces sensitivity to boundary placement and improves cross-page span localization

### [MINE: A Training-Free Defense against Indirect Prompt Injection via Mutual Information and Embedding Variance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34188)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.34188

> Directly addresses defenses against prompt injection in RAG applications, making it quite relevant.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Existing defenses against indirect prompt injection in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) are inherently reactive: prompt-engineering methods are often overridden by the injected instructions, while supervised detectors must be retrained for each new attack style. We propose a different paradigm: defend by maximizing relevance, not by detecting attacks. Our method, MINE, is training-free and attack-agnostic, and it also serves as a query-conditioned reranker. To evaluate defenses under realistic conditions, we introduce LC-Inj-SQuAD, a long-context benchmark with an average of 4,032 words per context. We evaluate on four datasets spanning short-context QA (Inj-SQuAD, Inj-TriviaQA), long-context QA (LC-Inj-SQuAD), and agent tool-call injection (InjecAgent). Across all four, MINE achieves the best accuracy--security trade-off among training-free defenses. It is also the only defense that stays usable against a fully adaptive attacker, generalizes to agent tool-call injection, and achieves the lowest inference latency on long contexts.

</details>

### [From Data Room to Report: End-to-End AI Support for M&A Legal Due Diligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34120)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.34120

> Relevant to document ingestion and extraction in legal due diligence, useful for RAG applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Legal due diligence in mergers and acquisitions requires lawyers to organize heterogeneous virtual data room documents, extract transaction-relevant information and consolidate reviewed findings into a due diligence report. We present LEXI (Legal Extraction and Intelligence), a work-in-progress system that demonstrates how large language models can support this process from document ingestion to report preparation. LEXI classifies documents under a configurable due diligence taxonomy, assigns relevant target entities, extracts predefined information with supporting passages and page-level provenance and prepares report content from reviewed findings. Structured outputs connect these stages and allow model-generated results to be passed through the workflow in a controlled form. Lawyers review and correct classifications and extractions and retain responsibility for legal assessments, recommendations, risk ratings and final report wording. Initial evaluations of the classification and extraction components provide preliminary evidence that the approach is feasible. LEXI illustrates how AI can be integrated across the legal due diligence workflow while preserving lawyer control over substantive legal judgment.

### [State Space Attention: Learning Context-Dependent Memory Retention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33625)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.33625

> Addresses long-context reasoning and memory retention, applicable for conversational memory in chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Standard attention's key-value cache grows linearly with context length. Bounding it raises the question of what a sequence model should retain for long-context reasoning. We argue this choice should be learned, and introduce State Space Attention (SSA), which learns to keep a bounded set of past representations and reads them with standard attention. A learned scorer rescores every chunk against newly arriving context and the rest of memory. A fixed-context Transformer is then the special case that always keeps the most recent tokens. We train the scorer and reader jointly, either moving gradually from a differentiable soft weighting of memories to hard retention, or with hard retention from the start. To produce signal for the scorer, we generate supervised samples during training, a technique we call recovery. We compare SSA with recurrent models, bounded-memory attention methods, and full-attention Transformers across retrieval, variable tracking, multi-hop reasoning, and language modeling. On our retrieval and reasoning benchmarks, SSA matches or comes within a few points of full attention on most tasks, far outperforms a Transformer with the same readable budget on every long-context retrieval task we evaluate, and matches or outperforms one with about 4 times that budget, while using constant inference memory. The largest remaining gap to full attention at small language model scale is on RULER. SSA makes a fixed number of tokens useful over a longer context, and since it reads memory with standard attention, we propose that it can be stacked with KV cache eviction methods. We also pretrain and post-train 400M-parameter models and measure how SSA's training and inference costs scale with context length and model size. Together, these experiments examine the capabilities and limitations of learned memory retention for long-context sequence modeling.

</details>

### [When the Knowledge Runs Out: A Part-Level Pilot Evaluation of How Language Models Handle Partially Covered Questions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33428)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.33428

> Evaluates how language models handle insufficient context, relevant for RAG evaluation.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Assistants that answer from supplied reference material often receive material that covers some parts of a user's question and not others. Existing evaluations of insufficient context mostly score one answer per question, and often treat partially sufficient context as simply insufficient. We describe a small, open evaluation that scores each part of a three-part question separately, with a verdict for parts that are answered correctly from the material but carry an added unsupported claim. On 30 cases (five scenarios, six coverage patterns, five samples each) we test claude-haiku-4-5 and llama3.1:8b. Without an explicit instruction to stay within the material, both models fill most gaps from their own knowledge (unsupported-claim rates of 0.71 and 0.90). One added sentence instructing them to answer only from the material brings these rates to 0.00 and 0.06, consistent with prior work. What remains is less visible: answers that strengthen the material, for example turning "many job sites prohibit bots" into a flat "no". We also report on the judge. Three rounds of blind hand labelling show that this added-claim verdict is the hardest to apply, for the judge and the author alike; RAGAS faithfulness is unsuitable for this task because it penalises correct withholding; and a difference between the two models that is clear under one judge disappears when the judge model is replaced. The dataset, code, hand labels and per-run figures are public.

</details>

### [Seeing the Right Context: Grounded On-Policy Distillation for Remote Sensing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34700)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CV` · 2611.34700

> Relevant to spatial contextual understanding which can enhance grounded answers in chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Remote sensing scene understanding requires multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to interpret queried targets in relation to nearby objects and surrounding structures, which serve as spatial references or define the scope of an answer. However, standard answers and bounding boxes often leave these contextual relationships implicit. Thus, we construct GeoContext-5K to make them explicit in 5,394 training pairs for visual question answering (VQA), with spatial cues, visual facts, and supporting regions. GeOPD uses these annotations for on-policy distillation, guiding a frozen teacher with region marks on the full image and a visual fact while preserving surrounding spatial references. It prioritizes teacher corrections that agree with the shift in the student's next token distribution when evidence is restored. A complementary objective weights the contrast between the student's scores on the two views by how much masking lowers the teacher's log probability for the same sampled token. Experiments on five remote sensing VQA benchmarks show that GeOPD outperforms the evaluated open-source MLLMs and matched post-training baselines, improving over the 4B and 9B base models by 5.81 and 2.97 percentage points on average. The dataset and code will be publicly released at https://github.com/YeeCharm/GeOPD.

### [Evidence-Aligned Benchmarking and Learning for Medical Video Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34465)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CV` · 2611.34465

> Evaluates grounding in medical video language models, pertinent to RAG evaluation.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

A medical video language model should answer from the visual evidence its question asks about. We find three places where this breaks down and address each with an approach we call MedEvidU. First, benchmarks reward answers that ignore the video: on one dataset, the position of a clip within its recording predicts the surgical step in 37% of questions, and a constant answer beats every trained model on a standard safety-assessment metric. We build MedVidBench-v2, 34 tasks from 18 surgical and clinical video datasets, in which prompts and metrics are audited with answers given without video, and which tests unseen hospitals and windows cut blindly from whole recordings. Second, common video encoders merge consecutive frames into shared tokens, so a question about one frame is answered from a blend of two. Query-slot inputs give each queried frame its own tokens at almost no extra cost. Third, a fine-tuned model has memorized its training questions, which leaves little to learn from. Label-matched question resampling (LQR) adds unseen questions about the same recordings while keeping the answer distribution fixed, in a final supervised epoch and in reinforcement learning. Together, these raise the grounding IoU of a 4B model from 30.4 to 44.4, above a supervised 27B model, and instrument grounding at an unseen hospital from 33.9 to 72.5. On blindly cut windows, event-presence accuracy improves by 5.5 points and localization F1 by 8.1 points. Project page: https://gaozhongpai.github.io/MedEvidU/

</details>
