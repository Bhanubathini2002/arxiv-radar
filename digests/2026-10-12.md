### [PRIME: Pre-Retrieval Index Modeling Enhancement for Structured Document Retrieval-Real](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13296)
**Score 10/10** · `cs.IR` · 2610.13296

> Presents an index-side optimization framework that is crucial for improving retrieval in RAG applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

High-precision retrieval over external knowledge bases is essential for LLM agents to operate effectively. However, existing Information Retrieval (IR) research primarily optimizes query-side retrievers (e.g., ColBERT, BGE) and generators (e.g., Self-RAG, GraphRAG), while reducing corpus-side representation to chunking. This leaves index-side quality an underexplored lever. We propose Pre-Retrieval Index Modeling & Enhancement (PRIME), an index-side optimization framework built on two complementary tracks: an LLM-driven Semantic Restructuring (SR) track that transforms long documents into structured, information-dense sections, and a deterministic Metadata Synthesis (MS) and Hierarchical Retrieval (HR) track that extracts semantic anchors and heading structure for coarse-to-fine section retrieval. Our evaluations show that deterministic metadata matches structure-aware chunking's retrieval quality while cutting embedding computation by 45.8 $\times$ and index vector count by 22.4 $\times$. On top of these gains, Semantic Restructuring (SR) further improves Answer-F1 by 13.7% in full deployment, with characterized quality-fidelity-cost trade-offs. Across QASPER, BRIGHT, and NFCorpus, we further characterize three qualitative regimes that determine whether index-side optimization helps or degrades retrieval, while showing that these index-side effects are neutralized under a strong reranker.

### [Tracing and Taming Hallucinated Terms via Subsequence-Association Filtering and Breadth-Aware Selection for LLM Query Expansion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13810)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.13810

> Directly addresses hallucination in LLMs and proposes a filtering method relevant to query expansion in RAG.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large language models (LLMs) are now a standard source of query expansion terms but they hallucinate. They inject non existent entities and off-topic terms that add ranking noise and can hurt strong retrievers. Existing remedies such as corpus grounding, mutual verification and preference alignment treat hallucinated terms as an undifferentiated nuisance to be filtered by agreement heuristics, without a mechanistic account of why a given term appears or a principled criterion for how many terms to keep. We propose Subsequence-Association Filtered, breadth-aware query expansion (SAFE-QE), a training-free, retriever agnostic method that draws on the subsequence-association account of hallucination. We score each candidate expansion term by its corpus conditioned cross-context reproducibility, terms that reappear under randomised query contexts yet lack support in the pseudo-relevant set are flagged as products of dominant hallucinatory associations and down-weighted. Drawing on the characterisation of language generation as a trade-off between consistency (no hallucination) and breadth (coverage), we cast term selection as constrained coverage maximisation over the pseudo-relevant set, keeping a set that is approximately broad but consistent and stable across resampling. We also introduce the Hallucinated Expansion Term Rate, a query side faithfulness metric that counts injected expansion terms no relevant document supports. We evaluate our approach on benchmarked TREC and BEIR collections. Our SAFE-QE is statistically indistinguishable from generative and corpus-steered expansion on nDCG@10 while injecting fewer unsupported terms 4.3 per query against 27.8 for Query2doc and 57.5 for HyDE and on a strong learned sparse retriever, where every expansion method regresses. Faithfulness is therefore available at no measurable cost in ranking quality.

</details>

### [Verifiable Retrieval for Legal Reasoning through Structure-driven Knowledge Graph](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13695)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.13695

> Focuses on RAG and structured retrieval strategies for legal documents, highly relevant.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Any automation within reliability-critical contexts such as medicine or law has to be transparent and verifiable. Despite their advantages, current LLM-based systems do not meet these requirements due to their inherent black-box nature. Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) attempts to ground the generative process in the relevant interpretable documents, thereby reducing the risk of AI 'hallucinations' and potentially enabling verification of the initial generation context. However, as the number and size of potential sources increases, naive retrieval approaches tend to perform worse, ignoring critical context elements. In this paper we aim to tackle this problem in the context of German Legal Code. Using structure-driven parsing to create a Knowledge Graph (KG) for the source document navigation and retrieval, we compare several KG-informed chunking and retrieval strategies and evaluate their effect on the accuracy of retrieved citations.

### [Retrieval-Augmented Multimodal Language Model with Heterogeneous Evidence Integration for Clinical Prognosis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13510)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.13510

> Introduces multimodal LLM for clinical contexts, relevant to document retrieval and integration.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Clinical prognosis requires integrating structured measurements, physiological waveforms, longitudinal history, and population context. We introduce PRISM (Population-Retrieved Inference for Structured Multimodal Prognosis), a multimodal language model that combines per-feature numerical tokenization, recency-weighted aggregation of longitudinal ECGs, and a dual-modality population memory bank. Retrieval operates in representation space: the model receives mean-pooled embeddings of similar training-cohort encounters, while no individual retrieved record enters the prompt. On the 1,443-task MDS-ED benchmark, PRISM achieves AUROCs of 86.2% for diagnosis, 93.1% for deterioration, 91.8% for ICU admission, and 92.8% for mortality. These results use a 96-hour pre-arrival window unavailable to reported baselines. With benchmark-window inputs only, PRISM reaches 0.8895 AUROC, compared with 0.8937 for UniPACT and 0.8889 for MDS-ED. A matched-input compact fusion model using the same five evidence streams and frozen encoders reaches 0.7652 AUROC, versus 0.9092 for PRISM. Replacing numerical EHR encoding with text serialization reduces AUROC by 3.66 points; removing pre-arrival history reduces it by 1.97 points; and zeroing retrieval reduces it by 0.99 points. Genuine retrieval outperforms random-neighbor retrieval by 0.97 macro-average points, although a paired task-level sign test does not establish a uniform advantage (p=0.067). On the external MC-MED cohort, supervised fine-tuning reaches 0.8611 AUROC, compared with 0.8227 for locally trained gradient boosting and 0.7841 for source-only PRISM. These results support structure-aware multimodal integration and supervised external adaptation.

### [Test-Time Memory Construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13348)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.13348

> Directly addresses memory construction for LLMs, relevant to conversational memory management.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle to answer questions over long interaction histories, where relevant evidence may be scattered across distant sessions. Existing memory systems typically construct and maintain sophisticated memory representations offline, before user queries are observed, incurring substantial overhead and limiting their ability to adapt memory construction to query-specific information needs. In this paper, we propose T$^2$Mem, a Test-Time Memory construction framework that defers memory construction to inference and progressively builds memory along the reasoning process. Specifically, T$^2$Mem extracts lightweight episode-level metadata offline to capture key events and temporal associations as retrieval cues, while retaining links to the original interactions to facilitate the reconstruction of dependencies across episodes. At test time, the LLM adaptively retrieves and organizes metadata and raw interactions, progressively constructing query-specific memory as reasoning unfolds. Experiments on LoCoMo and LongMemEval show the effectiveness of T$^2$Mem by consistently outperforming strong baselines across different backbone LLMs while substantially reducing the overhead of memory construction. Further analysis demonstrates that deferring memory construction to inference enables the memory construction process to dynamically adapt to the information needs of individual queries. All code is available at https://github.com/OpenBMB/T2Mem.

</details>

### [Beyond the Final Embedding: Retrieving Memories from Token-Level Evidence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13277)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.13277

> Directly addresses memory-augmented retrieval mechanisms, crucial for RAG chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Memory-augmented assistants retrieve evidence from stored conversations and documents to answer queries. Most embedding-based retrievers compare single-vector representations of queries and memories, even when relevance depends on a small part of the content. Such local evidence can be matched to queries using the contextual token representations already computed by the same embedding models. Our method uses sparse token matching to improve retrieval without additional training. Each query token retrieves a small set of matching tokens across candidate memories. The matching scores are aggregated for each memory and combined with whole-memory similarity to rank memories. An optional low-rank query adapter can be trained with supervision while keeping the encoder and stored memory representations fixed. Our method improves retrieval across eight benchmarks and multiple model families without additional training. Query adaptation further increases the gains to 13.3 and 14.3 percentage points in average Recall@10 and NDCG@10 on Qwen3-Embedding-4B. These retrieval gains translate into higher question-answering accuracy on long-term memory benchmarks.

</details>

### [Locate, Judge, Read: Role-Specialized Tool Agents over a Knowledge-Point Index for Open-Domain Multimodal Document QA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13128)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.13128

> Presents a hybrid-retrieval system for multi-modal document QA, very relevant to RAG architecture.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Question answering over large heterogeneous document collections requires first finding the right passages and then reading them, yet current retrieval-augmented agents give one model both kinds of tools and let it schedule itself. We present KnowForge, a pipeline separated by job: a hybrid-retrieval knowledge-point index; a Locator agent that can only search; a code-scheduled bridge judge that forces the missing second hop; a typed, size-bounded evidence pool that merges every search's ranking; a Deep Reader agent that can only read text, images and tables; and a synthesizer with a format checker. With a self-hosted Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (3B active), KnowForge reaches F1 47.5 on the full open-domain M3DocVQA (2,441 questions, 3,368 PDFs), +11.0 over the published M3DocRAG system and +7.1 over one-shot retrieve-and-answer on the same index and model, and 53.5 with DeepSeek-V4-Flash. On MultiHop-RAG it reaches 73.5 accuracy, above the strongest published RAG and GraphRAG results (71.2); on the single-document MMLongBench-Doc it lifts the same model from 34.8 to 47.0, level with GPT-4o reading page images (46.3). Per-job ablations on the full benchmarks attribute the gain: the Deep Reader is worth 3.8, 9.8 and 0 points on the three corpora, tracking how visual each is; merging both agents into one with all tools and twice the rounds loses 1.9 F1 because it keeps searching instead of reading; a round-budget sweep shows a step at four Locator rounds and a plateau beyond; query latency scales with model calls rather than corpus size. We serve the three indexed benchmarks as read-only knowledge bases (free registration) and release all prompts and the runner code: https://github.com/metask-ai/knowforge-bench

### [SEEDS: Preview-Guided Evidence Admission for Search Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12932)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.12932

> Directly addresses evidence admission during search, which is highly relevant to RAG processes for contextual document handling.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Search agents extend large reasoning models with multi-round access to external knowledge, yet most existing systems largely conflate retrieving evidence with consuming it. Retrieved documents are commonly incorporated according to fixed or rank-based policies, despite the fact that evidence utility is conditioned on the agent's current reasoning state. Consequently, a lower-ranked candidate may be critical for the current step, while a higher-ranked candidate may provide redundant, irrelevant, or misleading information. We formulate this overlooked problem as evidence admission, which asks, at each search step, which retrieved candidates should be allowed to enter the agent's evolving reasoning context. To enable such selective control, we introduce SEEDS (Selective Evidence Entry During Search), a preview-guided evidence admission framework for search agents. SEEDS first exposes lightweight previews of retrieved candidates and then lets the agent jointly determine which candidates and how many should be fully inspected, conditioned on its current reasoning state. Unlike fixed or adaptive prefix policies, SEEDS can select arbitrary subsets with flexible cardinality, and it can be instantiated directly with an existing reasoning agent or further optimized through learning. Across nine knowledge-intensive QA benchmarks under both local corpus and live web search, SEEDS consistently improves search effectiveness while substantially reducing full-evidence consumption. Experimental analyses show that SEEDS more effectively selects useful candidates beyond the corresponding retrieval prefix. These results establish evidence admission as a complementary dimension of agentic search, separating what information is retrieved from what information is ultimately allowed to shape reasoning.

</details>

### [Temporal Evidence Forests for Long-Horizon Multi-Party Conversational Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12874)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.12874

> Directly addresses conversational memory for multi-party interactions, crucial for chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Long-term memory has been widely adopted in LLM-based agents to support persistent interaction and personalized assistance. Long-term multi-party interactions, however, accumulate substantially more detailed information, with decisions repeatedly updated and states evolving across participants and interaction stages. Temporal structure is therefore crucial for tracing such memories: relevant information often appears densely within short intervals, while transitions among different states are anchored by a strict temporal order. Existing temporally aware memory frameworks primarily store time as metadata or use temporal signals to guide retrieval over preconstructed memory structures, without using temporal structure to both recover locally concentrated evidence and reconstruct its complete event progression. To address these limitations, we propose T-Mem, a temporal-structure-based memory framework that preserves interactions and organizes query-relevant evidence through two temporal mechanisms. Temporal Evidence Discovery exploits the local concentration of related messages to recover overlooked evidence, while Temporal Memory Structuring models state transitions and supporting relations over time and compresses low-information spans within the resulting forest. Evidence Forest Linearization then serializes the structured evidence for answer generation. Experiments on two long-term multi-party memory benchmarks demonstrate that T-Mem consistently outperforms competitive memory frameworks across LLMs, parameter settings, domains, and query types.

</details>

### [VEPA: Verified Evolving Formal Policy for Lifelong LLM Agent Guardrails](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12813)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.12813

> Covers guardrails and safety for LLM applications, directly relevant to securing chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

LLM agents increasingly operate over sensitive resources, such as private documents and mutable system states, which can create serious safety and security risks. Runtime guardrails should therefore make inspectable, reproducible, and deterministic allow or refuse decisions before potentially irreversible agent actions are executed. However, safety and security requirements are rarely complete before deployment, so guardrails need to keep adapting as new risks emerge. Existing adaptive guardrails learn from deployment experience, but they usually apply safety policies through LLM inference, which can lead to inconsistent decisions and ambiguous policy interpretations. Formal guardrails, on the other hand, enforce policies deterministically, but they usually rely on pre-deployment or task-specific policies and have no mechanism to adapt to newly observed risks. We introduce VEPA, a framework for the verified evolution of formal agent guardrails. VEPA represents the guardrail as a formal policy of executable symbolic rules over typed agent events. During deployment, we use an LLM guided by deployment feedback to propose policy patches that fix newly observed failures while preserving previously correct behavior. Then, a deterministic verifier only accepts policies that are structurally valid and conflict-free. Finally, an evidence-aware authority layer activates a verified rule only when it has enough supporting evidence, so uncertain updates cannot directly affect guardrail decisions. Across ASSEBench-Safety, ASSEBench-Security, and ATBench, VEPA consistently outperforms baselines while expanding the scope of deterministic formal-policy enforcement. These gains also hold under more challenging deployment conditions, including mixed streams, corrupted feedback, and longer horizons.

</details>

### [NEAR: Neighborhood-Aware Residuals for Memory Retrieval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13314)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2610.13314

> Introduces a novel approach for memory retrieval that directly aligns with retrieving evidence in RAG architectures.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Memory retrieval aims to identify evidence from stored records to answer a query. Stored records with different factual details may share topics, entities, or events. Such shared contents would make records without the required evidence more likely to receive high similarity scores and outrank the correct evidence. In this paper, we use the information shared by these records to construct local references for query-memory matching. We propose NEighborhood-Aware Residuals (NEAR), a training-free framework that uses these references to construct residual representations that complement the original embeddings. Specifically, each memory derives a reference direction from its neighbors, and each query obtains its reference from a precomputed table. Using these references, NEAR constructs projection residuals for both queries and memories. These residuals are combined with the original embeddings for a single approximate nearest-neighbor search over a fixed memory index. Experiments on eight long-context retrieval benchmarks show consistent improvements across nine embedding models. Downstream evaluations show that NEAR improves question-answering performance.

</details>

### [APEX-Harm: Evaluating Agent Safety in Shared Professional Workspaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13623)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.13623

> Relates to safety in LLMs, particularly about guardrails and malicious inputs, worth skimming.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Professional agents increasingly work through command-line interfaces and multiple tools over shared workspaces containing files, messages, and business applications. These environments create safety risks that are difficult to capture with attacks placed at a single, known read location. We introduce APEX-Harm, a benchmark for evaluating prompt and script injection against long-horizon agents performing finance, consulting, and legal tasks in large workspaces. Each task contains 115 to 328 files, together with email, calendar, chat, document, spreadsheet, PDF, and code-execution tools exposed through MCP servers. The benchmark includes six attack categories covering static and dynamic injections, task-specific script replacement, and attacker-provided generic scripts, and evaluates attack success, exposure, and task performance. We evaluate 12 frontier models on 180 task instances per model. Opus 5 and Sonnet 5 have the lowest overall attack success rates (8% and 20%), while task-specific script replacement and attacker-provided generic script attacks remain substantially more effective, averaging 81-84% and 73% attack success, respectively. Gemini 3.8 Flash achieves the highest mean task score (0.877) while still exhibiting a 33% attack success rate. These results show that completing the requested task is not sufficient evidence of safe behavior: an agent can produce a correct deliverable while also performing an unauthorized action in the workspace.

</details>

### [KV-Repurpose: A Testbed for Evaluating Non-Prefix KV-Cache Reuse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13060)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.13060

> Explores KV-cache reuse for LLMs, relevant for efficiency in production chat systems.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

KV-cache reuse in large language model serving is commonly limited to exact prompt prefixes, preventing reuse when the same retrieved document appears after different system prompts, conversation histories, or dynamic metadata. We present KV-Repurpose, an open-source, vLLM-based system for developing and evaluating segment-wise KV-cache reuse beyond exact prefixes. The system combines cache-aware segment scheduling, prompt construction, capacity management, workload generation, and runtime monitoring. Experiments on long-context benchmarks show that naive segment reuse largely preserves output quality for single-document question answering and summarization, while exposing clear limitations in cross-document reasoning. Under a Zipfian RAG-style workload in which 20% of requests share a fixed prefix, KV-Repurpose achieves a substantially higher token-level cache hit rate than prefix caching at the same 30 GB cache capacity, 63.9% versus 3.3%, resulting in lower TTFT. Even with 1 TB of cache capacity, prefix caching reaches only a 17.9% hit rate in this workload. Source code is available under the Apache-2.0 license at https://github.com/taiga333/eval-kv-repurpose.

We present SmartSalesBench, the first multi-turn conversational benchmark explicitly designed to evaluate large language model (LLM) sales agents against psychologically grounded, resistant buyer personas. Existing task-oriented dialogue benchmarks assume cooperative users and measure task completion; SmartSalesBench inverts this assumption, placing agents against personas programmed to resist and challenge across conversations of up to twenty turns. ### [COFFEQA: Counterfactual-Guided Few-Shot Distillation for LLM Question Answering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12837)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.12837

> Explores few-shot distillation for question answering, relevant to optimizing the QA aspect of RAG.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Knowledge distillation aims to train a smaller student model to mimic a larger teacher model for resource-constrained environments. However, the process of distillation is typically data-intensive and computationally expensive, particularly for generative tasks. Few-shot distillation offers an alternative by transferring teacher knowledge using limited training samples. However, we observe that few-shot distillation performance is heavily dependent on the choice of these training samples. In fact, we demonstrate that using more uncertain samples near the teacher's decision boundary can significantly deteriorate distillation performance. Motivated by this observation, we propose Counterfactual-guided Few-shot Distillation in Open-book Question Answering (COFFEQA), a novel strategy that aptly uses counterfactuals to provide additional supervision around challenging factual samples. In the generative QA setting, we define a counterfactual as a minimally modified context that induces the teacher to change its response to an alternative answer. We provide geometric insights on how factual-counterfactual pairs can constrain the deviation between the teacher's and student's decision boundaries. We further introduce a new metric, Context Following Difficulty (CFD) that further validates counterfactuals prior to distillation based on agreement with the intended alternative answer and dependence on the modified context. COFFEQA uses CFD-validated factual-counterfactual pairs for few-shot distillation. Experiments across five in-domain and three out-of-domain QA benchmarks using T5 and Qwen2.5 families show that COFFEQA outperforms factual-only distillation baselines under the same few-shot budget. For instance, on TriviaQA, COFFEQA improves over the factual-only baseline by 8.77 average EM points and 9.98 average F1 points under the T5 setting with 32 few-shot pairs.

</details>

### [RATED: Reliability-Aware AI Agent Tool Retrieval via Execution Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13889)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.IR` · 2610.13889

> Proposes a retrieval framework that focuses on semantic matching, which could be relevant for retrieval in RAG systems.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

As AI agents increasingly rely on external tools, accurate retrieval is essential for reliable behavior. Existing methods optimize semantic relevance but largely ignore tool availability during execution. To address this gap, we propose RATED (Reliability-Aware Tool Retrieval via Execution Dynamics), a retrieval framework that combines robust semantic matching with dynamic reliability adaptation. RATED employs a frozen dual-encoder with contrastive fine-tuning for semantic matching, and a lightweight contextual bandit module that re-ranks candidates based on real-time execution feedback. This design enables rapid adaptation to runtime tool failures without expensive model retraining. We introduce EcoAgent, a benchmark of 5,172 real-world tools and over 210,000 query-tool pairs for offline retrieval and dynamic reliability evaluation. Experimental results demonstrate that RATED achieves a 52.2% relative MRR improvement over the ToolLLM API retriever on real-world queries, and reduces fault selection rate from 86.2% to 2.9% under severe API outage scenarios with a 30% failure rate, while incurring negligible latency overhead of less than 1 ms over encode-only inference. The code and dataset are available at https://github.com/Elliotes/EcoAgent.

### [Benchmarking Answer-Grounding Consistency in Remote Sensing Visual Question Answering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13636)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CV` · 2610.13636

> Directly assesses consistency in answer-grounding, relevant for evaluating RAG applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

In grounded remote sensing visual question answering, a model produces a textual answer and identifies relevant objects in the image as grounding evidence. Existing evaluations measure answer correctness and localization quality, but do not directly assess answer-grounding consistency. We propose the Answer-Evidence Support Protocol (AESP) to assess this consistency through evidence support: whether the grounding evidence supports the model's own answer.
