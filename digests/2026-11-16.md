### [MeReader: An Offline, Privacy-Preserving, Progress-Aware AI Assistant for Narrative eBook Reading](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18513)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.18513

> It presents a hybrid retrieval architecture for contextual reading, directly relevant to document ingestion and retrieval.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

This paper introduces MeReader, a privacy-preserving, offline and progress-aware AI assistant designed to function as a contextual companion for narrative reading on consumer hardware. Unlike conventional Large Language Model (LLM)-based systems that process documents/books online in an all-texts-at-once type of way, thereby risking narrative spoilers, MeReader confines both retrieval and generation to the reader's distinct position in the text. The system integrates a hybrid retrieval architecture, combining vector similarity, BM25, and summary embeddings, to ensure high fidelity to the reader's current context. We evaluate the system across classical English books using both quantitative comprehension metrics and qualitative LLM-based independent judging. The results identify a quality-latency frontier, showing that mid-sized local models (2b-4b) can achieve comprehension parity with larger counterparts (5b+) while maintaining acceptable response quality. Importantly, the proposed progress-aware boundary mechanism is shown to prevent future content leakage without compromising retrieval quality and correctness. These findings suggest that a localized, privacy-centric approach can augment, rather than replace, the digital reading experience, offering a good alternative to cloud-dependent services. Therein, there remains a lack of amalgamate systems which combine progress-aware, privacy-preserving, and narrative eBook reading with AI support. It is in this very gap that the development of MeReader came to be; synthesizing said strands into an offline, book-constrained assistant focused on enhancing comprehension without compromising narrative continuity or user privacy.

</details>

### [RAG-FGO: Dynamic Optimization of Query-Rewriting Agents for Generative RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17788)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.17788

> The paper directly addresses query rewriting in generative RAG, a core relevant topic for the project.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

In retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), query rewriting serves as a core interface that aligns user intent with external knowledge bases, and its effectiveness directly determines overall system performance. However, generative retrieval faces three critical challenges in real-world deployment: noisy retrieval feedback, the difficulty of jointly optimizing discrete prompt templates and continuous decoding-control parameters, and the limited generalization of static rewriting strategies under semantically complex scenarios. To address these issues, we propose RAG-FGO, a dynamic optimization framework for generative retrieval strategies. RAG-FGO formulates query rewriting as a non-differentiable black-box optimization problem in a mixed discrete-continuous space, and represents prompt templates and decoding parameters as adaptively optimizable query-rewriting agents. The framework performs global search to enable semantic jumps and structural reorganization that escape local optima, while applying local perturbations for fine-grained alignment and precise refinement. In addition, RAG-FGO introduces an elite-preserving memory pool that accumulates high-quality strategies across iterations, thereby mitigating the impact of stochasticity and noisy feedback on optimization stability. Experimental results on a diverse suite of benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed method, supporting its applicability to complex semantic retrieval tasks.

</details>

### [SemRAG: Semantic-Guided Sentence-Level Retrieval-Augmented Sign Language Production](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18189)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CV` · 2611.18189

> Directly addresses retrieval-augmented generation for sign language, aligning closely with RAG concepts.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Sign language production (SLP) aims to generate sign pose sequences that faithfully express spoken-language text while preserving the rhythm and coordination patterns of signing. Common SLP methods learn text-to-pose mappings from training data but often suffer from semantic inaccuracies in long or complex sentences. Retrieval augmentation offers a way to introduce realistic signing priors to provide more explicit guidance for text-to-pose generation. However, existing retrieval-based SLP methods typically operate at the gloss or word level, relying on gloss-pose dictionaries or isolated sign segments that require costly annotations and fail to preserve continuous sentence-level dynamics. To bridge this gap, we propose SemRAG, a semantic-guided sentence-level retrieval-augmented framework that retrieves full-sentence signing templates as priors without constructing gloss/word-level sign dictionaries. While such templates preserve local motion patterns and global rhythm, they also introduce a semantic mismatch problem: irrelevant motions in the retrieved sentence may interfere with the target semantics. To address this issue, we introduce an SLT-guided template semantic localization module that identifies target-relevant temporal regions within the retrieved template while suppressing irrelevant signing content. To adaptively incorporate the template features into the generator, we further introduce layer-specific learnable gates that regulate their contribution across generator layers. Extensive experiments show that SemRAG improves semantic consistency and motion quality over baselines under both back-translation and motion accuracy metrics.

</details>

### [GT-FENG: General Theory of Factual Evaluation and Normative Grounding in Language Models *Author(s)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18279)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.18279

> Discusses factual evaluation in language models, relevant for assessing RAG outputs.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Whether a language model's answer is correct often depends on context: the same value can be right under one jurisdiction, date, or legal definition and wrong under another. We introduce GT-FENG (General Theory of Factual Evaluation and Normative Grounding), the first general framework to unify contextual/temporal correctness and rule-based error attribution across law, policy, and other rule-governed domains. GT-FENG checks answers against the applicable rules and identifies documented rules that match incorrect answers. It shows that when multiple rules give the same value, the answer alone cannot tell which rule the model used. In Medicaid income-limit answers from two models, documenting the asked state's own records reduces the number of wrong answers that match only other states' values by 68-78%, and more than half of stable wrong answers then match two or more documented sources. GT-FENG provides a foundation for evaluating language models in rule-governed domains and for future benchmarks and evaluation toolkits. We release GT-FENG as an open-source Python package, gtfeng.

</details>

### [CarryOn: Learning What to Keep in Context for Long-Horizon LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17808)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.17808

> The paper's context management for long-horizon LLM agents is relevant for conversational memory in chat assistants.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Long-horizon LLM agents can coordinate tools and applications to complete complex, multi-step tasks. As their interaction histories grow, effective context management becomes essential for reliable and efficient execution. Existing methods reduce context through summarization, pruning, or history selection. However, shorter contexts do not necessarily support better decisions: summaries can omit exact details needed for later actions, while retaining entire interactions can carry substantial irrelevant information. We propose CarryOn, a lightweight context manager that pairs compact execution state with fine-grained, source-linked evidence. A learned selector and adaptive retention policy keep relevant evidence available without additional LLM calls for context management. We introduce two complementary evaluation metrics: $S_α$ prioritizes task success while accounting for resource use, and normalized cost per success (NCS) measures the resources spent per successful task. Across AppWorld, OfficeBench, and Tau2 Telecom with three LLM Actors, CarryOn achieves 75.7% macro-averaged task success, exceeding the strongest baseline by 6.7 percentage points. It also achieves the best average scores on both metrics.

</details>

### [Explanations that Transfer: Interpreting Medical Retrieval with Sparse AutoEncoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18179)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.18179

> It addresses improvements in retrieval relevance, which is useful for RAG architectures in medical contexts.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Medical AI systems that answer users' queries rely on retrieving medical knowledge from large document collections. In this context, finetuning general models on medical datasets can improve retrieval relevance. Yet the dense embeddings underlying modern retrieval systems remain largely opaque, making it difficult to understand the mechanics behind their results. Moreover, adapting to a specific medical dataset risks losing transferability to new clinical corpora. In this work, we tackle these issues by applying Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) to medical information retrieval, transforming dense text embeddings into semantically interpretable representations. To do so, our model combines a reconstruction objective with an InfoNCE contrastive loss that directly preserves query-document relevance. We evaluate three embedding models across six medical retrieval datasets, both in-domain and out-of-domain. Trained on a medical corpus, our sparse codes improve in-domain retrieval over the original embeddings, and lose less out-of-domain performance than their dense counterparts, especially when the target corpus is distant from the training one. Importantly, our experiments show that the method yields interpretable features whose semantic content can be inferred from the queries and documents that activate them most strongly. The interpretation of these features sheds light on why a particular document was retrieved, and transfers beyond the training distribution.

</details>

### [DoubleTake: Focused Verification of Visual Evidence for Long-Document Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17853)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CV` · 2611.17853

> The paper addresses focused verification of visual evidence in long-document Q&A, relevant to grounded QA and retrieval.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

In long-document question answering, a vision-language model can answer incorrectly even when the retrieved pages contain sufficient evidence. Rereading those pages with the original question gives the model little guidance about which fact to check. We introduce DoubleTake, a training-free framework for focused verification of visual evidence. An initial reading produces a candidate answer and a separate evidence record that guides retrieval for a second reading. A planner compares the two candidates and their supporting evidence to formulate a factual question. A verifier answers it from the source images without seeing either candidate or evidence record, and a reasoner uses this observation to resolve the original question. With a frozen Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct backbone, DoubleTake achieves the highest overall accuracy among the evaluated methods on each of four benchmarks. With the same pages and number of model calls, asking a focused factual question yields higher accuracy on all four benchmarks than asking the original question again. Our code is available at https://github.com/small-code-cat/double_take.

</details>
