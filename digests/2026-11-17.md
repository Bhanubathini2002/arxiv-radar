### [MemDoctor: Stage-wise Error Attribution and Repair for MLLM Agent Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21019)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.21019

> Directly addresses improvements in MLLM memory which is vital for managing context in chat applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) now assist users across sessions of images and text, carried by an external memory of what was said and shown. When such an assistant answers wrongly, the failure usually began earlier, in how a record was written, updated or retrieved, and the answer alone does not say where. We formalize multimodal memory as four stages, Write, Update, Retrieve and Answer, and define nine error types across them. A stage-resolved analysis on Mem-Gallery traces most failures to a source stage, and most of those lie before the answer. Injecting known errors against fact-preserving control edits, we then follow each tainted record downstream. Early errors survive to the answer as apparent evidence, and hurt most when no other record holds the fact. A diagnosis that trusts the quoted evidence therefore blames the stage where the error surfaces, not the one where it began. We propose MemDoctor, in which the MLLM that built the memory diagnoses its own failures. It traces each needed fact backward through the four stages, accepts a diagnosis only when the cited evidence is found in memory, and repairs that stage before answering again. On a 4B backbone, MemDoctor improves accuracy on both Mem-Gallery and MemLens, outperforming all evaluated 8B baselines, and the same loop also improves MuRAG and A-Mem.

### [When the Bill Falls: Reusing the Old Index Defers,Never Avoids, Re-encoding in Complete Embedding-Model Migration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20702)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.20702

> Directly discusses embedding models and efficiency in vector databases, crucial for vector search.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Upgrading the embedding model behind a vector index requires re-encoding the corpus. Recent work reuses the old index through adapters or window re-ranking. Across sixteen model pairs on passage and code corpora, plus eleven on Natural Questions (NQ), we find three results. First, reuse never avoids re-encoding: if migration must complete, every strategy eventually encodes every document; reuse only changes when that work is paid and the quality served meanwhile. Second, adapters should be compared with the old model, not only the new one. Of five adapter variants, only same-family LLM bridges beat the old model, and only by a negligible margin. Third, window re-ranking cost can be priced from the old index alone; pre-registered replays on all 8,841,823 passages confirmed the prediction on all three tested pairs. Workload skew also matters: across 36 further pre-registered Zipf replays, the planned family matched on 32. These findings yield three pre-migration rules: whether reuse is worthwhile, which strategy to use, and how deep a window to take. The depth rule priced every window that reached target quality to within 4 parts in 10^5 of the corpus. On NQ, a pre-committed plan selected the family on 10 of 11 pairs and priced every window exactly. Quality inputs transferred less reliably across corpus scale, so the model gap and window quality loss must be measured on the serving index.

</details>

### [Estimating Outpatient Visit Prices from Hospital Price Transparency Data using Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20261)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.20261

> Directly involves RAG methods for healthcare cost estimation, highly relevant to user interests.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Objective: Hospital care prices in the United States have increased substantially, yet patients lack effective tools to access and compare costs. We aimed to develop and evaluate an LLM-based approach for improving access to and use of hospital price transparency (PT) data. Materials and Methods: To estimate visit prices, we used a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) framework to analyze PT files from three major hospitals in Maryland and the surrounding region. We compared BM25, OpenAI embedding models, and a GPT-BM25 hybrid against a no-RAG baseline for cost estimation of individual procedures and procedure groups, then evaluated the best approach for symptom-based visit cost estimation using synthetic simulations. Results: Across hospitals, the GPT-BM25 retriever achieved the best performance, with average price MAPE of 19.44% for individual procedures and 4.90% for groups, compared with 147.98% and 82.82%, respectively, for the no-RAG baseline. In simulations, our framework correctly identified approximately 50% of reference procedures and reduced MAPE relative to the LLM-only baseline at two of the three hospitals, while achieving comparable performance at the third. Discussion: Our framework uses RAG to improve care price estimation in real-world settings. Compared with the no-RAG baseline, it substantially improved cost estimates for both individual procedures and procedure groups. Improvements were smaller in synthetic simulations, likely because inferring procedures from clinical information is more complex. Practical challenges remain, including heterogeneous PT file formats, unexplained duplicate entries, and limited availability of insurer-negotiated rates. Conclusion: LLMs that effectively integrate PT data show strong potential to provide patients with more accessible and accurate healthcare cost estimates.

### [UniGraph: A Unified Graph-based Index for Dynamic Hybrid Dense-Sparse Vector Retrieval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20758)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.20758

> Describes a hybrid graph-based index for vector retrieval, relevant for optimizing retrieval processes in RAG.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Hybrid dense-sparse vector retrieval combines semantic signals from dense representations with lexical signals from sparse representations, typically ranking items by a query-dependent weighted sum of dense and sparse vector distances. Existing systems commonly search separate dense and sparse vector indexes and rerank the union of their candidates, incurring two retrieval passes and potentially discarding items that rank highly only after the semantic and lexical signals are combined. Constructing a graph-based index directly under a hybrid distance can mitigate this premature candidate loss. However, because varying the hybrid weight alters the underlying neighborhood relationships, a single static graph built at a fixed weight cannot accommodate dynamic query-specific weights. Therefore, we propose a Unified Hybrid Graph (UniGraph), which merges neighbor sets computed at multiple hybrid weights into a single bounded-degree graph index, enabling hybrid vector retrieval with different query-specific weights without index reconstruction. We further develop an adaptive weight-guided search framework that chooses between sparse- and dense-guided entry points according to the query weight, together with a dense-first hybrid distance pruning strategy that avoids unnecessary exact sparse distance computations. Extensive experiments show that UniGraph provides a favorable Recall-QPS trade-off across different query weights. In the mixed-weight setting, UniGraph delivers 27.6%~52.3% higher QPS than the fastest baseline on each dataset at Recall@100 greater than or equal to 0.95. UniGraph has been deployed at scale in Ant Group's production environment, where hybrid retrieval with query-dependent weights is in strong demand.

</details>

### [CARE: Contrastive-Anchored Residual Encoding with Listwise Reconstruction for Retrieval-Oriented Semantic IDs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20721)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.20721

> Explores retrieval-oriented semantic ID tokenization, relevant to document retrieval and RAG architecture.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Generative retrieval replaces nearest-neighbor search with the generation of a document's semantic ID (SID), so the SID tokenizer bounds what the retriever can recall. Common SID constructions (residual k-means, RQ-VAE, finite scalar quantization, capacity balancing) optimize criteria defined on document embeddings alone and do not control whether a query can still find its relevant documents after quantization. We propose two retrieval-oriented SID tokenizers. CARE-SID (Contrastive-Anchored Residual Encoding for Semantic IDs) quantizes in a retrieval-aligned space: a zero-initialized residual alignment network is trained with a query-document contrastive loss before quantization, an anchor loss and a reconstruction target fixed to the original embedding prevent drift and collapse, and reference-vector rating residual quantization assigns the codes. CARE-LR (CARE with Listwise Reconstruction) then distills, for each training query, the similarity distribution that the original embeddings induce over a candidate list into the SID reconstructions, through a function-preserving layer-resolved head. Both keep a document-only encoding interface. We judge SIDs without training a generator, by row-budget recall in a bucketing view (the partition) and a reconstruction view (the SID with its reconstruction). In offline evaluation on industrial video search data, both tokenizers achieve higher reconstruction-view recall than residual k-means, and CARE-LR improves further on CARE-SID, mainly through the listwise distillation. A comparison of two document embeddings shows that contrastive alignment helps most when the embedding has not been trained on search data, and that the embedding affects SID recall far more than the quantizer. CARE-SID is deployed in the generative retrieval stage of an industrial video search system.

</details>

### [DetouR: Plugging Association Routes into Search Agents for Multi-hop Question Answering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19967)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.19967

> Directly discusses multi-hop retrieval and evidence selection, crucial for RAG applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Search agents answer multi-hop questions by interleaving reasoning with retrieval, deciding at each step what to search next. Existing agents follow semantic routes: they infer what is missing and phrase it as a query, reaching only what the model can name. Yet the decisive page is often one the question never names, frequently just one hyperlink away from evidence already retrieved. Following such association routes reaches more gold pages than semantic search under the same budget. Exploiting them raises two challenges: obtaining the association structure without costly offline graph construction, and keeping linked evidence in the reader's limited context, where relevance- and fusion-based selection discards it. We propose DetouR, a plug-in that reuses the links arriving with retrieved pages and adds no search or LLM call. Link Rerouting replaces near-repeat queries with routes to linked pages, chosen by a lightweight learned scorer; Chain-aware Context Selection runs personalized PageRank over these links so that evidence chains reach the reader intact. Across four agents, DetouR improves EM by 4.0 points on 2Wiki and 1.4 on HotpotQA on average. We also find that heavier LLM models struggle with this non-semantic evidence, adding little over these simple selectors or even falling behind. In summary, our work reveals association routes as a cheap complement to semantic routes that current search agents overlook: added as a plug-in, they improve every agent we test, in replay and in live runs, at no extra search or LLM cost.

</details>

### [LMEG-Doc: Layout-Grounded Multi-View Evidence Graphs for Multimodal Long-Document Retrieval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18901)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.18901

> Addresses multimodal long-document retrieval, directly relevant to document ingestion strategies for RAG.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieving relevant evidence from multimodal long documents remains challenging, as supporting information is often distributed across different pages and modalities. Existing retrieval methods primarily rank document elements by their individual similarity to a question, often overlooking relationships among complementary evidence. Consequently, relevant figures and tables may receive low retrieval scores even when they contain essential information. To address this limitation, we introduce LMEG-Doc, a layout-grounded multi-view evidence graph framework for multimodal long-document retrieval. We first construct an evidence graph that captures structural and contextual relationships among document elements, and introduce Cross-Page Relation Refinement (CRR) to discover additional connections across distant pages through VLM-based verification. Building on this graph, we combine multimodal retrieval signals with graph-guided relevance propagation to recover connected supporting evidence at both layout and page levels. Experiments on MMDocIR, DocScope, and FinRAGBench-V demonstrate consistent retrieval improvements, including outperforming retrievers fine-tuned on MMDocIR without task-specific training. Furthermore, augmenting existing question-answering pipelines with our retrieved evidence improves answer accuracy by 7.6% on DocScope and 15.8% on FinRAGBench-V, demonstrating the effectiveness of relationship-aware multimodal retrieval.

### [Jetriever: Shifting Agent Memory Retrieval from Similarity to Utility](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21478)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.21478

> Introduces a memory retrieval method focusing on utility rather than similarity, relevant for context management.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

A useful memory need not resemble the question it helps answer. Jetriever ranks each stored memory by Jev's decision score for its usefulness to the current question, replacing similarity search with full-pool utility scoring. On InMind's 125 indirect-query tasks, it places the gold exchange first in 84.8% of cases; matched BM25 and MiniLM controls reach 1.6% and 0.0% when indexing user turns. A 20-question comparison separates finding evidence from ranking it: Jev and Qwen3.5 9B each find 90% of gold exchanges by rank 50, while Laya finds 30%; at rank one, Jev reaches 85% and Qwen3.5 9B 40%. On a dimension-proportional random sample of 60 MemoryAgentBench questions, Jetriever's four-dimension score is 55.4%, against 44.3% for ReFind on the same questions. Jev retrieval costs USD 0.00834 per InMind question and USD 0.03103 per MAB question on average. These results show the value of world knowledge, scores that rank decisive memories first, and affordable full-pool scoring.

### [Instant Weight-Space Consolidation for Long-Term Memory in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20759)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.20759

> Substantially discusses long-term memory systems in LLMs, relevant for conversational memory.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Agentic memory systems give LLMs persistent memory by extracting experience into text and retrieving it at query time, but the model itself does not evolve: however many interactions it has had, it starts each query the same. Extraction errors compound, implicit cross-session patterns that no single fragment states cannot be surfaced, and the construction cost grows with every conversation. Fine-tuning on past interactions does change the weights, but requires backpropagation at serving time and degrades on long histories. We introduce CLAM (Carried Latent Adapter Memory), which combines the strengths of both: a recurrent hypernetwork performs parametric consolidation, compiling a stream of session activations into a low-rank adapter in a single forward pass with no gradient steps and no language-model generation calls, while a complementary episodic buffer supplies verbatim detail at inference. A gated latent state carries knowledge across sessions in fixed size, so the parametric memory accumulates without growing. On LoCoMo and LongMemEval-S across three open models (2-7B parameters), CLAM leads all agentic baselines by 1-45 points and all gradient-based parametric methods by 10-45 points, while requiring zero generative LLM calls, zero gradient steps, and 3-151x less wall-clock time than extraction systems to build its memory. The two channels are complementary: the consolidated adapter adds 12-15 accuracy points when the episodic buffer misses the gold evidence, and still adds 4-5 points even with an oracle buffer.

</details>

### [Risk-certified prompt optimization for LLM-as-a-judge guardrails](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20156)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2611.20156

> Relevant to guardrails for LLM applications, particularly regarding prompt optimization.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

LLM-as-a-judge guardrails are often controlled primarily through system prompts, yet standard prompt optimization methods target average performance rather than explicit safety constraints. This paper studies system-prompt selection as a constrained optimization problem: maximize benign pass rate while keeping the missed-attack rate below a user-specified level α with finite-sample statistical control. The framework combines LLM-based judging, automatic prompt generation, and distribution-free risk certification. This work instantiates it in two procedures: CPS and CRISP. CPS generates a fixed pool of prompts from training data, then certifies candidates on a held-out evaluation set using simultaneous upper confidence bounds and selects the certified prompt with the highest benign utility. CRISP instead integrates risk feedback into an adaptive search, using hard example-guided rewriting and a reusable-holdout interface to steer generation toward the feasible utility frontier. Experiments on four datasets covering prompt injection, system-prompt leakage, and sensitive-information disclosure show that risk-certified selection consistently respects the target missed-attack level on heldout tests, while utility improves as the permitted risk is relaxed. CRISP is especially useful when fixed-pool search fails to produce certifiable high-utility candidates.

</details>

### [Adaptive-Elbow Graph Traversal for Schema Retrieval in Text-to-SQL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20979)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.20979

> Relevant to schema retrieval and context management for database queries in application development.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

LLM-based natural-language interfaces to relational databases are gated by a schema-retrieval step: the generator can only be as good as the slice of schema it is shown. On a database with hundreds of tables, that slice must be selected and the selection is two-sided---omit a needed table and the query becomes unrecoverable, include too many and the irrelevant ones act as noise that degrades the model's output. When an agentic retriever that lets the model inspect the schema can be too costly or too slow, the fallback is an embedder that ranks tables by cosine similarity and keeps a top-k. This fallback has two weaknesses: a fixed budget is suboptimal question by question, and embedding similarity overlooks foreign-key-linked tables that are necessary for joins. We present Adaptive-Elbow Graph Traversal (AEGT, ElbowHop for short), a property-graph table retriever that addresses both: an adaptive elbow cutoff replaces the fixed budget with a per-question one under a single aggressiveness parameter, and bidirectional foreign-key traversal deterministically recovers the bridge tables embedding similarity misses. We evaluate the retriever on 3 corpora of increasing candidate-pool size (BIRD Mini-Dev queried per database, 3--13 tables; its eleven schemas merged, 74; and a merged Spider-train graph, 546) and isolate the two mechanisms under a per-question budget-matched protocol. Across these settings the adaptive elbow emerges as the dominant mechanism---at matched recall it roughly doubles precision over a fixed budget on the larger pools, the margin widening as the pool grows---while the foreign-key traversal pays off on the join-critical bridge tables dense retrieval cannot reach, in the single-hop tight-budget case. We characterize the method as a low-cost, recall-robust first stage for schema retrieval on which a downstream selection or generation step can build.

</details>

### [EchoSpec: Training Search Agents to Weigh Evidence Above Echoes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20034)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.20034

> Addresses reinforcement learning in search and retrieval, useful for retraining models in RAG.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Search agents trained with reinforcement learning decide what to retrieve, when to search again, and when to answer. In real corpora, one claim is copied and paraphrased across many pages while the primary source settling a question appears once. We show that reinforcement-learned search agents grant echoes the credence of independent sources and lose their ability to abstain. Across five base models from 3B to 32B and seven released RL agents on multi-hop Wikipedia questions with primary evidence intact, eight paraphrased false-claim echoes overturn 58-73% of previously correct answers, while exact-match RL drives deleted-evidence abstention below 15%. Conditioning on retrieval localizes the failure to evidence acquisition: once primary evidence enters context, agents overrule eight echoes as reliably as one, whereas echoes fill top ranks and halt search early, so larger budgets, forced search turns, or passive passage filtering alone fail to recover displaced evidence. We introduce EchoSpec, specifying reliability across five corpus transformations with known target answers and training in two stages. Verification warm-start teaches multi-hop bridge-entity recovery past echoes, after which group RL optimizes an evidence-gated $λ$-reward with an outcome-independent acquisition bonus and scale-preserving advantages retaining the optimal rule $λ/(1+λ)$. Across Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Llama-3.1-8B backbones on nine evaluation suites, EchoSpec cuts eight-echo deference by 30-44 points on Qwen2.5-7B down to 29-31%, nearly quadruples eight-echo reach to 26-27% with zero-shot transfer to unseen BGE and BM25 retrievers, and raises standalone QA precision to 47-55%. Code is publicly available at https://github.com/unihsy/EchoSpec.

</details>

### [Attribute or Abstain: Evaluating Evidence-Grounded Retrieval on the Low-Resource African Business Web](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19900)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.19900

> Evaluates evidence-grounded retrieval, relevant for measuring query outputs in RAG systems.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented systems that research companies are judged by how much they find, yet in markets where public evidence is sparse, a confident answer without support costs more than no answer. We introduce AfriBizClaims, a benchmark of 150 company-research queries across six African markets in English, French and Portuguese, including queries about fictitious companies that a correct system must decline. Each claim a system makes is judged against the page it cites by an AI judge, checked against a second AI judge from another model family and against two human annotators, and abstentions are scored against answerability labels. We compare BM25 over a pooled corpus, standard web retrieval-augmented generation, and a multi-step agent pipeline (Konexium V1), all using the same language model. The two simple baselines are well attributed: 98% of their judged claims are supported by the page they cite, and they decline every field of every fictitious company. Counting claims about a company other than the one asked about as errors, the agent pipeline's per-query precision is 68%, and it never answers a question about a named company correctly: it substitutes other firms, including for 5 of the 24 fictitious names, and it is the least stable between runs and the most expensive. In the pipeline we evaluated, more steps between the query and the evidence did not buy more grounded answers. We will release the queries, system outputs, labels and short evidence excerpts.

### [Revisiting What Makes a Good Context Subset: A Sparse Latent Basis Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19220)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.IR` · 2611.19220

> Examines context subset selection which is important for effective RAG performance.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Selecting context subsets is increasingly important in modern LLM inference, yet what makes a subset effective remains unclear. We study this question in the controlled setting of few-shot demonstration selection. We examine a hypothesis that demonstrations should collectively provide task-relevant information needed by a target query. Assuming that the query representation provides a useful proxy for these requirements, we formulate selection as sparse reconstruction and instantiate it with Residual Explanation Search (RES), a Matching-Pursuit-style diagnostic procedure. Across seven benchmarks and eight open-weight LLMs, no retrieval objective is universally best. RES outperforms MMR in 34 of 56 model--dataset pairs, with its clearest advantage on sentiment tasks, suggesting that query-conditioned collective reconstruction may capture useful complementarity that pairwise dissimilarity misses. Relevance-based retrieval remains strong on template-heavy reasoning benchmarks, while all retrieval methods underperform zero-shot prediction on average on StrategyQA, where a solution-reconstruction analysis is consistent with the query not exposing the required external knowledge. Target-model hidden states further show that retrieval objectives interact differently with representation spaces: middle-layer representations generally favor similarity retrieval, whereas last-layer representations are often stronger for residual- and diversity-based selection. These findings support a conditional, representation-dependent account of context subset quality rather than a universally superior selector.

</details>
