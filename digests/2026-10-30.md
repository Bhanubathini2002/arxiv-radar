### [Quantum-Inspired Pairwise Ranking Aggregation via Active LLM Feedback](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38683)
**Score 10/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.38683

> This paper directly addresses ranking aggregation in RAG systems, essential for effective retrieval processes.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Ranking aggregation from multiple retrievers is a powerful yet under-explored technique in modern retrieval and RAG systems. We introduce LLM-IRA, the first extension of the quantum-inspired interactive ranking aggregation framework to document reranking. By leveraging large language models (LLMs) solely for pairwise judgments on high-disagreement document pairs, our method learns fusion weights in a principled, interactive way. The final ranking is a simple, ultra-fast pointwise linear fusion of scores, no full pairwise matrix or expensive listwise prompting is required. Experiments on the BEIR benchmark and TREC Deep Learning tracks with an open-weight instruction-tuned judge (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) show that LLM-IRA matches or numerically exceeds strong static fusion baselines on 5 of 6 evaluated benchmarks. We report two weight-update variants: momentum blending and a full accumulated-history refit, which together reach statistical significance (Wilcoxon signed-rank, p<0.05) over the best unsupervised aggregator on 4 of 6 benchmarks (DL19 under momentum; DL20, DBpedia, and NFCorpus under accumulated refit, which is the stronger of the two configurations overall). On the benchmarks we profiled (DL19, DL20), it already clears the best unsupervised baseline within as few as 5 LLM calls per query, well before exhausting its typical ~25--30 call budget. LLM-IRA also achieves cost-effectiveness comparable to state-of-the-art zero-shot LLM rerankers at an order of magnitude fewer LLM calls, and requires no training data or model fine-tuning.

### [ShallowDefend: Harnessing Shallow Safety Alignment for Multi-Turn Defender](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40627)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.40627

> Directly addresses safety for LLMs in conversational settings, crucial for robust RAG applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Safety alignment has enabled large language models to reliably refuse harmful requests directly, yet the same request distributed across a multi-turn conversation still succeeds far too often. This brittleness has a mechanistic root: Alignment is shallow, with safety behavior concentrated in a model's first few output tokens, and existing defenses inherit the weakness, supervising the surface form of the response without ever giving the model an explicit safety decision to learn. We take the opposite view of shallowness: if a short span determines the trajectory of an entire response, that span is the highest-leverage place to intervene, and the defender can occupy it first. We propose Assess $\to$ Decide $\to$ Respond, a framework in which the defender assesses the user's intent, commits to an explicit, parseable safety decision, and only then generates a reply conditioned on it, with the commitment trained by reinforcement learning at the payload turn. Because the decision is a token span, the reward can be a rule: a self-verified reward checks the committed label against the conversation's known nature, requiring no judge and no annotation, while the same format also supports an LLM-as-judge reward over a graded decision space with safe redirection. Across two model families and fourteen benchmarks spanning safety, utility, and helpfulness, our defenders achieve the strongest multi-turn safety in a controlled comparison, maintaining their lead in safety while preserving utility at a modest cost to helpfulness. A per-token KL analysis and interventional probes confirm why this works: training concentrates its effect on the brief deliberation that sets a response's trajectory, and the decision committed there is a causal control point. The very shallowness attackers exploit becomes the mechanism of defense.

</details>

### [Glance: The Generator Is Its Own Reranker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40454)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.40454

> The paper introduces an efficient reranking approach relevant to RAG architectures and retrieval performance.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented generation commonly serves separate models for reranking and answering, duplicating model residency and passage processing. We introduce Glance, which equips the generator with a lightweight decision adapter and pointer head to score candidate passages from a shared prefill. Short relevance probes branch from the cached context, producing one score per passage without separately prefilling each query-passage pair. A single resident 9B backbone supports generation, general decisions and reranking, with approximately 0.1 GiB of additional memory per decision skill. After 39 minutes of MS MARCO fine-tuning, Glance achieves nDCG@10 of 0.741 on SciFact and 0.345 on NFCorpus, compared with 0.721 and 0.326 for bge-reranker-v2-m3. On 300 HotpotQA questions, its selected context improves answer F1 by 0.045 over cross-encoder-selected context under the same base generator (paired bootstrap 95% CI [0.014, 0.076]). In a scaling study, reranking quality keeps rising up to 200 candidates (43k tokens) scored from one shared prefill, where it exceeds the cross-encoder by 0.058 nDCG@10, and a probe-only activated adapter lets generation continue from the original passage cache. These initial results demonstrate generator-based reranking with one resident backbone; evaluation currently covers two retrieval datasets, one QA dataset and one backbone family.

### [Incentivizing Self-Learning in Large Language Models via Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39671)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.39671

> Investigates self-learning in LLMs with RAG features, directly applicable to user interests.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large language models (LLMs) draw on parametric knowledge and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to solve knowledge-intensive tasks. Yet problem solving in unfamiliar domains calls for tightly coupled knowledge acquisition and reasoning, beyond factual retrieval and aggregation or learning solely from a preassembled context. We study self-learning -- autonomously retrieving, studying, and applying knowledge from long-form reference materials on the fly. To investigate this capability, we formulate a textbook-based mathematical problem-solving task. LLMs must navigate textbook exposition, learn and apply relevant principles, and construct proofs for exercises, with reference solutions withheld. We introduce a BookNavigator interface to support this human-inspired study process and incentivize self-learning through reinforcement learning (RL) with fine-grained rewards. Experiments show that our method improves performance across in-domain and out-of-domain textbooks and subjects, raising Intern-S2-Preview-35B's overall proof accuracy from 51.5% to 79.5% and outperforming RL-trained textbook-free and fixed-retrieval baselines. These gains extend to complex long-context benchmarks involving different task types, with improvements over the base model of 1.7 and 2.3 percentage points on BrowseComp-Plus and CL-bench, respectively. Together, these findings suggest that RL can foster a domain-agnostic self-learning strategy, highlighting the potential of self-learning from long-form corpora to strengthen broader problem-solving capabilities on complex, long-context tasks.

### [Unlimited OCR Works 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39308)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.39308

> Directly addresses document parsing, crucial for RAG document ingestion.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Unlimited OCR ushers the era of long-horizon parsing, substantially mitigating the growth of working memory as document lengthens. However, as document length scales to hundreds of pages, the visual context grows substantially, sharply increasing the computational cost of each decoding step and limiting the scalability of ultra-long document parsing. Inspired by human book transcription via localized focus without re-scanning preceding pages, we introduce Unlimited OCR 2, a document parsing model that looks only at what it transcribes. Building on Unlimited OCR, we upgrade all the decoder attention modules to Sparse Reference Sliding Window Attention (SR-SWA), bounding active reference tokens to a fixed capacity and thus sustaining constant computational costs across arbitrarily long documents. By combining SR-SWA with Unlimited-Horizon Decoding Paradigm, Unlimited OCR 2 enables document parsing over virtually unlimited lengths without prohibitive memory overhead. At the hundred-page scale, Unlimited OCR 2 achieves 7.0$\times$ and 5.6$\times$ per-step speedups over Full Attention and R-SWA, respectively. Furthermore, it delivers state-of-the-art end-to-end performance on both MPDocBench and MarathonDocBench, our newly proposed benchmark targeting hundred-page document parsing. Ultimately, the capacity for uninterrupted, one-shot document parsing signifies a fundamental paradigm shift into the OCR 3.0 era.

### [Contextual Embeddings Beyond the Gold Chunk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38854)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.38854

> This paper directly discusses chunking strategies and contextual embeddings, which are essential for RAG applications.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Contextual embeddings represent chunks of a document with respect to the document's global context. These models are typically trained and evaluated under the assumption that a relevant document contains only a single relevant chunk. In this work, we argue that in real-world use cases a retriever should not only find answers spread over several chunks but also surface the evidence needed to verify them. To address this, we introduce pplx-embed-v2-context, a family of contextual embedding models trained by distilling graded chunk-level relevance from a query-aware context compression model. This removes the need for gold-chunk labels and makes the training targets independent of the chunking. We utilize a vision-language backbone for training and release pplx-embed-v2-context in 9B and 0.6B sizes with compatible embedding spaces. On the evaluation side, we introduce two benchmarks that label both the chunks that answer a query and the evidence needed to verify the answer. Context-bench is a controlled diagnostic, and Q2D-Chunk is built from real-world search queries. To encourage further research, we also publish Q2D-Chunk as a public leaderboard. Our 9B model sets a new state of the art on both benchmarks and ConTEB. We find that the visual retrieval capabilities of our 9B model transfer from its vision-language base model and propose to extend chunking to image inputs. We demonstrate that our models achieve state-of-the-art performance among dense retrievers on visual ViDoRe-V3 and that performance improves with granular image chunking.

### [Decompose, Estimate, Select: Coverage-Aware Context Construction for Long-Form RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40314)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2610.40314

> The paper details a retrieval pipeline that enhances coverage for complex queries in RAG, making it essential for your project.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) grounds a language model's output in retrieved evidence, but for complex questions spanning multiple aspects of an information need, relevance-based retrieval may return redundant evidence while leaving some aspects unsupported. We propose a coverage-aware retrieval pipeline that decomposes queries into subquestions, ranks candidates with a coverage-trained bi-encoder, predicts a document\,$\times$\,subquestion coverage matrix with a cross-encoder, and selects documents by greedy maximum-marginal coverage. On TREC RAG 2024, our approach improves $α$-nDCG@10, which rewards newly covered nuggets and discounts redundant evidence, from $0.625$ to $0.702$, approaching the $0.718$ gold-nugget upper bound without sacrificing relevance. We then find that ground-truth oracle retrieval, despite substantially higher retrieval coverage, delivers no more nugget coverage to the answer than our estimated selection. This suggests that answer completeness depends not only on what evidence is retrieved, but also on how effectively the generator uses it. To address this, we introduce a \emph{grounded self-feedback loop} in which the model identifies underused evidence from its first answer and generates again without gold supervision, raising $V_{\mathrm{strict}}$, the fraction of vital reference nuggets fully supported by the answer, by $0.086$ over two passes while largely preserving faithfulness.

### [xPRT: Cross-Passage Retrieval Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40122)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2610.40122

> It introduces an unsupervised framework for better retrieval pretraining, directly relevant to RAG document ingestion.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Dense retrievers are a foundational component underlying modern RAG and search systems. Training these models typically requires a curated collection of annotated query-document pairs, which are prohibitively expensive to obtain outside a handful of well-resourced domains. This has motivated a line of work that leverages large-scale unsupervised retrieval pretraining from unlabelled text alone. Most such methods learn by optimizing a language modelling or contrastive learning objective over short spans within a document. This raises the question: how can we extract richer learning signals from a single document for retrieval pretraining? To address it, we introduce xPRT (Cross-Passage Retrieval Pretraining), an unsupervised framework that learns from the key information distributed across an entire document and the connections between its parts. We evaluate xPRT against five state-of-the-art objectives on MTEB English v2, continuing pretraining from a public unsupervised checkpoint while holding the corpus, schedule, and training procedure fixed. Across diverse tasks spanning all task types, we observe that xPRT consistently outperforms the strongest baseline by 3.2% on an average. Notably, in this continual pretraining setting, many baseline methods incur substantial performance regressions, whereas our method delivers the largest complementary gains over the initial pretrained checkpoint. Controlled ablations confirm that xPRT's performance gains stem from its proposed cross-passage signals. xPRT demonstrates that a single unlabelled document is a much richer source of retrieval signal than prior work assumed and offers complementary gains to what current retrievers already capture.

### [CoWalk: Cooperative Walks for Agentic Batch Queries on Disk-Resident Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38404)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2610.38404

> Looks into batch processing of vector queries, highly relevant to vector search efficiency.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Workloads such as query rewriting, deep research and group-relative policy optimization (GRPO) issue batches of semantically related vector queries. Existing disk-resident approximate nearest neighbor (ANN) systems process these queries independently. On three workloads, 31 to 67 percent of the I/Os of a batch fetch a page that another query of the batch has already fetched. A page cache shared by the batch removes these I/Os, yet it makes a single-thread search only $1.1$ to $1.2{\times}$ faster, because the search time goes to distance computation and the cache does not change the walk of any query. We present CoWalk, which runs the queries of a batch as cooperating walks on one thread. Every query keeps its own candidate list, a page is read once per batch, and the full-precision vector on every fetched page is ranked by every query of the batch. A query therefore reaches the target recall with a shorter candidate list, which computes fewer distances and issues fewer I/Os. At recall@10 of 0.95, CoWalk answers a batch on one thread $1.6$ to $1.9{\times}$ faster than the fastest per-query baseline and issues $2.8$ to $6.9{\times}$ fewer I/Os. On a server it serves $3.0$ to $6.9{\times}$ the throughput of that baseline at a similar batch latency.

</details>

### [DeFT-RAG: Incrementally Decomposing Multi-Hop Questions into Self-Contained Sub-Questions with Reusable Answers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37826)
**Score 9/10** · `cs.IR` · 2610.37826

> Directly addresses multi-hop questions in RAG, a core concern for the user's project.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) lets a large language model (LLM) answer questions by retrieving relevant passages from a large corpus. However, RAG struggles with multi-hop questions, which require chaining facts spread across several documents through intermediate entities that the question never mentions. Graph-based RAG methods retrieve once from the entities a question names and so rarely recover the full evidence chain. Iterative RAG methods recover it step by step, but their accuracy is limited by the many tasks they ask the LLM to perform, such as planning the sub-questions, deciding their dependencies, rewriting them as answers arrive, and keeping track of what is known. We propose DeFT-RAG, a RAG framework built on three components. First, DeFT-RAG generates one self-contained sub-question at a time from the original question and the sub-questions answered so far with their answers (QA pairs). Each sub-question names every entity it asks about, which removes the need to plan, track dependencies or rewrite, and lets the LLM focus on a single fine-grained task at a time. Second, DeFT-RAG moves the remaining LLM work outside the LLM: rule-based updates to a QA table that keeps track of what is known, and yes/no decisions made by a fine-tuned lightweight decision model. Third, DeFT-RAG caches the QA pairs and reuses an answer when a later question asks a sub-question with the same meaning. On MuSiQue, 2WikiMultiHopQA and HotpotQA, DeFT-RAG achieves the best accuracy among thirteen baselines with both gpt-4o-mini and Qwen3.6 as the backbone, while giving the LLM fewer input tokens than any other iterative method. With a QA cache shared across 1,000 MuSiQue questions, 23.1% of sub-questions are answered from the cache, which reduces LLM calls by 12.9% without lowering accuracy.

### [Neighborhood Elites: Quality-Diversity Search for Uncertainty Quantification in Question Answering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40278)
**Score 8/10** · `cs.CL` · 2610.40278

> It addresses uncertainty quantification in QA, directly relating to RAG evaluation and failure detection.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly capable, yet they produce wrong answers indistinguishable from correct ones. In question answering (QA), such an error propagates unnoticed into downstream tasks. Perturbation-based uncertainty quantification (UQ) addresses this by probing a model over a set of input rewrites and reading uncertainty from how much its predictions vary. Yet this set is usually built generically, by free paraphrasing, token-level perturbation, or adversarial search. Such rewrites leave two things uncontrolled that matter in QA: what kind of change is made to the context, and how explicitly the context supports the answer. When neither is controlled, the composition of the set is determined by the generator rather than by design, and the estimate inherits whatever that generator happens to produce. Quality-diversity (QD) search is built to cover a predefined exploration space, so we recast input perturbation as a QD problem and propose \emph{neighborhood elites (NE)}, a neighborhood constructor that spreads answer-preserving rewrites across two axes, the kind of edit and the explicitness of support. We compare NE against three other neighborhood constructors, holding the editor, QA model, uncertainty measure and search objective fixed, and against three scores read from the original context. Across five extractive-QA datasets and five instruction-tuned LLMs, NE improves failure detection against every other constructor in at least 24 of the 25 dataset-model pairs, reaching 0.726 AUROC and 0.211 AUPRC lift over the base rate. Additionally, NE detects failure better than semantic entropy computed over decoder samples, by 0.074 AUROC and 0.062 AUPRC lift, and this advantage widens as the neighborhood grows.

### [Efficient LLM-based Subgraph Retrieval for Multi-Hop Knowledge Base Question Answering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39667)

> This paper focuses on enhancing multi-hop KBQA retrieval methods, relevant to improving your chat application's retrieval capabilities.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Multi-hop Knowledge Base Question Answering (KBQA) aims to find answer entities in the knowledge base that are multiple hops away from the entities in the question. Information retrieval-based (IR-based) methods extract a pivotal subgraph from the entire KB to locate candidate answers and then evaluate their plausibility through semantic matching with the question. However, we observed that the extracted subgraphs often include nodes that are weakly related or irrelevant to the question. Without a proper node filtering mechanism, the number of irrelevant nodes grows as the number of hops increases, leading to excessive consumption of computational resources. To address these challenges, this study introduces an efficient LLM-based subgraph retrieval method for multi-hop knowledge base question answering, M-ER. The framework leverages Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) to transform subgraph exploration into a tree-structured search process. The framework further incorporates a large language model (LLM) to refine the search direction, ensuring that exploration remains focused on nodes relevant to the question. In addition, selected nodes are quantitatively scored, and these scores are fed back into the node selection process to effectively filter out irrelevant candidates, thereby improving the quality of the subgraph. This mechanism not only narrows the search space but also enhances the overall efficiency of multi-hop KBQA. Experiments on the WebQSP benchmark demonstrate that M-ER achieves 78.88\% on the Hits@1 metric, while also improving computational efficiency. These results not only validate the effectiveness of M-ER, but also offer a viable technical path to balance performance and computational efficiency.

Through experiments, we demonstrate that both the underlying retrieval method and the "deep research" policy employed can materially affect performance.
